\title{Covariance-Aware Gaussian Approximation of Coordinate Search}
\author{Th\'eo Voldoire and Nic Fishman}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
An analyst who may choose any $s$ of $p$ controls searches over a family
whose covariance geometry need not be separable.  We show that, in a
Gaussian linear model, the largest treatment coefficient over this family
is approximated by the maximum of a covariance-normalized bilinear Gaussian
chaos.  The result allows an arbitrary control covariance and holds whenever
$s\log(ep/s)=o(n)$, the same regime in which the empirical control Gram
matrix has a restricted isometry.  Local isotropy then makes the Gaussian
objective asymptotically separable, reducing its computation to the largest $s$
coordinatewise products.  When $s\log(ep/s)\to\infty$, those products may
also be replaced at the first-order search scale by mutually independent
Gaussian products.  Bounded
blocks yield an exact dynamic program, while a low-rank factor confines the
nonadditive correction to fixed-dimensional aggregates.  The proof also
isolates the sparse Gaussian coupling needed to extend the approximation
beyond Gaussian rows.
\end{abstract}

\section{The approximation}

Let $x\in\R^n$ denote the treatment, $X\in\R^{n\times p}$ the candidate
controls, and $y\in\R^n$ the outcome.  The rows are independent copies of
the centered linear model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
        y_i=\beta_0x_i+X_i^\top\beta+\varepsilon_i,
        \qquad
        \Var(x_i)=\sigma_x^2,
        \qquad
        \Cov(X_i)=C,
\end{equation}
where $(x_i,X_i)$ is jointly Gaussian and
$\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma_\varepsilon^2)$ is independent of it.  For a
set $S\subseteq[p]$, write
\begin{equation*}
        P_S=X_S(X_S^\top X_S)^{-1}X_S^\top,
        \qquad
        M_S=I-P_S,
        \qquad
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\frac{x^\top M_Sy}{x^\top M_Sx}.
\end{equation*}
The object of interest is $\max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}$.

Residualizing the outcome against the treatment removes the component
common to every specification.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residualization}
\begin{aligned}
        w_i&=y_i-\beta_0x_i=X_i^\top\beta+\varepsilon_i,\\
        \alpha&=\frac{\Cov(x_i,w_i)}{\sigma_x^2}
        =\frac{d^\top\beta}{\sigma_x^2},
        \qquad d=\Cov(X_i,x_i),\\
        r_i&=w_i-\alpha x_i,
        \qquad h=\Cov(X_i,r_i)=C\beta-\alpha d,
        \qquad \sigma_r^2=\Var(r_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then $\Cov(x_i,r_i)=0$, so $x_i$ and $r_i$ are independent under the
Gaussian model.  We assume $\sigma_r>0$.  The common alignment $\alpha$ is
absorbed by $\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}$; the proxy means entering the
specification-dependent term are $d$ and $h$.

For a vector $v\in\R^p$, define its covariance-normalized sparse norm by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-norm}
        \norm{v}_{C,k}
        =\max_{J\subseteq[p],\,|J|\leq k}
        \left(v_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}v_J\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
All population blocks $C_{JJ}$ with $|J|\leq2s$ are assumed positive
definite.  Set
\begin{equation*}
        L=\log(ep/s),
        \qquad
        \delta=\sqrt{\frac{sL}{n}},
\end{equation*}
and suppose that $sL=o(n)$ and, for a constant $K_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:proxy-energy}
        \norm{d}_{C,2s}\leq K_0\sigma_x\delta,
        \qquad
        \norm{h}_{C,2s}\leq K_0\sigma_r\delta.
\end{equation}
These conditions allow diffuse treatment and outcome proxies while keeping
their normalized sparse mean energy at the stochastic search scale.

The Gaussian approximator has the exact covariance of the two row scores.
Let $(G_1,G_2)\in\R^p\times\R^p$ be centered jointly Gaussian with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-covariance}
\begin{aligned}
        \Var(G_1)&=\sigma_x^2C+dd^\top,
        &\Var(G_2)&=\sigma_r^2C+hh^\top,\\
        \Cov(G_1,G_2)&=hd^\top,
        &\Cov(G_2,G_1)&=dh^\top,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and put
\begin{equation*}
        Z_1=\sqrt n\,d+G_1,
        \qquad
        Z_2=\sqrt n\,h+G_2.
\end{equation*}
For $a,b\in\R^p$, the covariance-aware search functional is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chaos-functional}
        T_C(a,b)
        =\max_{S\subseteq[p],\,|S|=s}
        \left\{-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S\right\}.
\end{equation}
It keeps the original coordinate subsets and incorporates their covariance
through the inverse of each selected principal submatrix.

\begin{theorem}[Covariance-aware Gaussian approximation]
\label{thm:gaussian-chaos}
Suppose the rows follow the Gaussian model \eqref{eq:model} with the
independent Gaussian noise stated above, $\sigma_r>0$, and
$C_{JJ}\succ0$ for $|J|\leq2s$.  If $sL=o(n)$ and
\eqref{eq:proxy-energy} holds, one can construct $(G_1,G_2)$ on an
extension of the data probability space such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-theorem}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        +\frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_x^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x}\frac{sL}{n}\delta
        \right).
\end{equation}
In particular, the remainder is
$o_{\Prob}((\sigma_r/\sigma_x)sL/n)$.
\end{theorem}

The random variable in \eqref{eq:main-theorem} is a noncentral bilinear
Gaussian chaos.  Its marginal law approximates the searched displacement
$\max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}$ at the
$sL/n$ scale; the coupling in the theorem is not required for simulation.
Fine tail or quantile accuracy would require a coupling error negligible at
the relevant fluctuation scale together with anti-concentration.

\section{Proof}

The proof separates the regression geometry from the Gaussian
approximation.  The regression calculation is exact and the empirical
geometry is controlled by a covariance-normalized restricted isometry.  A
conditional Gaussian decomposition then couples the two score vectors to
$(Z_1,Z_2)$ without a high-dimensional central limit theorem.

Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sample-scores}
        A=\frac{X^\top x}{\sqrt n},
        \qquad
        B=\frac{X^\top r}{\sqrt n},
        \qquad
        \widehat C=\frac{X^\top X}{n},
        \qquad
        \widehat\gamma=\frac{x^\top r}{x^\top x}.
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Exact regression reduction]\label{lem:exact-reduction}
For every $S$ for which $X_S^\top X_S$ is invertible,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-reduction}
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        =
        \frac{-A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}B_S
        +\widehat\gamma A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}A_S}
        {x^\top x-A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}A_S}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $y=(\beta_0+\alpha)x+r$, the coefficient with controls $S$ and the
coefficient without controls are
\begin{equation*}
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\beta_0+\alpha+\frac{x^\top M_Sr}{x^\top M_Sx},
        \qquad
        \widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        =\beta_0+\alpha+\frac{x^\top r}{x^\top x}.
\end{equation*}
Subtracting them, using $M_S=I-P_S$, and collecting the common denominator
gives
\begin{equation*}
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        =\frac{-x^\top P_Sr
        +(x^\top r/x^\top x)x^\top P_Sx}{x^\top M_Sx}.
\end{equation*}
The identities
$x^\top P_Sr=A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}B_S$ and
$x^\top P_Sx=A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}A_S$ yield
\eqref{eq:exact-reduction}.
\end{proof}

The next lemma supplies the Gaussian approximation of the scores in the
same sparse norm that enters the search.

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian score coupling]\label{lem:score-coupling}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-chaos}, the Gaussian
pair in \eqref{eq:score-covariance} can be coupled to $(A,B)$ so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-coupling}
        \frac{\norm{A-Z_1}_{C,s}}{\sigma_x}
        +\frac{\norm{B-Z_2}_{C,s}}{\sigma_r}
        =O_{\Prob}(\delta).
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-size}
        \norm{A}_{C,s}+\norm{Z_1}_{C,s}
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sqrt{sL}),
        \qquad
        \norm{B}_{C,s}+\norm{Z_2}_{C,s}
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_r\sqrt{sL}).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Standardize the treatment and residual outcome as
$\widetilde x_i=x_i/\sigma_x$ and
$\widetilde r_i=r_i/\sigma_r$, and write
$\widetilde d=d/\sigma_x$ and $\widetilde h=h/\sigma_r$.  The variables
$\widetilde x_i$ and $\widetilde r_i$ are independent standard Gaussians.
Gaussian regression gives the row decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-decomposition}
        X_i=\widetilde d\,\widetilde x_i
        +\widetilde h\,\widetilde r_i+U_i,
        \qquad
        U_i\ \perp\ (\widetilde x_i,\widetilde r_i),
\end{equation}
where the rows $U_i$ are independent Gaussian vectors with covariance
\begin{equation*}
        C_\perp=C-\widetilde d\widetilde d^\top
        -\widetilde h\widetilde h^\top\succeq0.
\end{equation*}

The centered, standardized score vectors satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-decomposition}
\begin{split}
        \frac{A}{\sigma_x}-\sqrt n\,\widetilde d
        &=\widetilde d Q_{11}+\widetilde h Q_{12}
          +\frac{U^\top\widetilde x}{\sqrt n},\\
        \frac{B}{\sigma_r}-\sqrt n\,\widetilde h
        &=\widetilde d Q_{12}+\widetilde h Q_{22}
          +\frac{U^\top\widetilde r}{\sqrt n},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        Q_{11}&=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
        (\widetilde x_i^2-1),
        \qquad
        Q_{12}=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
        \widetilde x_i\widetilde r_i,\\
        Q_{22}&=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n
        (\widetilde r_i^2-1).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
All three variables are $O_{\Prob}(1)$.

Let
\begin{equation*}
        R_n=\frac1n
        \begin{bmatrix}\widetilde x&\widetilde r\end{bmatrix}^\top
        \begin{bmatrix}\widetilde x&\widetilde r\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}
be their $2\times2$ sample Gram matrix.  Conditional on these two vectors, the
$p\times2$ matrix
\begin{equation*}
        W=\frac1{\sqrt n}U^\top
        \begin{bmatrix}\widetilde x&\widetilde r\end{bmatrix}
\end{equation*}
is matrix Gaussian with row covariance $C_\perp$ and column covariance
$R_n$.  Since $R_n$ is positive definite almost surely, set
$\mathcal K=W R_n^{-1/2}$.  Its conditional law does not depend on
$(\widetilde x,\widetilde r)$, so the columns
$\mathcal K_1,\mathcal K_2$ are independent
$N(0,C_\perp)$ vectors and are independent of
$(\widetilde x,\widetilde r)$.  Also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matrix-gram-error}
        \norm{R_n-I_2}_{\mathrm{op}}=O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2}),
        \qquad
        W=\mathcal K R_n^{1/2}.
\end{equation}

On an extension of the probability space, let
$g_{11},g_{12},g_{22}$ be independent of everything above and
independent of each other, with distributions
$N(0,2),N(0,1),N(0,2)$.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constructed-gaussian}
\begin{split}
        \widetilde G_1
        &=\mathcal K_1+\widetilde d\,g_{11}
          +\widetilde h\,g_{12},\\
        \widetilde G_2
        &=\mathcal K_2+\widetilde d\,g_{12}
          +\widetilde h\,g_{22},
        \qquad
        G_1=\sigma_x\widetilde G_1,
        \quad G_2=\sigma_r\widetilde G_2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The covariance $C_\perp$ and the three low-rank terms in
\eqref{eq:constructed-gaussian} give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        \Var(G_1)&=\sigma_x^2C+dd^\top,
        \qquad
        \Var(G_2)=\sigma_r^2C+hh^\top,\\
        \Cov(G_1,G_2)&=hd^\top,
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
which is \eqref{eq:score-covariance}.

It remains to bound the coupling error.  A union over the at most
$(ep/s)^s$ supports, followed by a fixed sphere-net argument on each
support, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-gaussian-size}
        \norm{\mathcal K_j}_{C,s}=O_{\Prob}(\sqrt{sL}),
        \qquad j=1,2,
\end{equation}
because $C_\perp\preceq C$.  Equations
\eqref{eq:matrix-gram-error} and \eqref{eq:sparse-gaussian-size} show
that replacing $W$ by $\mathcal K$ costs
$O_{\Prob}(\sqrt{sL/n})$ in each
standardized sparse norm.  The differences
$Q_{11}-g_{11}$, $Q_{12}-g_{12}$, and
$Q_{22}-g_{22}$ are $O_{\Prob}(1)$, while
\eqref{eq:proxy-energy} gives
$\norm{\widetilde d}_{C,s}+\norm{\widetilde h}_{C,s}=O(\delta)$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:score-decomposition} proves
\eqref{eq:score-coupling}.  The same sphere-net bound, the proxy-energy
conditions, and the deterministic means
$\sqrt n\,\widetilde d$ and $\sqrt n\,\widetilde h$ then give
\eqref{eq:score-size}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-chaos}]
The covariance-normalized block-Gram event satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rip}
        \epsilon_n=
        \max_{|J|\leq2s}
        \norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}\widehat C_{JJ}C_{JJ}^{-1/2}-I}
        _{\mathrm{op}}
        =O_{\Prob}(\delta).
\end{equation}
Indeed, for each $J$ the rows $C_{JJ}^{-1/2}X_{iJ}$ are standard
Gaussian.  A fixed sphere net and a union bound over principal submatrices
of size at most $\min(2s,p)$ have entropy $O(sL)$ and give
\eqref{eq:rip}.  In particular,
$\epsilon_n=o_{\Prob}(1)$.

For every $S$, write
$E_S=C_{SS}^{-1/2}\widehat C_{SS}C_{SS}^{-1/2}-I$.  On
$\epsilon_n<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inverse-error}
\begin{split}
        &\left|A_S^\top
        (\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}-C_{SS}^{-1})B_S\right|\\
        &\qquad\leq
        \frac{\epsilon_n}{1-\epsilon_n}
        \norm{A}_{C,s}\norm{B}_{C,s}
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL\delta),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where the last equality uses Lemma~\ref{lem:score-coupling}.  The same
argument gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:treatment-energy}
        \max_{|S|\leq s}
        A_S^\top\widehat C_{SS}^{-1}A_S
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x^2sL).
\end{equation}

Since $x_i/\sigma_x$ and $r_i/\sigma_r$ are independent standard
Gaussians,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-concentration}
        x^\top x=n\sigma_x^2\{1+O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2})\},
        \qquad
        \widehat\gamma
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x\sqrt n}\right).
\end{equation}
Equations \eqref{eq:treatment-energy} and
\eqref{eq:scalar-concentration} flatten every denominator in
\eqref{eq:exact-reduction} to
\begin{equation*}
        n\sigma_x^2\{1+O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2}+\delta^2)\}
\end{equation*}
uniformly over $S$.  The numerator has size
$O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL)$, and its term multiplied by
$\widehat\gamma$ contributes at most
$O_{\Prob}((\sigma_r/\sigma_x)sL/(n\sqrt n))$ after division by the
denominator.  Since $n^{-1/2}\leq\delta$ and $\delta^2\leq\delta$ for
large $n$, Lemma~\ref{lem:exact-reduction} and
\eqref{eq:inverse-error} imply
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-geometry}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        +\frac{T_C(A,B)}{n\sigma_x^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x}\frac{sL}{n}\delta
        \right).
\end{equation}

For arbitrary vectors $a,b,z_1,z_2$, Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$C_{SS}^{-1}$ inner product gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chaos-lipschitz}
\begin{split}
        |T_C(a,b)-T_C(z_1,z_2)|
        &\leq\norm{a-z_1}_{C,s}\norm{b}_{C,s}\\
        &\quad+\norm{z_1}_{C,s}\norm{b-z_2}_{C,s}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Lemma~\ref{lem:score-coupling} therefore yields
\begin{equation*}
        |T_C(A,B)-T_C(Z_1,Z_2)|
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_x\sigma_r sL}{\sqrt n}\right)
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL\delta).
\end{equation*}
Combining this bound with \eqref{eq:population-geometry} proves
\eqref{eq:main-theorem}.
\end{proof}

\section{Separable and structured covariance}

The covariance-aware chaos leaves the original subset search intact, but
it also identifies the covariance structures under which that search
simplifies.  Local isotropy makes the objective asymptotically additive.
Bounded block structure gives an exact dynamic program, while a small
number of common factors confines the nonadditive interaction to
low-dimensional aggregates.

Assume in this section that the controls have unit variance and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-isotropy}
        \rho_s(C)=\max_{|J|\leq2s}\norm{C_{JJ}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Separable Gaussian approximation]
\label{cor:separable}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-chaos}, if
$\rho_s(C)=o(1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separable}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        +\frac1{n\sigma_x^2}
        \sum_{j=1}^s\{-Z_{1k}Z_{2k}\}_{(j)}
        +o_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x}\frac{sL}{n}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\{-Z_{1k}Z_{2k}\}_{(j)}$ is the $j$th largest of the $p$
coordinatewise products.  The Gaussian coordinates retain the covariance
in \eqref{eq:score-covariance}; separability concerns the optimization and
does not require them to be mutually independent.

If also $sL\to\infty$, let $Z_1^\circ,Z_2^\circ\in\R^p$ have all $2p$
coordinates mutually independent, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:independent-coordinates}
        Z_{1k}^\circ\sim N(\sqrt n\,d_k,\sigma_x^2),
        \qquad
        Z_{2k}^\circ\sim N(\sqrt n\,h_k,\sigma_r^2).
\end{equation}
They may be drawn independently of the data on an extension of the
probability space, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:independent-separable}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        +\frac1{n\sigma_x^2}
        \sum_{j=1}^s\{-Z_{1k}^\circ Z_{2k}^\circ\}_{(j)}
        +o_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x}\frac{sL}{n}\right).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every $S$, local isotropy gives
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{C_{SS}^{-1}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \leq\frac{\rho_s(C)}{1-\rho_s(C)}.
\end{equation*}
The marginal Gaussian tails and the proxy-energy bounds imply
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{|S|\leq s}\norm{Z_{1,S}}_2
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sqrt{sL}),
        \qquad
        \max_{|S|\leq s}\norm{Z_{2,S}}_2
        =O_{\Prob}(\sigma_r\sqrt{sL});
\end{equation*}
independence across coordinates is not used in this union bound.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        &\max_{|S|=s}\left|
        Z_{1,S}^\top(C_{SS}^{-1}-I)Z_{2,S}\right|\\
        &\qquad=O_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r\rho_s(C)sL)
        =o_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The maximum of $-\sum_{k\in S}Z_{1k}Z_{2k}$ over $|S|=s$ is exactly
the sum of the largest $s$ coordinatewise products.  Theorem
\ref{thm:gaussian-chaos} now gives \eqref{eq:separable}.

It remains to compare the correlated products with the independent ones.
Normalize the target as
$\overline Z=(Z_1/\sigma_x,Z_2/\sigma_r)$, and write
$\norm{v}_{2,s}=\max_{|S|\leq s}\norm{v_S}_2$.  For $J\subseteq[p]$,
let $\Gamma[J]$ be the covariance matrix of the centered coordinates in
both score channels indexed by $J$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:score-covariance}
gives
\begin{equation*}
        \chi_n=\max_{|J|\leq2s}
        \norm{\Gamma[J]-I_{2|J|}}_{\mathrm{op}}
        =O\!\left(\rho_s(C)+\delta^2\right)=o(1),
\end{equation*}
where local isotropy converts the proxy-energy bounds into Euclidean
norm bounds on $d_J/\sigma_x$ and $h_J/\sigma_r$.

For $v=(v_1,v_2)$, write
$q_S(v)=-v_{1,S}^\top v_{2,S}$ and smooth its maximum by
\begin{equation*}
        f_\tau(v)=\frac1\tau
        \log\sum_{|S|=s}\exp\{\tau q_S(v)\}.
\end{equation*}
The smoothing error is at most $sL/\tau$.  If $w_S$ denotes the
corresponding softmax weight, then
\begin{equation*}
        \nabla^2f_\tau
        =\sum_Sw_S\nabla^2q_S
        +\tau\operatorname{Cov}_w(\nabla q_S).
\end{equation*}
Gaussian interpolation between $\Gamma$ and $I_{2p}$ uses only the union
of two selected supports.  To see this, let
$\Delta_\Gamma=\Gamma-I_{2p}$ and let $Y_t$ have the common mean of the two
targets and covariance $I_{2p}+t\Delta_\Gamma$.  Gaussian interpolation
gives
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{d}{dt}\E f_\tau(Y_t)
        =\frac12\E\operatorname{tr}
        \{\Delta_\Gamma\nabla^2f_\tau(Y_t)\}.
\end{equation*}
For a fixed support,
$|\operatorname{tr}\{\Delta_\Gamma\nabla^2q_S\}|
\leq2s\chi_n$.  If $g_S=\nabla q_S$, then
\begin{equation*}
        \operatorname{Cov}_w(g_S)
        =\frac12\sum_{S,T}w_Sw_T
        (g_S-g_T)(g_S-g_T)^\top.
\end{equation*}
Each difference is supported on the two score channels over $S\cup T$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        |\operatorname{tr}\{\Delta_\Gamma
        \operatorname{Cov}_w(g_S)\}|
        &\leq\chi_n\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}_w(g_S)\\
        &\leq\chi_n\left\{
        \norm{Y_{t,1}}_{2,s}^2+\norm{Y_{t,2}}_{2,s}^2\right\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The expectation of the last display is $O(\chi_n sL)$ by the same
Gaussian sparse-norm bound used above.  Integrating over $t$ and including
the smoothing error bounds the difference of the two expected maxima by
$O(s\chi_n+\tau\chi_n sL+sL/\tau)$.  The comparison is exact if
$\chi_n=0$; otherwise, taking $\tau=\chi_n^{-1/2}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:independent-comparison}
\left|
        \E\max_{|S|=s}q_S(\overline Z)
        -\E\max_{|S|=s}q_S(Z_1^\circ/\sigma_x,
        Z_2^\circ/\sigma_r)
\right|=o(sL).
\end{equation}
Gaussian Poincar\'e gives, for either normalized target
$Y=(Y_1,Y_2)$,
\begin{equation*}
        \Var\!\left(\max_{|S|=s}q_S\right)
        \leq(1+\chi_n)\E\left\{
        \norm{Y_1}_{2,s}^2+\norm{Y_2}_{2,s}^2\right\}=O(sL).
\end{equation*}
Thus each maximum differs from its expectation by
$O_{\Prob}(\sqrt{sL})$.  This is
$o_{\Prob}(sL)$ when $sL\to\infty$.  Thus the two top-$s$ values differ
by $o_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL)$ on the enlarged probability space,
and \eqref{eq:independent-separable} follows from \eqref{eq:separable}.
\end{proof}

The independent replacement in \eqref{eq:independent-separable} is a
first-order approximation at the $sL$ search scale.  Local isotropy alone
does not generally preserve the centered extreme-value law at its finer
fluctuation scale.

Local isotropy is the singleton-block special case of a broader
approximation.  Let $C^\circ$ be any positive definite covariance whose
principal submatrices indexed by sets of size at most $s$ approximate
those of $C$, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restricted-covariance-error}
        \eta_s(C,C^\circ)=
        \max_{|J|\leq s}
        \norm{(C^\circ_{JJ})^{-1/2}
        (C_{JJ}-C^\circ_{JJ})
        (C^\circ_{JJ})^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Structured covariance replacement]
\label{prop:structured-replacement}
If $\eta_s(C,C^\circ)<1$, then for all $a,b\in\R^p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:structured-replacement}
        |T_C(a,b)-T_{C^\circ}(a,b)|
        \leq
        \frac{\eta_s(C,C^\circ)}{1-\eta_s(C,C^\circ)}
        \norm{a}_{C^\circ,s}\norm{b}_{C^\circ,s}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, a sequence satisfying
$\eta_s(C,C^\circ)=o(1)$ can replace $C$ in the optimization whenever
the two sparse score norms are $O_{\Prob}(\sqrt{sL})$ after variance
normalization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $S$, write
\begin{equation*}
        C_{SS}=(C^\circ_{SS})^{1/2}(I+E_S)
        (C^\circ_{SS})^{1/2},
        \qquad \norm{E_S}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\eta_s(C,C^\circ).
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{(I+E_S)^{-1}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \leq\frac{\eta_s(C,C^\circ)}{1-\eta_s(C,C^\circ)}.
\end{equation*}
Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the difference between the two support objectives
by the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:structured-replacement}.  Taking the
maximum over $S$ and using
$|\max_Su_S-\max_Sv_S|\leq\max_S|u_S-v_S|$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

The useful structured choice is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-factor}
        C^\circ=D+\Lambda\Lambda^\top,
\end{equation}
where $D\succ0$ is block diagonal and
$\Lambda\in\R^{p\times r}$ has small rank.
Woodbury's identity retains the coordinate support and gives, for every
$S$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:woodbury-objective}
\begin{split}
        -a_S^\top(C^\circ_{SS})^{-1}b_S
        &=-a_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}b_S\\
        &\quad+\ell_{a,S}^\top(I+H_S)^{-1}\ell_{b,S},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
        \ell_{a,S}=\Lambda_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}a_S,
        \qquad
        \ell_{b,S}=\Lambda_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}b_S,
        \qquad
        H_S=\Lambda_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}\Lambda_S.
\end{equation*}
The first term in \eqref{eq:woodbury-objective} separates across the
blocks of $D$, while every factor interaction is summarized by two
$r$-vectors and one symmetric $r\times r$ matrix.  Thus principal
components or other factors can be used without rotating the searched
coordinates when their block-plus-factor reconstruction satisfies the
restricted approximation bound \eqref{eq:restricted-covariance-error}.

To make the additive structure explicit, let
$\mathcal G_1,\ldots,\mathcal G_m$ be the blocks of $D$ and put
$S_j=S\cap\mathcal G_j$.  The quantities in
\eqref{eq:woodbury-objective} obey
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
        -a_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}b_S
        &=\sum_{j=1}^m-a_{S_j}^\top D_{S_jS_j}^{-1}b_{S_j},\\
        \ell_{a,S}&=\sum_{j=1}^m\ell_{a,S_j},
        &\ell_{b,S}&=\sum_{j=1}^m\ell_{b,S_j},
        &H_S&=\sum_{j=1}^mH_{S_j}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

When $\Lambda=0$ and the blocks of $D$ have size at most $\nu$, the search
is exactly computable.  For each block $\mathcal G_j$ and each
$k\leq\min(s,|\mathcal G_j|)$, enumerate its subsets and calculate
\begin{equation*}
        v_{jk}=
        \max_{S\subseteq\mathcal G_j,\,|S|=k}
        \{-a_S^\top D_{SS}^{-1}b_S\}.
\end{equation*}
A budget dynamic program then maximizes
$\sum_j v_{jk_j}$ subject to $\sum_j k_j=s$.  A naive implementation
takes $O(m2^\nu\nu^3+ms\nu)$ arithmetic operations.  Bounded $\nu$
therefore gives an exact polynomial-time algorithm; the diagonal case
$\nu=1$ is ordinary top-$s$ sorting.

For fixed positive $r$, \eqref{eq:woodbury-objective} is an algebraic
dimension reduction, not by itself a polynomial-time algorithm.  It
suggests a range-dependent additive approximation by a gridded dynamic
program when the
block size and the ranges of $\ell_{a,S}$, $\ell_{b,S}$, and $H_S$ are
controlled.  Indeed, if the first two ranges are bounded by $R_a,R_b$,
then the factor correction $\phi(\ell_a,\ell_b,H)
=\ell_a^\top(I+H)^{-1}\ell_b$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        |\phi(\ell_a,\ell_b,H)-\phi(\widetilde\ell_a,
        \widetilde\ell_b,\widetilde H)|
        &\leq R_b\norm{\ell_a-\widetilde\ell_a}_2
        +R_a\norm{\ell_b-\widetilde\ell_b}_2\\
        &\quad+R_aR_b\norm{H-\widetilde H}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
For fixed rank, this is the stability bound needed to discretize the
$2r+r(r+1)/2$ aggregate coordinates.  A complete algorithmic guarantee
would also have to specify the grid, recursion, accumulated rounding error,
and numerical ranges; no unconditional fixed-rank complexity guarantee is
asserted here.

The chaos also has a closed-form marginal gain.  It reduces to the
coordinatewise product in the diagonal case and suggests a conditional
product heuristic more generally.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional product increment]
\label{prop:marginal-gain}
Let $F(S)=-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S$.  For $j\notin S$, define
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        c_{j\mid S}&=C_{jj}-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}C_{Sj},\\
        a_{j\mid S}&=a_j-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}a_S,\\
        b_{j\mid S}&=b_j-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}b_S.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal-gain}
        F(S\cup\{j\})-F(S)
        =-\frac{a_{j\mid S}b_{j\mid S}}{c_{j\mid S}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the block-inverse formula to $C_{S\cup\{j\},S\cup\{j\}}$ with
Schur complement $c_{j\mid S}$ and collect the terms involving the new
coordinate.  The resulting rank-one correction is exactly
\eqref{eq:marginal-gain}.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-gain} suggests starting from the empty set,
adding the coordinate with the largest right-hand side of
\eqref{eq:marginal-gain}, and updating the inverse by a rank-one formula.
This heuristic is exact when $C$ is diagonal and otherwise residualizes
each candidate against the selected coordinates.  The standard
monotone-submodular greedy guarantee does not apply: with
$C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$a=b=(1,1)^\top$, and $0<\rho<1$, the marginal gain of coordinate two is
$-1$ at the empty set but $-(1-\rho)/(1+\rho)>-1$ after selecting
coordinate one.  Thus $F$ is neither monotone increasing nor submodular in
general.

\section{What a sub-Gaussian extension requires}

The regression reduction does not use Gaussianity.  A natural candidate
extension assumes that the rows
$V_i=(x_i,r_i,X_i^\top)^\top$ are i.i.d., centered, and uniformly
sub-Gaussian relative to their covariance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subgaussian-row}
        \norm{u^\top V_i}_{\psi_2}
        \leq K_{\mathrm{sg}}
        \{\E(u^\top V_i)^2\}^{1/2}
        \quad\text{for every }u,
\end{equation}
where
$\norm{\zeta}_{\psi_2}=\inf\{t>0:\E\exp(\zeta^2/t^2)\leq2\}$ and
$\Cov(x_i,r_i)=0$.  Define the centered row score
\begin{equation*}
        \Psi_i=
        \begin{pmatrix}
        x_iX_i-d\\ r_iX_i-h
        \end{pmatrix}
        \in\R^{2p},
        \qquad
        \Omega=\Var(\Psi_i),
\end{equation*}
and let $(G_1,G_2)\sim N(0,\Omega)$.  The covariance $\Omega$ now retains
the fourth moments of the row distribution; it need not equal
\eqref{eq:score-covariance}.  Write
$Z_1=\sqrt n\,d+G_1$ and $Z_2=\sqrt n\,h+G_2$.  The proof of Theorem
\ref{thm:gaussian-chaos} shows that one further statement would extend the
result.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse score coupling is sufficient]
\label{prop:coupling-suffices}
Retain $sL=o(n)$, $\sigma_x,\sigma_r>0$, and a common control covariance
$C$ whose searched principal submatrices are positive definite.  Suppose
the covariance-normalized block-Gram event \eqref{eq:rip}, the
proxy-energy bounds \eqref{eq:proxy-energy}, and the score-size bounds
\eqref{eq:score-size} hold under \eqref{eq:subgaussian-row}.  If one can
construct the matched Gaussian pair so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:needed-coupling}
        \frac1{\sigma_x}
        \norm{\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(x_iX_i-d)-G_1}_{C,s}
        +\frac1{\sigma_r}
        \norm{\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n(r_iX_i-h)-G_2}_{C,s}
        =o_{\Prob}(\sqrt{sL}),
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\widehat\beta_{0,\varnothing}
        +\frac{T_C(\sqrt n\,d+G_1,\sqrt n\,h+G_2)}
        {n\sigma_x^2}
        +o_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_x}\frac{sL}{n}\right).
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Gram and score-size assumptions repeat the argument from
\eqref{eq:inverse-error} through \eqref{eq:population-geometry}; the row
condition \eqref{eq:subgaussian-row} also gives the scalar concentration in
\eqref{eq:scalar-concentration}.  Applying
\eqref{eq:chaos-lipschitz} to the coupling in
\eqref{eq:needed-coupling} changes the chaos by
$o_{\Prob}(\sigma_x\sigma_r sL)$, which gives the stated coefficient
error after division by $n\sigma_x^2$.
\end{proof}

The concentration assumptions in Proposition~\ref{prop:coupling-suffices}
are automatic under \eqref{eq:subgaussian-row}.  For example, fix $J$ and
$u$ with $u^\top C_{JJ}u=1$.  The centered product
$x_i u^\top X_{iJ}-u^\top d_J$ is sub-exponential, so Bernstein's
inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
        \Prob\!\left(
        \left|u^\top(A_J-\sqrt n\,d_J)\right|>t\sigma_x
        \right)
        \leq2\exp\{-c\min(t^2,t\sqrt n)\}.
\end{equation*}
The same bound holds for $B_J-\sqrt n\,h_J$ at scale $\sigma_r$.
A sphere net and a union over the supports have entropy $O(sL)$; because
$sL=o(n)$, taking $t$ of order $\sqrt{sL}$ remains in the quadratic part
of the Bernstein bound.  This proves the score bounds for $A$ and $B$.
Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
        \Var(x_i u^\top X_{iJ})
        \leq\{E x_i^4\,\E(u^\top X_{iJ})^4\}^{1/2}
        \leq c\sigma_x^2,
\end{equation*}
and analogously for the residual channel.  The same net argument therefore
controls the matched Gaussian scores.  Adding the proxy means proves
\eqref{eq:score-size}.  Applying Bernstein to
$(u^\top X_{iJ})^2-1$ proves \eqref{eq:rip} in the same regime.

The remaining problem is a functional Gaussian approximation in the
sparse norm, or a direct comparison of the two chaos maxima that avoids
coupling the entire score process.  Proving that comparison under the same
$sL=o(n)$ regime would
extend the covariance-aware theorem, the correlated-product separable
reduction, and the structured replacements without changing their
regression arguments.  The mutually independent product replacement would
additionally require the variance-normalized matched score covariance to be
close to the identity on the same sparse principal submatrices.
