\subsection{A Separable Approximation of Coordinate Search}
\label{sec:plugin-regression}

\tc{The bounds of Section~\ref{sec:bounds} identify the scale of the
searched supremum but leave its distribution implicit: applied to a given
study, they return an order of magnitude, not a number. This section
approximates the supremum itself by a separable surrogate, a sum of order
statistics of independent products of Gaussians built from population
quantities alone. Separability is not available in general: the
covariance $C$ may encode combinatorial structure --- clusters of
near-duplicate controls, say --- that no coordinatewise object can
capture.

The price of a law is therefore a strictly stronger hypothesis than the
one the bounds were proved under, and it is worth naming the increment.
Section~\ref{sec:bounds} asked for subgaussian tails, a sample-size
floor $n \ge C_0\, s\log(ep/s)$, and a restricted condition number
bounded by a constant; the experimental upper bound consumed no
condition on $C$ whatever. None of that suffices here. Where
the restricted conditioning of Assumption~\ref{ass:observational}(ii)
tolerates correlation at
any fixed level
--- equicorrelated controls keep $\kappa_s(C)$ bounded at fixed $s$ ---
the assumption below asks that
correlation vanish at the scale search reaches; where the bounds needed
$n$ merely to clear the floor, the approximation needs
$s\log(ep/s) = o(n)$, so the critical regime in which search moves the
estimate by the order of the effect is now excluded; and where the
bounds constrained no moment beyond the subgaussian tail, the
approximation constrains the fourth. The assumption also excludes
systematic means beyond the scale that search itself generates. None of
this can be read as defensive bookkeeping that a user of the surrogate
might safely ignore: what a violation costs is not a conservative error
but an error of either sign, as Remark~\ref{rem:plugin-direction}
records. Correlation at a fixed level, carried by few directions, is the
subject of Remark~\ref{rem:plugin-factors}. Throughout the section the
controls are normalized to
unit variance, so that $C$ is a correlation matrix, and local geometry
is measured by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-isotropy}
        \rho_s(C) = \sup_{|J| \le 2s}
        \left\| C_{JJ} - I \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}}

\begin{assumption}[Plug-in regularity]\label{ass:plugin}
\tc{The controls have unit variance, and the following hold as
$n, p \to \infty$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Local isotropy.} The controls are uncorrelated at the
scale search works on: $\rho_s(C) = o(1)$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{No control is a visible proxy for the treatment or
for the outcome.} The confounding and the outcome signal stay,
coordinate by coordinate, at the $t$-scale
$\sqrt{\log(ep/s)}$ that search itself generates,
\begin{equation*}
        \sqrt n\, \max_{k \le p}
        \left( \frac{|d_k|}{\sigma_0}
        + \frac{|(C\beta_{-0})_k|}
        {\sqrt{\beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2}} \right)
        = O\big(\sqrt{\log(ep/s)}\big).
\end{equation*}
\item[(iii)] \emph{The treatment is nearly uncorrelated with the
outcome material.}
$|\Cov(x_0^{(i)},\, y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)})| = |d^\top \beta_{-0}|
= o\big(\sigma_0 \sqrt{(\beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0}
+ \sigma_\varepsilon^2)\, s \log(ep/s) / n}\big)$.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Fourth moments as under a Gaussian design.} Write
$\varsigma^2 = \beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$ for the
variance of $y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}$. Every second moment of the
searched products $x_0^{(i)} X_k^{(i)}$ and
$(y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}) X_k^{(i)}$ agrees with the value it takes
for a Gaussian row of the same covariance, up to an error negligible
against the relevant one of $\sigma_0^2$, $\varsigma^2$ and
$\sigma_0 \varsigma$: uniformly over $j \ne k$,
\begin{equation*}
        \Var(x_0^{(i)} X_k^{(i)}) = \sigma_0^2\,(1 + o(1)),
        \qquad
        \Cov(x_0^{(i)} X_j^{(i)},\, x_0^{(i)} X_k^{(i)})
        = \sigma_0^2\, C_{jk} + o(\sigma_0^2),
\end{equation*}
and likewise for the pairs built from $y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}$ alone,
and from it together with $x_0^{(i)}$, the latter centered at
$d^\top \beta_{-0} + o(\sigma_0 \varsigma)$.
\end{enumerate}}
\end{assumption}

\tc{Each condition closes one channel along which the surrogate can be
broken, and Remark~\ref{rem:plugin-sharpness} records where the
boundaries are attained. Condition (i) is the local isotropy announced
in the introduction, and it is the substantive restriction of the
section: every group of $2s$ controls search can reach must be
near-orthogonal, which at fixed $s$ is the requirement that pairwise
correlations vanish. The unit-variance normalization is without loss,
since $\hat\beta_{0,S}$ depends on $X_S$ only through its span, so a
diagonal covariance becomes $C = I$ and satisfies (i) exactly. What (i)
rules out is correlation at a fixed level, and it rules it out for a
reason of proof rather than of substance;
Remark~\ref{rem:plugin-factors} states the extension. It also absorbs
the restricted conditioning of Section~\ref{sec:bounds}: local isotropy
drives $\kappa_s(C)$ to $1$, so the restricted conditioning is not
cited again here, and becomes load-bearing only in the extension, where
$C$ itself is no longer locally isotropic.

Condition (ii) asks that no control be a visible proxy, on the treatment
side as well as the outcome side: no control's population association
with either exceeds the $t$-scale $\sqrt{\log(ep/s)}$ that search itself
generates, so no coordinate is visible before the search begins. Past that scale the surrogate
double-counts, explained variance being sub-additive over correlated
coordinates where the surrogate's means simply add; the cap also makes
every searched denominator concentrate at a common value, so no separate
overlap condition is needed. Condition (iii) bounds a single scalar
playing two roles: $d^\top \beta_{-0}$ is the population covariance
between the two factors of each searched product, which the surrogate
takes independent, and it is the center of the common sampling noise
$\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset} - \beta_0$, which search cannot reach. Asking
the corresponding correlation to sit below $\sqrt{s\log(ep/s)/n}$, the
relative scale at which search operates, keeps that noise out of the
searched coordinates. Condition (iv) asks that the
design's fourth moments be the ones the surrogate's Gaussian variances
presuppose; a Gaussian design satisfies every identity exactly given
(ii), so (iv) restricts fourth cross-cumulants only.}

\begin{theorem}[Separable approximation]\label{thm:plugin-separable}
\tc{Let Assumptions~\ref{ass:dgp}(i)--(ii) and~\ref{ass:plugin} hold,
with $s \log(ep/s) = o(n)$ and $p/s \to \infty$. On
the same probability space as the data one can construct Gaussian
variables $(\xi_{1k})_{k \le p}$ and $(\xi_{2k})_{k \le p}$, all $2p$ of
them mutually independent, with
\begin{equation*}
        \xi_{1k} \sim N\big(\sqrt n\, d_k,\ \sigma_0^2\big),
        \qquad
        \xi_{2k} \sim N\big(\sqrt n\, (C\beta_{-0})_k,\
        \beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2 \big),
\end{equation*}
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plugin-theorem}
        \sup_{S \subseteq [p],\, |S| = s} \hat\beta_{0,S}
        =
        \hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}
        + \frac{1}{n\, \sigma_0^2}
        \sum_{j=1}^{s} \left\{ -\xi_{1k}\, \xi_{2k} \right\}_{(j)}
        + o_{\mathbb P}\!\left( \frac{s \log(ep/s)}{n} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\{-\xi_{1k}\, \xi_{2k}\}_{(j)}$ denotes the $j$-th largest of the
$p$ products: the searched supremum equals the surrogate up to an error
vanishing relative to the searched displacement.}
\end{theorem}

\tc{The surrogate replaces a search over $\binom{p}{s}$ overlapping
configurations by $p$ independent coordinates, of which the largest $s$
products are kept. Each coordinate multiplies the two channels search
can exploit: $\xi_{1k}$ carries the alignment of the treatment with
control $k$, centered at the confounding $\sqrt n\, d_k$, and $\xi_{2k}$
the alignment of the outcome material $y - \beta_0 x_0$ with control $k$,
centered at
the signal $\sqrt n\, (C\beta_{-0})_k$; the common sampling noise
$\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}$, beyond the reach of search, enters as a shared
shift. Taking expectations of the top-$s$ sum recovers the rate
$s \log(ep/s)/n$ of Section~\ref{sec:bounds}; the surrogate refines the
bounds by supplying a law around that rate. In simulations the
approximation error concentrates at the sharper scale
$s \log(ep/s)/n^{3/2}$, one factor $\sqrt n$ inside the displacement,
uniformly over the designs tried.}

\begin{remark}[Designs with fixed correlation]\label{rem:plugin-factors}
\tc{Local isotropy is more restrictive than it looks: for
equicorrelated controls $\rho_s(C) = \rho\,(2s-1)$, so
Assumption~\ref{ass:plugin}(i) forces $\rho \to 0$ and excludes every
design carrying correlation at a fixed level, factor structures among
them. The display survives such
designs when the correlation is carried by few directions and the
budget can shed them. Remove from $C$ a bounded number $M$ of
orthonormal eigenvectors $f_1, \dots, f_M$, delocalized in the sense
$p \max_{m,k} f_{mk}^2 = O(1)$; the equicorrelated matrix is this with
$M = 1$ and $f_1 = \1/\sqrt p$, leaving
$(1 - \rho)(I - \1 \1^\top / p)$, whose correlation matrix is locally
isotropic. Whenever what is left behind is locally isotropic in this
sense, the confounding and signal carried by the removed directions
obey the cap of (ii) with room to spare, and the budget exceeds the
number of directions removed, $s \ge M + 1$, the conclusion of
Theorem~\ref{thm:plugin-separable} is unchanged; the argument is given
in Appendix~\ref{app:plugin-factors}, where one step at fixed factor
strength remains open. The budget floor is not an artifact of that
argument. At $s = 1$ under a single equicorrelated factor of
correlation $0.5$, the searched supremum falls short of the surrogate
by the factor $0.74$ at $p = 100$ and $0.71$ at $p = 400$, a deficit of
order $\rho \log p$, because one coordinate offers no span freedom;
from $s = 2$ on, search sheds the direction through in-span contrasts
and the display is restored.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Direction of the failure]\label{rem:plugin-direction}
\tc{It would be convenient if the surrogate erred in a known direction
when the assumption fails, so that a violation cost only conservatism
and the plug-in could be read as an upper bound on what search can
reach. It does not. Which way it errs is decided by which side of the
model is disturbed, and
Appendix~\ref{app:plugin-direction} tabulates the two regimes,
violating one condition at a time and leaving the rest in force.

Disturbances of the outcome channel are conservative. Correlated
controls carrying co-aligned signal have sub-additive sparse explained
variance where the surrogate's means simply add, so the surrogate
overstates, by up to a factor two at equicorrelation $0.8$; the budget
floor of Remark~\ref{rem:plugin-factors} is conservative for a companion
reason, the maximum of $p$ correlated coordinate statistics growing like
$(1-\rho)\log p$ rather than $\log p$. Disturbances of the treatment
channel are not. Sub-additivity is a property of the alignment between
$C\beta_{-0}$ and the eigenbasis of $C_{JJ}$, not of correlation as
such --- for two equicorrelated controls the relevant ratio is
$1/(1+\rho)$ when the signal is aligned but $1/(1-\rho)$ when it
alternates in sign --- and confounding at the cap of (ii) under fixed
correlation buys search a freedom among spans that no coordinatewise
object tracks. In both cases the searched supremum exceeds the
surrogate, by half again as much in the second at equicorrelation
$0.8$. Alignment of positive sign and product variances above the value
condition (iv) fixes fail in the same direction.

Two consequences. The surrogate is a conservative diagnostic only
under hypotheses that are themselves one-sided, so restricting the
claim of Theorem~\ref{thm:plugin-separable} to its upper half would not
buy weaker hypotheses --- in particular condition (iv) is needed
whichever direction is wanted, since a heavy-tailed common row scale
inflates the truth without bound. And the conservative cases should not
be read as a safety margin: they are as large as the anti-conservative
ones, so a violation is a reason to distrust the surrogate in either
direction rather than to discount it.}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sharpness of the hypotheses]\label{rem:plugin-sharpness}
\tc{Two further hypotheses sit exactly at the boundary of validity, by
direct falsification. The sub-critical condition
$s\log(ep/s) = o(n)$ is exact: the relative error of the display equals
$0.57\, s\log(ep/s)/n$ across $n/(s\log(ep/s)) \in [5, 40]$. And if the
variance identities of condition (iv) fail --- as they do for designs
with heavy fourth cross-cumulants, such as rows sharing a random
scale --- the display fails by exactly the variance mismatch, and the
theorem then holds verbatim with the product variances
$\Var(x_0^{(i)} X_k^{(i)})$ and
$\Var((y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}) X_k^{(i)})$ in place of $\sigma_0^2$
and $\beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$; no such repair exists for the
cross-coordinate identities of (iv). The alignment ceiling of
condition (iii) is attained by densely aligned confounding and is
conservative for sparse alignment.}
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Exact calibration at a fixed budget]
\label{cor:plugin-calibration}
\tc{Let Assumptions~\ref{ass:dgp}(i)--(ii) and~\ref{ass:experimental}
hold with $\beta_{-0} = 0$, unit-variance controls and
$\rho_s(C) \log p = o(1)$; let $s$ be fixed and $n, p \to \infty$ with
$\log p = o(n^{1/3})$. Set
$b_p = \log p - \tfrac12 \log \log p - \tfrac12 \log 2\pi$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plugin-calibration}
        \frac{\sigma_0\, n}{\sigma_\varepsilon}
        \left( \sup_{S \subseteq [p],\, |S| = s} \hat\beta_{0,S}
        - \hat\beta_{0,\emptyset} \right)
        - s\, b_p
        \;\Rightarrow\;
        \sum_{k=1}^{s} \left( -\log \Gamma_k \right),
        \qquad
        \Gamma_k = E_1 + \dots + E_k,
\end{equation}
with $E_1, E_2, \dots$ independent standard exponential variables: the
limit is the law of the sum of the top $s$ points of a Poisson point
process with intensity $e^{-x}\, dx$. For $s = 1$ it is a standard
Gumbel law.}
\end{corollary}

\tc{The calibration quantifies how much more dangerous searched products
are than searched Gaussians: a supremum of $p$ single Gaussians grows
like $\sqrt{2 \log p}$, while the product coordinates have
exponential-type tails and excess kurtosis $6$, so their maximum grows
like $\log p$ and a budget of $s$ controls buys $s$ full factors of
$\log p$ --- a small budget moves the estimate a lot. The correction
terms in $b_p$ are quantitatively essential: calibrating at the naive
$\log p$ overstates the center by about $2$ even at $p = 10^6$, while
$b_p$ is accurate up to a $O(1/\log p)$ term at realistic sizes.}

\tc{In applications the surrogate is computable from $p$ univariate
quantities --- the sample covariances of the treatment and of the
outcome with each control, together with variance estimates --- without
visiting any of the $\binom{p}{s}$ configurations; under
Assumption~\ref{ass:dgp}(i) these sample counterparts are uniformly
consistent at the required scale. The result is a sensitivity analysis
in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: given a published
specification, the analyst can report how far a coordinate search with
budget $s$ could have moved the coefficient in the dataset at hand.}
