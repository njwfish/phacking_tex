\subsection{A Separable Approximation of Coordinate Search}
\label{sec:plugin-regression}

\tc{The results of Section~\ref{sec:bounds} take the form of bounds,
which do not characterize the law of the searched supremum. That law is
in general hard to reach: the supremum in \eqref{eq:searched-sup} can
encode combinatorial hardness in $C$, $\beta_{-0}$ and $d$, and even
where it does not, the process resists direct study. This section
proposes to work instead with a surrogate process, separable, whose
supremum matches the searched one with vanishing relative error. This
requires excluding the critical regime $s\log(ep/s) \asymp n$, where only
an $O(1)$ relative error remains, and working at $s\log(ep/s) = o(n)$,
alongside stronger assumptions on the design covariance $C$, but it
provides a plug-in computable from simple order statistics, usable in a
given application to study how far the choice of specification could have
moved the estimate.

Some notation is needed to state assumptions. The variance of the outcome
net of the treatment is written
$\varsigma^2 = \Var(y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)})
= \beta_{-0}^\top C \beta_{-0} + \sigma_\varepsilon^2$. For centered
variables, $\kappa_4$ denotes the joint fourth cumulant, which vanishes
identically when the four are jointly Gaussian,
\begin{equation*}
        \kappa_4(a, b, c, d) = \E[abcd]
        - \E[ab]\,\E[cd] - \E[ac]\,\E[bd] - \E[ad]\,\E[bc].
\end{equation*}
The geometry of the controls is measured on the correlation blocks search
can reach,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-isotropy}
        \rho_s(C) = \sup_{|J| \le 2s}
        \big\| \diag(C_{JJ})^{-1/2}\, C_{JJ}\, \diag(C_{JJ})^{-1/2}
        - I \big\|_{\mathrm{op}} .
\end{equation}}

\begin{assumption}[Plug-in regularity]\label{ass:plugin}
\tc{The following hold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Local isotropy.} The controls are uncorrelated at the
scale search works on: $\rho_s(C) = o(1)$.
\item[(ii)] \emph{No control is a visible proxy for the treatment or
for the outcome.} There is a constant $C_t$ such that
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{k \le p} \big| \Corr(x_0^{(i)}, X_k^{(i)}) \big|
        \;\le\; C_t \sqrt{\frac{\log(ep/s)}{n}},
        \qquad
        \max_{k \le p}
        \big| \Corr(y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}, X_k^{(i)}) \big|
        \;\le\; C_t \sqrt{\frac{\log(ep/s)}{n}} .
\end{equation*}
\item[(iii)] \emph{The treatment is nearly uncorrelated with the outcome
material.} Its alignment with the part of the outcome the controls carry
sits below the scale at which search moves the estimate,
\begin{equation*}
        \big| d^\top \beta_{-0} \big|
        = o\big( \sigma_0\, \varsigma\, \delta_{s,n,p} \big).
\end{equation*}
\item[(iv)] \emph{Negligible fourth cumulants.} The fourth cumulants of
the searched pairs are negligible against the products they perturb:
uniformly over $j, k \le p$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
        &\kappa_4\big(x_0^{(i)}, X_j^{(i)}, x_0^{(i)}, X_k^{(i)}\big)
        = o(\sigma_0^2),
        \qquad
        \kappa_4\big(x_0^{(i)}, X_j^{(i)},
        y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}, X_k^{(i)}\big)
        = o(\sigma_0 \varsigma),
        \\
        &\kappa_4\big(y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}, X_j^{(i)},
        y^{(i)} - \beta_0 x_0^{(i)}, X_k^{(i)}\big)
        = o(\varsigma^2).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}}
\end{assumption}

\tc{Condition (i) is what supports separability and is the strong
assumption of the section. It is not a conditioning assumption: a spike
whose direction is delocalized carries correlations of order $\theta/p$,
so that $\rho_s(C) = O(s \theta / p)$ and any strength $\theta = o(p/s)$
is admitted, including strengths that send the condition number of $C$ to
infinity. What it excludes is correlation among neighbours: the Toeplitz
design $C_{jk} = \rho^{|j-k|}$ has a bounded spectrum and a bounded
condition number, yet $\rho_s(C) \ge \rho$ already at $s = 1$, so that
(i) forces $\rho \to 0$. The section then shows that a design separable
in this sense at the population level stays separable in the empirical
process, below the isometry scaling of Section~\ref{sec:bounds}.

Condition (ii) asks that no control correlate with the treatment or with
the outcome by more than $\sqrt{\log(ep/s)/n}$, the size of the largest
correlation search manufactures from noise alone across $p$ coordinates,
and controls that explain a constant share of the outcome must then
spread it over a support of order $n/\log(ep/s)$, so that the design
described is diffuse rather than sparse. Condition (iii) asks the
confounding of the omitted-variable kind to be small,
$d^\top \beta_{-0}$ being the covariance between the treatment and the
outcome net of it, and may be read as a non-triviality assumption: where
it fails the question is a different and an easier one, the displacement
being governed by $d^\top \beta_{-0}$ itself rather than by search, and
randomization gives it outright. Condition (iv) is a technical
strengthening of the subgaussian hypothesis of
Assumption~\ref{ass:dgp}(i), which the vanishing relative error requires
and which subgaussian tails do not deliver on their own.}

\begin{theorem}[Separable approximation]\label{thm:plugin-separable}
\tc{Let Assumption~\ref{ass:dgp}(i) and Assumption~\ref{ass:plugin} hold,
with $s \log(ep/s) = o(n)$, $p/s \to \infty$, and the controls scaled to
unit variance, which changes no $\hat\beta_{0,S}$. On
the same probability space as the data one can construct Gaussian
variables $(\xi_{1k})_{k \le p}$ and $(\xi_{2k})_{k \le p}$, all $2p$ of
them mutually independent, with
\begin{equation*}
        \xi_{1k} \sim N\big(\sqrt n\, d_k,\ \sigma_0^2\big),
        \qquad
        \xi_{2k} \sim N\big(\sqrt n\, (C\beta_{-0})_k,\ \varsigma^2 \big),
\end{equation*}
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:plugin-theorem}
        \sup_{S \subseteq [p],\, |S| = s} \hat\beta_{0,S}
        =
        \hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}
        + \frac{1}{n\, \sigma_0^2}
        \sum_{j=1}^{s} \left\{ -\xi_{1k}\, \xi_{2k} \right\}_{(j)}
        + o_{\mathbb P}\!\left( \frac{s \log(ep/s)}{n} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\{-\xi_{1k}\, \xi_{2k}\}_{(j)}$ denotes the $j$-th largest of the
$p$ products.}
\end{theorem}

\tc{The surrogate replaces a search over $\binom{p}{s}$ overlapping
configurations by $p$ independent coordinates, of which the largest $s$
products are kept. Each coordinate is a product of two Gaussians rather
than a Gaussian, one carrying the alignment of the treatment with control
$k$, shifted by the confounding $\sqrt n\, d_k$, the other that of the
outcome net of the treatment, shifted by the signal
$\sqrt n\, (C\beta_{-0})_k$. When $d^\top \beta_{-0} = 0$ the two factors
are independent, and it is the excess kurtosis $6$ of their product
rather than its variance that sets the growth of the maximum, of order
$\log p$ against the $\sqrt{2 \log p}$ of a Gaussian field, so that a
small budget buys a large displacement. Taking expectations of the
top-$s$ sum recovers the rate $s\log(ep/s)/n$ of
Section~\ref{sec:bounds}, and the surrogate refines it by supplying a law
around that rate.}

\tc{Computing the surrogate in an application needs four population
quantities, the covariances $d$ and $C\beta_{-0}$ of the treatment and of
the outcome with each control, and the two variances $\sigma_0^2$ and
$\varsigma^2$; checking the hypotheses needs in addition the conditioning
of the small blocks of $C$. The two vectors are the difficulty, their
coordinatewise sampling error being of the same order as the means they
carry, so that a direct plug-in reports the search realized in the
dataset at hand rather than the population surrogate, and a debiased
estimate would be needed where $p$ is proportional to $n$. What the
display supports is therefore a sensitivity statement in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}: given a published specification, how far a
search with budget $s$ could have moved the coefficient.}
