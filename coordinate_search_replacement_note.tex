\begin{abstract}
This companion note records how the coordinate-search claims in
\emph{The Geometry of p-hacking} change under the unified moment theory.
The experimental coefficient upper rate is retained.  The observational
and studentized upper bounds require a treatment-projection term omitted
from the original statements.  The coefficient and studentized lower
bounds require stochastic separation that a signal norm or a count of
supports does not provide.  The original independent-product conclusion
is not justified by entrywise fourth-cumulant bounds; the new comparison
uses joint sparse control of the score covariance.  The new theory removes restricted
conditioning from coefficient upper bounds, supplies a general
covariance-matched approximation, and gives an observable top-$s$
construction.  Scalar lower rates and independent products are recovered
under the explicit packing and score-comparison conditions that their
conclusions use.
\end{abstract}

The comparison covers size-$s$ coordinate search.  Auxiliary uniform
bounds range over $|S|\le s$.  Continuous basis search is outside its scope.

\section{Common notation and moment object}

Let $(x_0^{(i)},X^{(i)},y^{(i)})$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, be independent and
identically distributed centered observations, where
$X^{(i)}\in\R^p$.  The linear specialization is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
 y^{(i)}=\beta_0x_0^{(i)}+(X^{(i)})^\top\beta_{-0}
 +\varepsilon^{(i)},
\end{equation}
with centered noise independent of $(x_0^{(i)},X^{(i)})$.  Write
$\mathbf x_0,\mathbf y\in\R^n$ for the sample vectors and
$\mathbf X\in\R^{n\times p}$ for the control matrix, put
$\sigma_\varepsilon^2=\Var(\varepsilon)$, and let
$\boldsymbol\varepsilon$ be the noise sample.  If $M_S$ projects
orthogonally off the columns of $\mathbf X_S$, define
\begin{equation*}
 \hat\beta_{0,S}
 =\frac{\mathbf x_0^\top M_S\mathbf y}
 {\mathbf x_0^\top M_S\mathbf x_0},
 \qquad
 \hat\rho_S
 =\frac{\mathbf x_0^\top M_S\mathbf y}
 {\{\mathbf x_0^\top M_S\mathbf x_0\,
 \mathbf y^\top M_S\mathbf y\}^{1/2}},
\end{equation*}
on the event that the denominators are positive.  The scaled
partial-correlation statistic and conventional least-squares statistic
are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-definitions}
 \hat t_{0,S}=\sqrt{n-|S|-1}\,\hat\rho_S,
 \qquad
 \hat t^{\mathrm{LS}}_{0,S}
 =\sqrt{n-\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf X_S)-1}\,
 \frac{\hat\rho_S}{(1-\hat\rho_S^2)^{1/2}}.
\end{equation}

Put $C=\E(XX^\top)$ and $\sigma_0^2=\Var(x_0)>0$.  Zero-variance
controls may be discarded, and rescaling the rest changes none of their
selected spans, so take $C_{jj}=1$.  Every inverse below acts on the
range of the relevant positive semidefinite block.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:control-isotropy}
 \rho_k(C)=\sup_{|J|\le2k}
 \norm{C_{JJ}-I_{|J|}}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 L=\log(ep/s),\qquad m=sL,\qquad \delta=\sqrt{m/n}.
\end{equation*}
Define the marginal population residual and its two control scores by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-objects}
 \beta_{0,\emptyset}^\star
 =\frac{\E(x_0y)}{\sigma_0^2},
 \qquad r=y-\beta_{0,\emptyset}^\star x_0,
 \qquad d=\E(Xx_0),\qquad h=\E(Xr),
\end{equation}
and write $\sigma_r^2=\Var(r)>0$.  For $z\in\R^p$ and $k\le p$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geometric-scales}
 \norm{z}_{C,k}
 =\sup_{|J|\le k}(z_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}z_J)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \phi_k=\frac{\norm d_{C,k}^2}{\sigma_0^2},
 \qquad
 \Psi_k^r=\norm h_{C,k},
 \qquad
 \theta_k^r=\frac{(\Psi_k^r)^2}{\sigma_r^2}.
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:model}, if
$\varsigma^2=\beta_{-0}^\top C\beta_{-0}+\sigma_\varepsilon^2$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translation}
 h=C\beta_{-0}
 -\frac{d^\top\beta_{-0}}{\sigma_0^2}d,
 \qquad
 \sigma_r^2=\varsigma^2
 -\frac{(d^\top\beta_{-0})^2}{\sigma_0^2}.
\end{equation}
The original signal quantities are
$\Psi_k=\norm{C\beta_{-0}}_{C,k}$ and, under the testing null,
$\theta_k=\Psi_k^2/\Var(y)$.
The coefficient and score fluctuation scales are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-scale}
\begin{split}
 a_n&=\frac1{\sigma_0}
 \{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta
 +\Psi_s^r\delta+\sigma_r\delta^2\},\\
 a_n^{\mathrm{score}}
 &=\frac{\Psi_s^r}{\sigma_0}\delta
 +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

For $v=(x_0,r,X^\top)^\top$, let
$\Gamma=\E(vv^\top)$ and
$\hat\Gamma=n^{-1}\sum_i v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top$.  For a symmetric
matrix $A$, write
\begin{equation*}
 A_{ab\mathbin{\cdot}S}
 =A_{ab}-A_{aS}A_{SS}^{-1}A_{Sb},
 \qquad
 B_S(A)
 =\frac{A_{x_0r\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
 {A_{x_0x_0\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
 -\frac{A_{x_0r}}{A_{x_0x_0}}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-representation}
 \hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}=B_S(\hat\Gamma),
 \qquad
 B_S(\Gamma)
 =-\frac{d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}h_S}
 {\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S}.
\end{equation}
This exact identity carries the population landscape and sampling
variation in one object.  Studentization uses the same moment-matrix
method with the distinct Schur correlation
\begin{equation*}
 \tau_S(A)
 =\frac{A_{x_0y\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
 {\{A_{x_0x_0\mathbin{\cdot}S}
 A_{yy\mathbin{\cdot}S}\}^{1/2}}.
\end{equation*}

The unified results assume relative sub-Gaussianity of the augmented row
and sparse overlap: for a fixed $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-overlap}
 \norm{a^\top v}_{\psi_2}
 \le K(a^\top\Gamma a)^{1/2}
 \quad\text{for every }a\in\R^{p+2},
 \qquad
 \inf_{|S|\le s}
 \{\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S\}
 \ge c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2.
\end{equation}
When $\delta\le\delta_0$, with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
the second-order Taylor field
\begin{equation*}
 \mathcal T_{n,S}
 =B_S(\Gamma)+DB_S(\Gamma)[\hat\Gamma-\Gamma]
 +\tfrac12D^2B_S(\Gamma)
 [\hat\Gamma-\Gamma,\hat\Gamma-\Gamma]
\end{equation*}
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-expansion}
 \sup_{|S|\le s}
 |B_S(\hat\Gamma)-\mathcal T_{n,S}|
 \le C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3.
\end{equation}
If $m=o(n)$, let $G$ be centered Gaussian with
\begin{equation*}
 \Cov(G_{ab},G_{cd})=\Cov(v_av_b,v_cv_d)
\end{equation*}
and form the same Taylor polynomial with $G/\sqrt n$ in place of
$\hat\Gamma-\Gamma$.  If $B_*=\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$, the two maxima,
centered by $B_*$ and normalized by $a_n$, are within
$O\{(m/n)^{1/6}+\delta\}$ in the smooth $d_3$ metric.  This
covariance-matched maximum, rather than an independent coordinate field,
is the general stochastic approximator.  Applying the same construction
to $v_y=(x_0,y,X^\top)^\top$ and $\tau_S$ gives the studentized matched
field.  Here $d_3$ is the supremum of expectation differences over
$C^3$ test functions whose first three derivatives and level are bounded
by one.

\section{Why the corrections are substantive}

Three Gaussian examples satisfy the relevant assumptions of the original
theorems and isolate the missing structure.  They show why the added
terms and separation conditions are unavoidable.

\begin{proposition}[Three obstructions]\label{prop:obstructions}
Assume throughout that all displayed nonzero variances and coefficients
are fixed and bounded away from zero.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{Signal without multiplicity.}
Let $s=1$, $C=I_p$, and let
$x_0,X_1,\ldots,X_p,\varepsilon$ be mutually independent centered
Gaussian variables.  Set
\begin{equation*}
 y=bX_1+\varepsilon,
 \qquad L=\log(ep)\longrightarrow\infty,
 \qquad L=o(\sqrt n).
\end{equation*}
Then $\Psi_1=|b|$, but
\begin{align}
 \max_{j\le p}(\hat\beta_{0,\{j\}}-
 \hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
 &=O_{\Prob}\!\left(\frac{|b|}{\sqrt n}
 +\frac{\varsigma L}{n}\right)
 =o_{\Prob}\!\left(|b|\sqrt{\frac Ln}\right),
 \label{eq:single-signal-coefficient}\\
 \max_{j\le p}\hat t_{0,\{j\}}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
 &=O_{\Prob}(1+L/\sqrt n)=O_{\Prob}(1).
 \label{eq:single-signal-t}
\end{align}
The original coefficient and studentized lower endpoints are respectively
of orders $|b|\sqrt{L/n}$ and $\sqrt L$.

\item[(ii)] \emph{Diffuse treatment projection.}
Let $p=2s$, $s\to\infty$, $s=o(\sqrt n)$, and let
$X_1,\ldots,X_p,U$ be independent standard Gaussian variables.  For
fixed $0<\rho<1$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffuse-example}
 x_0=\frac{\rho\sigma_0}{\sqrt p}\sum_{j=1}^pX_j
 +\sigma_0\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,U,
 \qquad y=\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
with independent Gaussian noise.  Then $\Psi_s=0$ and
$\phi_s=\rho^2/2$, while for any fixed population maximizer $S^\star$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:diffuse-orders}
 \max_{|S|=s}\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,S^\star}
 =\Theta_{\Prob}\!\left(
 \frac{\sigma_\varepsilon}{\sigma_0}\sqrt{\frac sn}\right),
 \qquad
 \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
 =\Theta_{\Prob}(\sqrt s).
\end{equation}
The original upper rates are only of orders
$(\sigma_\varepsilon/\sigma_0)s/n$ and $1+s/\sqrt n$.

\item[(iii)] \emph{Cardinality without fluctuation entropy.}
Let $p=2s$, $s\to\infty$, $s=o(n)$, $C=I_p$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:entropy-example}
 x_0=\rho\sigma_0X_1+\sigma_0\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,U,
 \qquad y=\beta_0x_0+bX_1,
 \qquad 0<\rho<1,\quad \rho b<0.
\end{equation}
Every support containing coordinate $1$ is population-optimal and gives
$\hat\beta_{0,S}=\beta_0$ exactly.  With probability tending to one, no
support omitting coordinate $1$ reaches $\beta_0$.  Hence the searched
excess is zero, although
\begin{equation*}
 \ell_0=\log\binom{p-1}{s-1}\asymp s.
\end{equation*}
The original observational lower bound is strictly positive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For (i), use the uniform score reduction proved in the unified note.  If
\begin{equation*}
 Z_{1n,j}=\frac{\mathbf X_j^\top\mathbf x_0}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 Z_{2n,j}=\frac{\mathbf X_j^\top\mathbf r}{\sqrt n},
\end{equation*}
then the leading coefficient field is
$-Z_{1n,j}Z_{2n,j}/(n\sigma_0^2)$ and its uniform remainder is
$O_{\Prob}(\delta a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$.  At $j=1$ the two scores have
orders $O_{\Prob}(1)$ and $\sqrt n b+O_{\Prob}(1)$.  Over $j>1$, both
coordinatewise maxima are $O_{\Prob}(\sqrt L)$ under the stated
moderate-deviation regime.  This gives
\eqref{eq:single-signal-coefficient}; the remainder is smaller because
$L=o(\sqrt n)$.  Conditional on $(\mathbf X,\mathbf y)$, Gaussian
treatment and the exact normalized-residual representation give an
$O_{\Prob}(1)$ contribution from coordinate $1$ and
$O_{\Prob}(L/\sqrt n)$ from the irrelevant coordinates, proving
\eqref{eq:single-signal-t}.

For (ii), put
$\eta=\sigma_0^2(1-\rho^2/2)$.  Because every size-$s$ support has the
same population residual treatment variance, the support-varying part of
the anchored linear coefficient field is exactly
\begin{equation*}
 -\frac1\eta\,d_S^\top
 \frac{\mathbf X_S^\top\boldsymbol\varepsilon}{n},
 \qquad d_j=\frac{\rho\sigma_0}{\sqrt p}.
\end{equation*}
Conditional on $\boldsymbol\varepsilon$, the coordinates
$\mathbf X_j^\top\boldsymbol\varepsilon/\sqrt n$ are independent
Gaussians with variance converging to $\sigma_\varepsilon^2$.  The sum
of their largest signed half is $\Theta_{\Prob}(s)$, which gives the
coefficient order in \eqref{eq:diffuse-orders}.  The quadratic remainder
is $O_{\Prob}(s/n)$ and is smaller.  The Schur-correlation linear field
has the same selected sum divided by
$\sqrt\eta\,\sigma_\varepsilon$ rather than $\eta$ and multiplied by
$\sqrt n$; its quadratic scale is $s/\sqrt n=o(1)$.  This proves the
studentized order $\Theta_{\Prob}(\sqrt s)$.

For (iii), a regression containing $X_1$ represents
$y=\beta_0x_0+bX_1$ exactly and therefore returns $\beta_0$.  A support
omitting $X_1$ has a fixed negative population gap because $\rho b<0$.
The corrected uniform coefficient bound is $o(1)$ when $s=o(n)$, so this
gap holds uniformly with probability tending to one.  Counting the
optimal supports gives the displayed value of $\ell_0$.
\end{proof}

The greedy transfer used in the original experimental proof has a
separate deterministic obstruction.  For fixed $0<\rho<1$, let
\begin{equation*}
 R=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\ \rho&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad a=(M,1)^\top,
 \qquad b=(1,M)^\top.
\end{equation*}
Every coordinate product $a_jb_j$ is positive and
$\lambda_{\min}(R)=1-\rho$, but
\begin{equation*}
 a^\top R^{-1}b
 =\frac{2M-\rho(M^2+1)}{1-\rho^2}<0
\end{equation*}
for large $M$.  A block eigenvalue floor therefore cannot turn the
coordinatewise greedy sum into a lower bound for the inverse-Gram
objective.

\section{Replacement hierarchy and exact tradeoffs}

\subsection{Conditions recovering the lower rates}

When $m=o(n)$, any lower quantile of the complete matched Taylor maximum
transfers to the empirical search.  Canonical-metric packing of the
linear field yields the usual $\lambda\sqrt{\log M}$ leading term; the
full matched field also covers the quadratic regime.  The following
direct constructions retain the critical regime $\delta\le\delta_0$.

At the critical boundary, let $\mathcal P$ be a family of size-$s$
supports, measurable with respect to $(\mathbf X,\mathbf r)$, and have deterministic cardinality $M$ with
$\log M\gtrsim m$.  The randomized coefficient rate shown in the
experimental-upper row of the crosswalk follows if, with probability at
least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-packing}
 \min_{S\ne T\in\mathcal P}
 \norm{q_{n,S}-q_{n,T}}_C
 \gtrsim\sqrt n(\Psi_s^2+\varsigma^2\delta^2)^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{q_{n,S}}_C
 \lesssim\min_{S\ne T}\norm{q_{n,S}-q_{n,T}}_C,
\end{equation}
where $Z_{2n}=\mathbf X^\top\mathbf r/\sqrt n$,
$q_{n,S}=\iota_SC_{SS}^{-1}Z_{2n,S}$, $\iota_S$ is coordinate
embedding, and
$\norm z_C=(z^\top Cz)^{1/2}$.  A constant-weight code drawn from a
two-sided comparable score pool verifies \eqref{eq:score-packing} under
local isotropy small enough relative to the score-comparability
constants.  Uniform exponential multiplier minoration is also
required; Gaussian treatment has it.  These conditions recover the old
tight rate in both signal and quadratic-noise branches.

For studentized search, on the event that the residual outcome vectors
below are nonzero, define
\begin{equation*}
 u_{n,S}=\frac{M_S\mathbf y}{\norm{M_S\mathbf y}_2},
 \qquad
 \nu_{n,S}
 =\sqrt{\frac{n-|S|-1}
 {n-\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf X_S)}},
 \qquad
 u_{n,S}^{\Delta}
 =\nu_{n,S}u_{n,S}-\nu_{n,\emptyset}u_{n,\emptyset}.
\end{equation*}
Under the studentized assumptions in the crosswalk, random assignment,
and multiplier minoration, let $\mathcal P$ be a family of size-$s$
supports, measurable with respect to $(\mathbf X,\mathbf y)$, and have deterministic
cardinality $M$ with $\log M\gtrsim m$.  If, with probability at least
$1-Ce^{-cm}$, there is a $\lambda_n$ such that
\begin{equation*}
 \min_{S\ne T\in\mathcal P}
 \norm{u_{n,S}^{\Delta}-u_{n,T}^{\Delta}}_2\ge\lambda_n,
 \qquad
 \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{u_{n,S}^{\Delta}}_2\le K\lambda_n,
 \qquad
 \lambda_n\gtrsim(\theta_s+\delta^2)^{1/2},
\end{equation*}
then the original studentized lower rate follows.  The score-to-direction lemma in the
unified note gives sufficient residual-floor, score-radius, rank-scale,
and angular or energy-balance conditions for this packing.  Proposition
\ref{prop:obstructions}(i) proves that some multiplicity condition is
necessary; it does not make that particular sufficient bridge necessary.

\subsection{From the matched field to \texorpdfstring{top-$s$}{top-s} products}

Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-isotropy}
 \Omega=\Cov\!\begin{pmatrix}
 (Xx_0-d)/\sigma_0\\ (Xr-h)/\sigma_r
 \end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}
 =\sup_{|J|\le2s}
 \norm{\Omega_{(1,2)J,(1,2)J}-I_{2|J|}}_{\mathrm{op}},
\end{equation}
where $(1,2)J$ contains both score coordinates associated with $J$.
The reductions below retain relative sub-Gaussianity, sparse overlap,
and the small-$\delta$ regime; each arrow adds the displayed premise:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hierarchy}
\begin{aligned}
 \text{matched Schur field}
 &\xrightarrow{\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta}
 \text{covariance-aware scores},\\
 \text{covariance-aware scores}
 &\xrightarrow{\rho_s(C)=o(1)}
 \text{observable top-$s$ sort},\\
 \text{observable top-$s$ sort}
 &\xrightarrow{\substack{\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}=o(1)\\
 \text{mean cap},\ m\to\infty,\ m=o(n)}}
 \text{independent products}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the first reduction, set
\begin{equation*}
 Z_{1n}=\frac{\mathbf X^\top\mathbf x_0}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 Z_{2n}=\frac{\mathbf X^\top\mathbf r}{\sqrt n},
 \qquad
 T_C(a,b)=\max_{|S|=s}[-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S],
\end{equation*}
Sparse treatment alignment then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance-aware}
 \max_{|S|=s}(\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
 =\frac{T_C(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})}{n\sigma_0^2}
 +R_n^C.
\end{equation}
With probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
$|R_n^C|\le C\delta a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$.  This retains arbitrary
sparse covariance geometry, possibly with a hard combinatorial search.

Let
\begin{equation*}
 \hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf y-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}\mathbf x_0,
 \quad
 \hat Z_{2n}=\frac{\mathbf X^\top\hat{\mathbf r}}{\sqrt n},
 \quad
 \hat\sigma_0^2=\frac{\mathbf x_0^\top\mathbf x_0}{n}.
\end{equation*}
Local isotropy converts \eqref{eq:covariance-aware} into the observable
static sort
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-sort}
 \max_{|S|=s}(\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
 =\frac1{n\hat\sigma_0^2}
 \sum_{t=1}^s
 \{-Z_{1n,j}\hat Z_{2n,j}:j\le p\}_{(t)}
 +R_n^{\mathrm{sort}}.
\end{equation}
Here $(t)$ denotes decreasing order, and with probability at least
$1-Ce^{-cm}$,
$|R_n^{\mathrm{sort}}|\le
C\{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$.
This observable top-$s$ construction applies directly to the realized
sub-Gaussian scores.  At critical $\delta$, its error is
$o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$ when $m=o(n)$ and $\rho_s(C)=o(1)$.

For the final arrow, suppose $m\to\infty$, $m=o(n)$, $\rho_s(C)=o(1)$,
$\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}=o(1)$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-cap}
 \sup_{|J|\le s}\left\{
 \frac{n\norm{d_J}^2}{\sigma_0^2}
 +\frac{n\norm{h_J}^2}{\sigma_r^2}
 \right\}\lesssim m.
\end{equation}
Let all $2p$ variables below be mutually independent, with
\begin{equation*}
 \xi_{1j}\sim N(\sqrt n\,d_j,\sigma_0^2),
 \qquad
 \xi_{2j}\sim N(\sqrt n\,h_j,\sigma_r^2).
\end{equation*}
After composing matched-moment invariance with the score and static
reductions above, there is an enlarged-space coupling such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:independent-products}
 \max_{|S|=s}(\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
 =\frac1{n\sigma_0^2}
 \sum_{t=1}^s\{-\xi_{1j}\xi_{2j}:j\le p\}_{(t)}
 +o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}}).
\end{equation}
Sparse score isotropy is sufficient, not asserted necessary.  It follows,
for example, from local isotropy, the mean cap, and sparse operator-norm
control of the fourth-cumulant blocks; entrywise $o(1)$ cumulants do not
imply that control when $s$ grows.

Under the confounding bridge
$\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta$ and
$|d^\top\beta_{-0}|=o(\sigma_0\varsigma\delta)$,
\eqref{eq:translation} permits the independent second factors to be
written with mean $\sqrt n(C\beta_{-0})_j$ and variance $\varsigma^2$.
For jointly Gaussian rows, local isotropy and the mean cap also give
sparse score isotropy, recovering the intended independent-product formula.

\section{Other manuscript corrections}

Three ancillary statements require corresponding changes.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The experimental headline should concern
$\max_S\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}$ at the full
$\sigma_0^{-1}(\Psi_s\delta+\varsigma\delta^2)$ scale.  Centering at
$\beta_0$ adds the fixed-specification fluctuation
$O_{\Prob}\{\varsigma/(\sigma_0\sqrt n)\}$; a matching lower headline
also needs packing and minoration.
\item The plug-in introduction gives a
$s\sqrt{\log(ep/s)}/n$ remainder, while its theorem is stated at the
$s\log(ep/s)/n$ scale.  The latter is the scale of the separable result.
\item The discussion compares correlation-normalized sparse condition
numbers with the condition number of an unnormalized full covariance and
invokes interlacing.  Interlacing supports that comparison only after the
same diagonal normalization is applied to both matrices.
\end{enumerate}
