\title{A Unified Gaussian Moment Field for Specification Search}
\author{Th\'eo Voldoire and Nic Fishman}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Sparse specification search is a search over Schur complements of one
empirical second-moment matrix.  A support-compressed expansion remains
valid at the critical restricted-isometry scale and yields the
experimental, observational, and studentized bounds.  In the subcritical
regime, a covariance-matched Gaussian moment field preserves both the
linear influence process and the quadratic terms.  Sparse treatment
alignment reduces this field, throughout $s\log(ep/s)=o(n)$, to a matched
Gaussian score field while leaving the outcome signal unrestricted.
Every support increment of the reduced field is exactly a product of two
residual scores.  Polarization then gives an $O(ps^2)$ forward search with
a realized regret certificate, and relative restricted isometry transfers
a sparse restricted-conditioning bound to that certificate.
The same bound is an exact finite-sample regression certificate when the
outcome signal is nonnegligible and in observational designs.  Forward
products are
first-order accurate under block-diagonal covariance with arbitrary
within-block anisotropy, while local isotropy recovers the top-$s$ product
law.  The matched field also gives the corresponding upper, packing-lower,
independent-coordinate, and studentized results.
\end{abstract}

\section{The sparse moment field}

For independent and identically distributed observations
$i=1,\ldots,n$, consider the centered linear model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
        y^{(i)}
        =\beta_0x^{(i)}+(X^{(i)})^\top\beta+\varepsilon^{(i)},
        \qquad X^{(i)}\in\R^p.
\end{equation}
To keep the moment formulas light, abbreviate the focal regressor
$x_0$, the controls $X_{-0}$, and their coefficient vector $\beta_{-0}$
as $x$, $X$, and $\beta$.
The design is jointly sub-Gaussian relative to its covariance, and the
centered sub-Gaussian noise is independent of the design.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \beta_\emptyset^*=\frac{\E[x^{(i)}y^{(i)}]}{\E[(x^{(i)})^2]},
        \qquad
        r^{(i)}=y^{(i)}-\beta_\emptyset^*x^{(i)}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\E[x^{(i)}r^{(i)}]=0$.  Set
\begin{equation*}
        \E[(x^{(i)})^2]=\sigma_0^2,
        \qquad
        \E[(r^{(i)})^2]=\sigma_r^2,
        \qquad
        \E[(\varepsilon^{(i)})^2]=\sigma_\varepsilon^2,
        \qquad
        \E[X^{(i)}(X^{(i)})^\top]=C,
\end{equation*}
and assume $\sigma_0\sigma_r>0$.  Rescaling the controls changes none of
their selected spans, so $C_{jj}=1$ throughout.
Under random assignment, $\beta_\emptyset^*=\beta_0$; hence
$r=y-\beta_0x$ and $\sigma_r^2=\Var(y-\beta_0x)$.

The augmented row and its second-moment matrix are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-matrix}
        v^{(i)}=
        \begin{pmatrix}x^{(i)}\\ r^{(i)}\\ X^{(i)}\end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        \Gamma=\E[v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top]
        =\begin{pmatrix}
        \sigma_0^2&0&d^\top\\
        0&\sigma_r^2&h^\top\\
        d&h&C
        \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $d=\E[X^{(i)}x^{(i)}]$ and $h=\E[X^{(i)}r^{(i)}]$.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-moment-matrix}
        \hat\Gamma
        =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top.
\end{equation}

For an empirical or population positive semidefinite control block,
every inverse and inverse square root below acts on its range;
equivalently, it is the Moore--Penrose inverse in the original
coordinates.  Perturbations of a singular population block are first
compressed to that fixed range, where the inverse is ordinary.  For a
symmetric matrix $A$, two indices $u,v\in\{x,r\}$, and a control set
$S$, define the residual moment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-moment}
        A_{uv\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        =A_{uv}-A_{uS}A_{SS}^{-1}A_{Sv}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient contrast associated with $S$ is the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-functional}
        B_S(A)
        =\frac{A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}}{A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        -\frac{A_{xr}}{A_{xx}},
        \qquad B_\emptyset(A)=0.
\end{equation}
This function carries both the observational population landscape and the
stochastic search around it.  It is invariant under replacing $r$ by
$r+c x$ for any scalar $c$, because both ratios in
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} then increase by $c$.  The empirical
field can therefore be computed from $(x,y,X)$ even though the orthogonal
residual $r$ makes its population geometry clearer.

For $S\subseteq[p]$, let $M_S$ project orthogonally off the columns of
$X_S$, and write
\begin{equation*}
        \hat\beta_{0,S}
        =\frac{x^\top M_Sy}{x^\top M_Sx}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[Exact moment representation]\label{lem:exact-moment}
For every support with $x^\top M_Sx>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-moment-representation}
        \hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}
        =B_S(\hat\Gamma).
\end{equation}
At the population matrix,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-landscape}
        B_S(\Gamma)
        =\beta_{0,S}^*-\beta_{0,\emptyset}^*
        =-\frac{d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}h_S}
        {\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $y=\beta_\emptyset^*x+r$, the coefficient on $x$ after controlling
for $X_S$ is $\beta_\emptyset^*$ plus the coefficient on $x$ in the
regression of $r$ on $(x,X_S)$.  The partitioned-regression formula makes
the latter coefficient
$\hat\Gamma_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}/
\hat\Gamma_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}$.  Subtracting the corresponding
coefficient with $S=\emptyset$ proves
\eqref{eq:exact-moment-representation}.  Substitution of
\eqref{eq:moment-matrix} into \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} proves
\eqref{eq:population-landscape}.
\end{proof}

The exact moment field therefore depends only on selected column spans.
The following compression shows that singular population blocks require
no separate regularity condition.

\begin{lemma}[Support compression]\label{lem:support-compression}
Let
\begin{equation*}
        C_{JJ}=U_J\Lambda_JU_J^\top,
        \qquad \Lambda_J\succ0,
\end{equation*}
where the columns of $U_J$ span the range of $C_{JJ}$, and put
\begin{equation*}
        \widetilde X_J=X_JU_J\Lambda_J^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Then, almost surely,
\begin{equation*}
        X_J=X_JU_JU_J^\top,
        \qquad
        \operatorname{span}(X_J)=\operatorname{span}(\widetilde X_J),
        \qquad
        \E[(\widetilde X_J^{(i)})^\top\widetilde X_J^{(i)}]=I.
\end{equation*}
The empirical and matched Gaussian moment perturbations preserve this
fixed range.  Consequently every sparse moment bound and derivative
calculation may be carried out in the compressed coordinates, whose
dimension is at most $|J|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $a\in\ker(C_{JJ})$, then
$\E[(X_J^{(i)}a)^2]=0$, so $X_J^{(i)}a=0$ almost surely.  This proves the
first two identities and also shows that $d_J$ and $h_J$ lie in the same
range.  The last identity follows from the spectral decomposition.
Writing
\begin{equation*}
        \hat R_J=\frac1n\widetilde X_J^\top\widetilde X_J
\end{equation*}
gives
\begin{equation*}
        \hat C_{JJ}
        =U_J\Lambda_J^{1/2}\hat R_J\Lambda_J^{1/2}U_J^\top.
\end{equation*}
Every row-moment perturbation therefore vanishes on the orthogonal
complement of the same range.  The corresponding linear functionals of
the matched Gaussian perturbation have zero variance and hence vanish
almost surely as well.  On the relative restricted-isometry event,
$\hat R_J$ is invertible and its ordinary inverse represents the
Moore--Penrose inverse of $\hat C_{JJ}$ in the original coordinates.
\end{proof}

For each $J$, let $\Pi_J=U_JU_J^\top$ be the projection onto the range of
$C_{JJ}$.  A symmetric perturbation $H$ is range-compatible if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:range-compatible}
        H_{Jx},H_{Jr}\in\operatorname{range}(C_{JJ}),
        \qquad H_{JJ}=\Pi_JH_{JJ}\Pi_J,
        \qquad |J|\le2s.
\end{equation}
Every empirical row-moment perturbation and every matched Gaussian
perturbation is range-compatible.  All derivatives below are taken on
this fixed-rank affine face.  When a function is evaluated on an ambient
matrix, its support-specific blocks are projected onto the face first.

The population residual treatment variance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
        \eta(S)
        =\Gamma_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        =\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S.
\end{equation}
\begin{assumption}[Sparse moment regime]\label{ass:sparse-moments}
The augmented row $v^{(i)}$ is sub-Gaussian relative to $\Gamma$, with a
uniformly bounded constant, and
\begin{equation*}
        \inf_{|S|\le s}\eta(S)\ge c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2
\end{equation*}
for a constant $c_{\mathrm{ov}}>0$.
\end{assumption}

Overlap is the domain condition of
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional}; relative sub-Gaussianity supplies
uniform moment concentration on the compressed sparse blocks.
Under the model above, the relative sub-Gaussian condition on $v^{(i)}$
follows from the stated design and noise conditions.  Indeed,
\begin{equation*}
        r^{(i)}=(X^{(i)})^\top\beta+\varepsilon^{(i)}
        -\frac{d^\top\beta}{\sigma_0^2}x^{(i)},
\end{equation*}
so every linear form in $v^{(i)}$ is the sum of an independent design
form and a multiple of $\varepsilon^{(i)}$; its sub-Gaussian norm is
therefore bounded by a constant times its own standard deviation.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:search-scale}
        L=\log(ep/s),
        \qquad m=sL,
        \qquad \delta=\delta_{s,n,p}=\sqrt{m/n}.
\end{equation}
The regime $m=o(n)$ makes the empirical sparse blocks a vanishing relative
perturbation of their population values.  The stronger condition used for
the Gaussian replacement will be stated where it enters.

\section{Matched Gaussian moments}

The central result replaces the searched empirical moment field by the
same smooth functional of a covariance-matched Gaussian matrix.  The
empirical matrix in \eqref{eq:empirical-moment-matrix} is an average of
row outer products, so its matched Gaussian perturbation is a
centered symmetric matrix $G$ defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-moment-matrix}
        \Cov(G_{ab},G_{cd})
        =\Cov(v_a^{(i)}v_b^{(i)},v_c^{(i)}v_d^{(i)}),
        \qquad a,b,c,d\in\{x,r,1,\ldots,p\}.
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussianized-moment-matrix}
        \Gamma^G=\Gamma+\frac{G}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}
The Gaussian perturbation need not be positive semidefinite outside the
sparse neighborhood of $\Gamma$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-localization}
constructs smooth global versions of $B_S$ that agree with
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} on that neighborhood.  Denote these
extensions by $B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}$.  Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments}
and the moment bound below put both endpoint matrices in the unmodified
region with probability tending to one.
The covariance in \eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix} retains the fourth
moments of the original rows.  The Gaussian field
$\{B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G):|S|=s\}$ therefore preserves the population landscape,
the random treatment and outcome scores, the control Gram fluctuations,
and the support-dependent denominators.

For a symmetric perturbation $H$, define its relative sparse size by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-size}
\begin{split}
        \lvert H\rvert_s
        ={}&\frac{|H_{xx}|}{\sigma_0^2}
        +\frac{|H_{xr}|}{\sigma_0\sigma_r}\ +
        \sup_{|J|\le2s}\bigg\{
        \frac{\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{Jx}}}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{Jr}}}{\sigma_r}\\
        &\hspace{43mm}
        +\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{JJ}C_{JJ}^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Sparse hybrid moments]\label{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments}
Let $Y_i=v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$, and let $Y_i^G$ be independent
centered Gaussian matrices with the covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.  Uniformly over every deterministic
hybrid pattern $\widetilde Y_i\in\{Y_i,Y_i^G\}$ and each fixed
$1\le q\le6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-concentration}
        \left\|
        \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde Y_i\right|_s
        \right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim\delta,
        \qquad sL=o(n).
\end{equation}
For $m+t\lesssim n$, the corresponding tail bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-tail}
\Prob\left(
\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde Y_i\right|_s
>C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{m+t}{n}}+\frac{m+t}{n}\right\}
\right)
\le 2e^{-ct}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-row-moment}
        \max_i
        \left\|\lvert\widetilde Y_i\rvert_s\right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim sL.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation*}
        \lvert\hat\Gamma-\Gamma\rvert_s
        +\frac{\lvert G\rvert_s}{\sqrt n}
        =O_{\Prob}(\delta).
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed support and unit directions, every entry and quadratic form in
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} is a centered product of two standardized
sub-Gaussian linear forms and therefore has uniformly bounded
$\psi_1$ norm.  A $1/4$-net for each sparse sphere, followed by a union over
the supports, has logarithmic size $O(sL)$.  Bernstein's inequality on
this net gives \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail}; tail integration gives
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-concentration}.  Gaussian net directions have
sub-Gaussian tails with the same variance bound, so the same conclusions
hold for every deterministic hybrid pattern.  For one original row, the
control-block term is bounded by the squared norm of the largest $2s$
standardized coordinates, whose fixed $L_q$ norm is $O(sL)$.  The vector
terms are smaller by Cauchy--Schwarz.  For $Y_i^G$, a union over the same
Gaussian net gives an $O(\sqrt{sL})$ bound and hence the weaker stated
$O(sL)$ bound.  This proves \eqref{eq:one-row-moment}.
\end{proof}

The control-block component of \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} is the
relative restricted-isometry event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-rip}
        \sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}\hat C_{JJ}C_{JJ}^{-1/2}
        -\Pi_J}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \lesssim\delta,
        \qquad \hat C=\frac{X^\top X}{n}.
\end{equation}
It is centered at the population covariance $C$.  The local-isotropy
condition used later instead compares $C$ with $I$; the former stabilizes
the empirical field, while the latter makes its search separable.

The next lemma records the deterministic part of the approximation.  Let
$DB_S(\Gamma)[H]$ and $D^2B_S(\Gamma)[H,H]$ denote the first two
derivatives of \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} along $H$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform moment expansion]\label{lem:uniform-expansion}
There is a constant $c>0$, depending only on the overlap constant, such
that every range-compatible $H$ with $|H|_s\le c$ satisfies
\begin{align}
\sup_{|S|\le s}
\left|B_S(\Gamma+H)-B_S(\Gamma)-DB_S(\Gamma)[H]\right|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}|H|_s^2,
\label{eq:first-order-remainder}\\
\sup_{|S|\le s}
\left|B_S(\Gamma+H)-B_S(\Gamma)-DB_S(\Gamma)[H]
-\frac12D^2B_S(\Gamma)[H,H]\right|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}|H|_s^3.
\label{eq:second-order-remainder}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The calculation takes place in the compressed coordinates of
Lemma~\ref{lem:support-compression}.  For a fixed $S$, write
$K_S=C_{SS}^{-1}$, $\alpha=K_Sd_S$, $\gamma=K_Sh_S$, and
$\eta=\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top\alpha$.  The inverse identity
\begin{equation*}
        (C_{SS}+H_{SS})^{-1}
        =K_S-K_SH_{SS}K_S+K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}K_S
        -K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}(C_{SS}+H_{SS})^{-1}
\end{equation*}
expands both Schur complements in
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional}.  The relative norm in
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} bounds every linear term by its population
standard deviation, while Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S}\le\sigma_0$ and
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S}\le\sigma_r$.  Overlap bounds the reciprocal
denominators by $(c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2)^{-1}$.  Expanding each scalar
reciprocal through second order and collecting terms of degree at least
two proves \eqref{eq:first-order-remainder}.  Retaining the quadratic
terms leaves products of degree at least three and proves
\eqref{eq:second-order-remainder}, uniformly in $S$.
\end{proof}

The deterministic expansion and the sparse tail bound give a field
approximation throughout the critical regime, without replacing the
empirical moments by Gaussian ones.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-second-order-field}
        \mathcal H_{n,S}
        =DB_S(\Gamma)[\hat\Gamma-\Gamma]
        +\frac12D^2B_S(\Gamma)
        [\hat\Gamma-\Gamma,\hat\Gamma-\Gamma].
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Critical empirical expansion]
\label{thm:critical-empirical-expansion}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and
$\delta\le\delta_0$ for a sufficiently small constant.  With probability
at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$, every searched coefficient is defined and,
simultaneously over $|S|\le s$,
\begin{align}
\left|B_S(\hat\Gamma)-B_S(\Gamma)
-DB_S(\Gamma)[\hat\Gamma-\Gamma]\right|
&\le C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2,
\label{eq:critical-first-order-expansion}\\
\left|B_S(\hat\Gamma)-B_S(\Gamma)-\mathcal H_{n,S}\right|
&\le C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3.
\label{eq:critical-second-order-expansion}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-field-maximum}
\left|
\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma)
-\max_{|S|=s}\{B_S(\Gamma)+\mathcal H_{n,S}\}
\right|
\le C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3.
\end{equation}
For any fixed support $S_0$, subtracting its value from both fields
changes the right side of \eqref{eq:critical-field-maximum} by at most a
factor of two.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments}, with $t=m$, gives
$|\hat\Gamma-\Gamma|_s\le C\delta$ outside an event of probability
$Ce^{-cm}$.  Support compression makes the same statement valid when a
population block is singular.  On this event the compressed empirical
Gram blocks are invertible, and overlap gives
$\hat\Gamma_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}\ge
c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2/2$ uniformly over $|S|\le s$.  Thus every
searched coefficient is defined.  Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion}
gives the two displays.  The maximum statement follows from
$|\max_S a_S-\max_S b_S|\le\max_S|a_S-b_S|$.
\end{proof}

For $z\in\R^p$, define
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{z}_{C,k}
        =\sup_{|J|\le k}
        \left(z_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}z_J\right)^{1/2},
\end{equation*}
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-sizes}
        \phi_k=\frac{\norm{d}_{C,k}^2}{\sigma_0^2},
        \qquad
        \widetilde\Psi_k=\norm{h}_{C,k},
        \qquad
        a_n=\frac1{\sigma_0}
        \{(\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s)\delta
        +\sigma_r\delta^2\}.
\end{equation}
The first two terms in $a_n$ bound the treatment and outcome parts of the
linear field; the last is the size of the quadratic field when that
derivative degenerates.  The tilde distinguishes the residual score $h$
from the outcome score $C\beta$; they agree under random assignment.
Indeed, the model gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-translation}
\begin{split}
        h&=C\beta-\frac{d^\top\beta}{\sigma_0^2}d,
        \qquad
        \sigma_r^2
        =\beta^\top C\beta+\sigma_\varepsilon^2
        -\frac{(d^\top\beta)^2}{\sigma_0^2},\\
        \widetilde\Psi_k
        &\le \Psi_k
        +\frac{|d^\top\beta|}{\sigma_0^2}\norm{d}_{C,k},
        \qquad
        \Psi_k=\norm{C\beta}_{C,k}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Under random assignment, the residual-score quantities equal their
outcome-score counterparts.  Away from random assignment,
\eqref{eq:score-translation} records the exact alignment correction.

The same calculation gives the derivative bounds used in the Gaussian
replacement.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse derivatives]\label{lem:sparse-derivatives}
If $A-\Gamma$ is range-compatible and $|A-\Gamma|_s\le c$ for a
sufficiently small constant $c$, then for $j=1,2,3$ and
range-compatible symmetric directions $H_1,\ldots,H_j$,
\begin{align}
|DB_S(A)[H_1]|
&\lesssim \frac1{\sigma_0}
\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
+\sigma_r|A-\Gamma|_s\}|H_1|_s,
\label{eq:first-derivative-bound}\\
|D^jB_S(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
\prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_s,
\qquad j=2,3.
\label{eq:higher-derivative-bound}
\end{align}
The constants are uniform over $|S|\le s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $K_A=A_{SS}^{-1}$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        D A_{uv\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]
        ={}&H_{uv}-H_{uS}K_AA_{Sv}-A_{uS}K_AH_{Sv}\\
        &+A_{uS}K_AH_{SS}K_AA_{Sv}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        DB_S(A)[H]
        ={}&\frac{D A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]}
        {A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        -\frac{A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        D A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]}
        {A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}^2}\\
        &-\frac{H_{xr}}{A_{xx}}
        +\frac{A_{xr}H_{xx}}{A_{xx}^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
At $A=\Gamma$, every noncancelling term contains one factor of either
$C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S$ or $C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S$.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives the
first two terms in \eqref{eq:first-derivative-bound}.  Every further
derivative uses
\begin{equation*}
        D(A_{SS}^{-1})[H]
        =-A_{SS}^{-1}H_{SS}A_{SS}^{-1}
\end{equation*}
and inserts another relative perturbation in one of these products.
Overlap bounds the reciprocal treatment Schur complement.  The second
derivative along the segment from $\Gamma$ to $A$ adds at most
$\sigma_r|A-\Gamma|_s$ to the first derivative.  The bounds
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S}\le\sigma_0$ and
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S}\le\sigma_r$ control every second- and
third-order term, proving \eqref{eq:higher-derivative-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smooth localization]\label{lem:smooth-localization}
The functions $B_S$ admit three times continuously differentiable
ambient extensions $B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}$, obtained by projection onto the
support-specific fixed-rank face, that agree with
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} on
range-compatible $A-\Gamma$ with $|A-\Gamma|_s\le c$.  They satisfy, for
every symmetric $A$ and $H$,
\begin{align*}
        |DB_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(A)[H]|
        &\lesssim\frac1{\sigma_0}
        \left\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
        +\sigma_r(|A-\Gamma|_s\wedge1)\right\}|H|_s,\\
        |D^jB_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
        &\lesssim\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_s,
        \qquad j=2,3.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The blocks $A_{Sx}$, $A_{Sr}$, and $A_{SS}$ are first projected onto the
range of $C_{SS}$.
A smooth radial clip is then applied in the compressed coordinates to
the full non-Gram moment tuple, including
$A_{xx}-\sigma_0^2$, $A_{xr}$, $A_{Sx}-d_S$, and $A_{Sr}-h_S$.
Each scalar reciprocal is replaced by a smooth bounded function that equals
$u\mapsto u^{-1}$ on the overlap interval.  For a control block, use
\begin{equation*}
        U_S\Lambda_S^{-1/2}
        f(\Lambda_S^{-1/2}U_S^\top A_{SS}U_S\Lambda_S^{-1/2})
        \Lambda_S^{-1/2}U_S^\top,
\end{equation*}
where the fixed matrix function $f$ equals inversion near $I$ and has
bounded Fr\'echet derivatives through order three in operator norm.  Such
an $f$ follows from a smooth spectral cutoff on a fixed neighborhood of
the positive cone.  The chain rule and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-derivatives} give the displayed bounds.  All
thresholds are fixed by the overlap constant, so the constants are
uniform in $S$ and $p$.
\end{proof}

The first derivative has the regression influence form.  In a population
row, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-residuals}
\begin{aligned}
        \alpha_S&=C_{SS}^{-1}d_S,
        &u_S&=x-X_S^\top\alpha_S,\\
        \gamma_S&=C_{SS}^{-1}h_S,
        &e_S&=r-X_S^\top\gamma_S-B_S(\Gamma)u_S.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then, for the centered rank-one direction
$v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-function}
        DB_S(\Gamma)
        [v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma]
        =\frac{u_S^{(i)}e_S^{(i)}}{\eta(S)}
        -\frac{x^{(i)}r^{(i)}}{\sigma_0^2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the same moment expansion contains the observational first-order
field.

For real random variables $U,V$, let
\begin{equation*}
        d_3(U,V)
        =\sup_f|\E f(U)-\E f(V)|,
\end{equation*}
where the supremum is over three times continuously differentiable $f$
with $\max_{0\le j\le3}\norm{f^{(j)}}_\infty\le1$.

\begin{lemma}[One-sided smooth transfer]\label{lem:smooth-lower-transfer}
If $d_3(U,V)\le\omega$, then, for every $t\in\R$ and
$0<\zeta\le1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:smooth-lower-transfer}
        \Prob(U\le t-\zeta)
        \le \Prob(V<t)+C\omega\zeta^{-3}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A three times continuously differentiable function can be chosen to
equal one on $(-\infty,t-\zeta]$, vanish on $[t,\infty)$, and have
$j$th derivative bounded by $C\zeta^{-j}$.  Dividing this function by
$C\zeta^{-3}$ places it in the defining class for $d_3$ and proves
\eqref{eq:smooth-lower-transfer}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Matched moment approximation]\label{thm:matched-moments}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and $m^7=o(n)$.  If
\begin{equation*}
        B_* = \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma),
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-moment-approximation}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma)-B_*}{a_n},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)-B_*}{a_n}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
If $S^\star\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$, then also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-observational-field}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        &\frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma)-B_{S^\star}(\hat\Gamma)}{a_n},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        -B_{S^\star}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)}{a_n}
        \bigg)\\
        &\hspace{45mm}\lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $Y_i=v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$.  A Lindeberg path replaces the
$Y_i$ one at a time by independent centered Gaussian matrices with the covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.  The first two Taylor terms cancel at
each replacement.  Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} applies to every
deterministic partially replaced sum.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \overline B_S(A)=\frac{B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(A)-B_*}{a_n}.
\end{equation*}
Since
$a_n\ge\sigma_0^{-1}\delta
\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
+\sigma_r\delta\}$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-localization} gives, along a replacement path with
leave-one-row base $A_i$ and row direction $Y/n$,
\begin{align*}
        |D\overline B_S(A)[Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s}{\sqrt{mn}}\,\Lambda_i(Y),\\
        |D^2\overline B_S(A)[Y/n,Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s^2}{mn},\\
        |D^3\overline B_S(A)[Y/n,Y/n,Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s^3}{mn^2}.
\end{align*}
Here
\begin{equation*}
        \Lambda_i(Y)
        =1+\frac{|A_i-\Gamma|_s\wedge1}{\delta}
        +\frac{|Y|_s}{n\delta}.
\end{equation*}
The base $A_i$ is independent of the replacement row; the hybrid-moment
lemma applies to it after inserting one deterministic zero term.  The $L_6$
bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments}, H\"older's inequality,
and $n\delta=\sqrt{mn}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-row-moment}
        \E\{|Y|_s^3\Lambda_i(Y)^3\}\lesssim m^3.
\end{equation}

The smooth maximum is
\begin{equation*}
        F_\lambda(A)
        =\frac1{\lambda m}
        \log\sum_{|S|=s}\exp\{\lambda m \overline B_S(A)\}.
\end{equation*}
Because $\log\binom ps\le m$, this changes the maximum by at most
$1/\lambda$ at each endpoint, for a total smoothing cost at most
$2/\lambda$.  Softmax differentiation and the preceding display bound
the third derivative in one row direction by
\begin{equation*}
        C|Y|_s^3\Lambda_i(Y)^3\left\{
        \frac1{mn^2}
        +\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt m\,n^{3/2}}
        +\frac{\lambda^2\sqrt m}{n^{3/2}}
        \right\}.
\end{equation*}
For the choice of $\lambda$ below, $\lambda m\ge1$.  The terms produced
by composing with a test function in the definition of $d_3$, including
$(F_\lambda')^3$ and $F_\lambda'F_\lambda''$, are then absorbed by the
same display.  Taylor's formula, cancellation of the first two moments,
and \eqref{eq:localized-row-moment} therefore bound the sum of the
replacement errors by
\begin{equation*}
        C\left\{
        \frac{m^2}{n}
        +\frac{\lambda m^{5/2}}{\sqrt n}
        +\frac{\lambda^2m^{7/2}}{\sqrt n}
        \right\}.
\end{equation*}
Smooth localization makes this calculation global, so no simultaneous
good event over the $n$ hybrids is used.  At the two endpoints, the
localized and original statistics differ only if the relative sparse
distance exceeds $c$.  The $L_6$ endpoint bound makes the contribution to
$d_3$ at most $C\delta^6\le C\delta$.  Choosing
$\lambda=(\sqrt n/m^{7/2})^{1/3}$ proves
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-approximation}.  Subtracting one fixed support
changes neither the entropy nor the derivative bounds, which proves the
final statement.
\end{proof}

The seventh power in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments} comes from the
rank-one control Gram term, whose one-row sparse operator norm is of order
$m$.  Sparse treatment alignment removes this term from the leading field
and permits a sharper score replacement.

\section{Gaussian score reduction}

The general field simplifies when the treatment has little population
projection on the controls.  For $a,b\in\R^p$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chaos-functional}
        Q_S(a,b)=-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S,
        \qquad
        T_C(a,b)=\max_{|S|=s}Q_S(a,b).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian score pair contained in $G$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-scores}
        Z_1=\sqrt n\,d+G_{Xx},
        \qquad
        Z_2=\sqrt n\,h+G_{Xr}.
\end{equation}
Its covariance is matched to the two centered row scores
$X^{(i)}x^{(i)}-d$ and $X^{(i)}r^{(i)}-h$; it is the corresponding
subvector of the full moment covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.

\begin{theorem}[Sparse-treatment score reduction]\label{thm:chaos-degeneration}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment}
        \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta.
\end{equation}
No restriction is imposed on $h$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-chaos-degeneration}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        =\frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3
        \right).
\end{equation}
The empirical scores
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-scores}
        Z_{1n}=\frac{X^\top x}{\sqrt n},
        \qquad
        Z_{2n}=\frac{X^\top r}{\sqrt n}
\end{equation}
satisfy the same deterministic reduction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma)
        =\frac{T_C(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})}{n\sigma_0^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3
        \right).
\end{equation}
If $\delta\le\delta_0$, each remainder is bounded by a constant times
its displayed scale with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a range-compatible perturbation $H$, write
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
        a_S=d_S+H_{Sx},\qquad b_S=h_S+H_{Sr},\\
        \widetilde\sigma_0^2=\sigma_0^2+H_{xx},
        \qquad \widetilde C_S=C_{SS}+H_{SS}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-local-treatment-perturbation}
        B_S(\Gamma+H)
        =\frac{
        -a_S^\top\widetilde C_S^{-1}b_S
        +(H_{xr}/\widetilde\sigma_0^2)
        a_S^\top\widetilde C_S^{-1}a_S}
        {\widetilde\sigma_0^2
        -a_S^\top\widetilde C_S^{-1}a_S}.
\end{equation}
The leading term is
$-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S/\sigma_0^2$.
The relative inverse bound and \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} bound the
uniform difference, on $|H|_s\le c$, by a constant times
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)
        \left\{\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_r}+|H|_s\right\}
        \left\{|H|_s+\left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)^2\right\}
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)^2|H|_s.
\end{equation*}
Under \eqref{eq:local-treatment}, taking $H=G/\sqrt n$ and using
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-concentration} gives
\eqref{eq:gaussian-chaos-degeneration}.  Taking
$H=\hat\Gamma-\Gamma$ gives
\eqref{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration}.  For the final probability
statement, the sparse-moment and score tails at $t=m$ give
$|H|_s\le C\delta$ and
\begin{equation*}
\norm{Z_{1n}-\sqrt n d}_{C,s}\le C\sigma_0\sqrt m,
\qquad
\norm{Z_{2n}-\sqrt n h}_{C,s}\le C\sigma_r\sqrt m
\end{equation*}
with failure probability $Ce^{-cm}$.  The Gaussian moment and score tails
give the same bounds for $H=G/\sqrt n$ and $(Z_1,Z_2)$.  Substitution in
the deterministic bound above gives the displayed remainder scale.
\end{proof}

The sparse-treatment condition \eqref{eq:local-treatment} includes random
assignment, where
$d=0$.  The natural envelope of the searched displacement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-scale}
        a_n^{\mathrm{score}}
        =\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{equation}
The two summands bound the linear score process produced by $h$ and the
bilinear chaos.  The remainder in
Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration} is
$O_{\Prob}(\delta a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$, so no upper bound on the signal
energy is needed for an approximation at this envelope when
$\delta\to0$.

The full moment theorem can be used after this reduction, but replacing
the two score channels directly gives the sharper rate associated with
their smaller row norm.

For a score pair $z=(z_1,z_2)$, write
\begin{equation*}
        |z|_{C,s}
        =\frac{\norm{z_1}_{C,s}}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\norm{z_2}_{C,s}}{\sigma_r}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse score moments]\label{lem:sparse-score-moments}
Let
\begin{equation*}
        \chi_i=
        \begin{pmatrix}X^{(i)}x^{(i)}-d\\X^{(i)}r^{(i)}-h\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
and let $\chi_i^G$ be independent centered Gaussian vectors with the same
covariance.  Uniformly over deterministic hybrid patterns
$\widetilde\chi_i\in\{\chi_i,\chi_i^G\}$ and fixed $1\le q\le6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-hybrid-moments}
        \left\|
        \left|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde\chi_i
        \right|_{C,s}\right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim\sqrt m,
        \qquad
        \max_i\norm{|\widetilde\chi_i|_{C,s}}_{L_q}\lesssim\sqrt m.
\end{equation}
For $m+t\lesssim n$, the corresponding tail bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-hybrid-tail}
\Prob\left(
\left|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde\chi_i\right|_{C,s}
>C\left\{\sqrt{m+t}+\frac{m+t}{\sqrt n}\right\}
\right)
\le2e^{-ct}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed support and unit sparse direction, each score is a centered
product of standardized sub-Gaussian linear forms and has bounded
$\psi_1$ norm.  Nets over the sparse spheres and supports have logarithmic
size $O(m)$, so Bernstein's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:score-hybrid-tail}, and tail integration gives the first bound.
For one row, the largest sparse norm of $X^{(i)}$ has $L_{2q}$ norm
$O(\sqrt m)$, while $x^{(i)}$ and $r^{(i)}$ have bounded standardized
$L_{2q}$ norms.  Hölder's inequality gives the second bound; $q\le6$
requires moments no higher than twelve.
The Gaussian case follows from the same net calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Matched Gaussian score approximation]
\label{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} and
\eqref{eq:local-treatment} hold, and $m=o(n)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-stochastic-approximation}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})}
        {a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},
        \frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}
        {n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}}}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration} reduces the proof to a
rowwise replacement of the centered score sums
$(Z_{1n}-\sqrt n d,Z_{2n}-\sqrt n h)$ by Gaussian scores with the same
covariance.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        D_n=n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}}
        =\sigma_0\{\sqrt{nm}\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_rm\}.
\end{equation*}
Let
\begin{equation*}
 W_i=(W_{1i},W_{2i})
 =\frac1{\sqrt n}\left(\sum_{k<i}\chi_k^G+\sum_{k>i}\chi_k\right)
\end{equation*}
be the centered leave-one-row hybrid score sum.  It is independent of the
inserted row $\widetilde\chi=(\widetilde\chi_1,\widetilde\chi_2)$.  Write
\begin{equation*}
        z_i=(\sqrt n\,d+W_{1i},\sqrt n\,h+W_{2i}).
\end{equation*}
The normalized support value changes exactly by
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
 \ell^{(1)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)
 +\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)
 ={}&-\frac{\widetilde\chi_{1S}^\top C_{SS}^{-1}z_{2i,S}
        +z_{1i,S}^\top C_{SS}^{-1}\widetilde\chi_{2S}}
        {\sqrt n\,D_n}\\
 &-\frac{\widetilde\chi_{1S}^\top C_{SS}^{-1}
        \widetilde\chi_{2S}}{nD_n}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
For every bounded collection of supports and every fixed moment order,
relative sub-Gaussianity and H\"older's inequality
give
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{\ell^{(1)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)}
        _{L_q(\widetilde\chi\mid W_i)}
 \le \frac{C\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}}{\sqrt{mn}},
 \qquad
 \norm{\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)}
        _{L_q(\widetilde\chi)}\le\frac{Cs}{nm},
 \qquad
 \left\|\max_{|S|=s}|\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)|\right\|
        _{L_q(\widetilde\chi)}
 \le\frac Cn,
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
 \Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}
 =1+\frac{\norm{W_{1i}}_{C,s}}{\sigma_0\sqrt m}
       +\frac{\norm{W_{2i}}_{C,s}}{\sigma_r\sqrt m}.
\end{equation*}
For deterministic $a,b\in\R^p$, the directional estimate behind these
bounds is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-score-direction}
 \norm{a^\top\widetilde\chi_1+b^\top\widetilde\chi_2}_{\psi_1}
 \le C\left\{\sigma_0(a^\top Ca)^{1/2}
 +\sigma_r(b^\top Cb)^{1/2}\right\}.
\end{equation}
This follows by writing each term as a centered product of two linear
forms in the augmented sub-Gaussian row.  The matched Gaussian score has
the same bound because it has the same covariance.
Indeed, \eqref{eq:local-treatment} gives
$\sqrt n\norm{d}_{C,s}\lesssim\sigma_0\sqrt m$, while
\begin{equation*}
 \norm{z_{2i}}_{C,s}\le
 \sqrt n\,\widetilde\Psi_s
 +\sigma_r\sqrt m\,\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}},
 \qquad
 D_n=\sigma_0\sqrt m
 \{\sqrt n\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_r\sqrt m\}.
\end{equation*}
These relations give the stated linear-increment bound even when the
outcome score is unrestricted.  Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-score-moments}
gives bounded fixed moments of $\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}$.

Let $\mathbf Q_i=(Q_{i,S}:|S|=s)$ be the vector of normalized base
values, where
\begin{equation*}
        Q_{i,S}=\frac{Q_S(z_{1i},z_{2i})}{D_n},
\end{equation*}
and, for a test function $f$ from the definition of $d_3$, define
\begin{equation*}
 \Phi_{\lambda,f}((t_S))=f\left\{
 \frac1{\lambda m}\log\sum_{|S|=s}\exp(\lambda m t_S)
 \right\}.
\end{equation*}
Its first three
derivative tensors have total variation bounded by
\begin{equation*}
        C,\qquad C(1+\lambda m),\qquad
        C\{1+\lambda m+(\lambda m)^2\},
\end{equation*}
respectively.  Let $\pi_S$ be the Gibbs weights before inserting the
row.  At an intermediate point of the linear increment they are
\begin{equation*}
 w_S(t)=\frac{\pi_S\exp\{t\lambda m
        \ell^{(1)}_{i,S}(\widetilde\chi)\}}
 {\sum_T\pi_T\exp\{t\lambda m
        \ell^{(1)}_{i,T}(\widetilde\chi)\}},
 \qquad \overline\ell_i=\sum_T\pi_T\ell^{(1)}_{i,T}.
\end{equation*}
Here $0\le t\le1$.  Jensen's inequality gives, for $k\le3$,
\begin{equation*}
 \prod_{a=1}^kw_{S_a}(t)
 \le\prod_{a=1}^k\pi_{S_a}
 \exp\left\{t\lambda m
 \left(\sum_{a=1}^k\ell^{(1)}_{i,S_a}
 -k\overline\ell_i\right)\right\}.
\end{equation*}
The linear form in the exponent is centered in the inserted score row.
Equation~\eqref{eq:fixed-score-direction}, the triangle inequality for
the $C$-seminorm, and convexity over the weights $\pi_T$ bound its
conditional $\psi_1$ norm by
$C\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}/\sqrt{mn}$.  On
\begin{equation*}
        \Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}\le c(n/m)^{1/3},
\end{equation*}
the tilt has bounded exponential moment because
\begin{equation*}
        \lambda m\frac{\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}}{\sqrt{mn}}\le c
        \qquad\text{for}\qquad
        \lambda=(n/m)^{1/6}.
\end{equation*}
The tail bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-score-moments} gives
\begin{equation*}
 \max_{i\le n}\Prob\{\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}>c(n/m)^{1/3}\}
 \le 2\exp(-c'n^{2/3}m^{1/3}),
\end{equation*}
so the sum over the $n$ hybrids of the complementary probabilities is
$o\{(m/n)^{1/6}\}$.  The same
tilt bound, followed by H\"older's inequality, applies when one of the
factors is the quadratic increment $\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}$.

Writing $\ell_i^{(j)}=(\ell_{i,S}^{(j)}:|S|=s)$, expand the value in
$\ell_i^{(2)}$ around $\mathbf Q_i+\ell_i^{(1)}$, expand the value at
$\mathbf Q_i+\ell_i^{(1)}$ to second order in $\ell_i^{(1)}$, and expand
its derivative on $\ell_i^{(2)}$ to first order in $\ell_i^{(1)}$.  This
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-stage-score-expansion}
\begin{split}
 \Phi_{\lambda,f}(\mathbf Q_i+\ell_i^{(1)}+\ell_i^{(2)})
 ={}&\Phi_{\lambda,f}(\mathbf Q_i)
 +D\Phi_{\lambda,f}(\mathbf Q_i)[\ell_i^{(1)}+\ell_i^{(2)}]
 +\frac12D^2\Phi_{\lambda,f}(\mathbf Q_i)
        [\ell_i^{(1)},\ell_i^{(1)}]\\
 &+D^2\Phi_{\lambda,f}(\mathbf Q_i)
        [\ell_i^{(1)},\ell_i^{(2)}]
 +\mathcal R_i.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
On $\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}}\le c(n/m)^{1/3}$, the preceding Gibbs
bound and the increment
moments give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-stage-score-remainder}
\begin{split}
 \E(|\mathcal R_i|\mid W_i)
 \le C\{1+\lambda m+(\lambda m)^2\}
        \left\{\frac{(\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}})^3}{(mn)^{3/2}}
 +\frac{(\Lambda_i^{\mathrm{score}})^2s}{m^2n^2}\right\}
 +\frac{C(1+\lambda m)}{n^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The quadratic-increment expansion has deterministic remainder
$C(1+\lambda m)\max_S|\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}|^2$.  The quadratic increment
therefore never enters an exponential tilt; the two terms inside braces
in \eqref{eq:two-stage-score-remainder} come from three linear
increments and from two linear increments with one quadratic increment.
The conditional expectations of $\ell^{(1)}_{i,S}$ and
$\ell^{(2)}_{i,S}$, and the
conditional products
$\ell^{(1)}_{i,S}\ell^{(1)}_{i,T}$, agree for the original and Gaussian
rows because their first two score moments agree.  The uncancelled terms,
summed over the replacement path, are therefore bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:refined-score-replacement-error}
 C\left\{
 \frac{\lambda s}{\sqrt{nm}}
 +\lambda^2\sqrt{\frac mn}
 +\frac{1+\lambda m}{n}
 \right\}.
\end{equation}
The linear--quadratic product contributes the first term, three linear
increments contribute the second, and the uniform $L_2$ bound on the
quadratic increment gives the last.  The mixed higher-order term is at
most $C\lambda^2s/n$ and is absorbed by the second term when
$s\le m\le n$.  Since $s\le m\le n$, the choice
$\lambda=(n/m)^{1/6}$ makes every term in
\eqref{eq:refined-score-replacement-error} at most a constant times
$(m/n)^{1/6}$.  The soft-maximum error is $1/\lambda$ and has the same
order.

Writing $\mathcal E_n$ for the degeneration remainder, its deterministic
bound and the $L_3$ sparse-moment bound give
\begin{equation*}
        \E\min\left\{\frac{|\mathcal E_n|}{a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},1\right\}
        \lesssim\delta,
\end{equation*}
which supplies the final term in the smooth distance.
\end{proof}

The score tail \eqref{eq:score-hybrid-tail} and relative-RIP tail
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} also give the following direct result.  If
$\delta\le\delta_0$ for a
sufficiently small constant, then, with probability at least
$1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \le C\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        \right).
\end{equation}
The bound follows from
$T_C(a,b)\le\norm{a}_{C,s}\norm{b}_{C,s}$.  Under random assignment,
$\widetilde\Psi_s=\Psi_s$ and
$\sigma_r^2=\beta^\top C\beta+\sigma_\varepsilon^2$, so the right side is
equivalent to the standard experimental coordinate-search scale
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\delta}{\sigma_0}
        \sqrt{\Psi_s^2+\sigma_r^2\delta^2}.
\end{equation*}
A matching scalar lower bound cannot depend on $\widetilde\Psi_s$ alone:
the linear maximum also depends on how many separated supports carry that
energy.  The Gaussian field retains this multiplicity.

\section{Forward product search}

\subsection{Conditional products and projection certificate}

For the matched score field, the marginal has a product form.  A vector $v$ is
score-compatible when $v_J\in\operatorname{range}(C_{JJ})$ for every
$|J|\le2s$; all population, empirical, and matched score vectors have
this property.  If $j\notin S$ and
$C_{jj}-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}C_{Sj}>0$, define its standardized residual score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-score}
        v_{j\mid S}
        =\frac{v_j-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}v_S}
        {\{C_{jj}-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}C_{Sj}\}^{1/2}},
\end{equation}
and put $v_{j\mid S}=0$ when the residual variance is zero.
Compatibility makes the residual numerator vanish in the latter case.
Block inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-product}
        Q_{S\cup\{j\}}(a,b)-Q_S(a,b)
        =-a_{j\mid S}b_{j\mid S}.
\end{equation}
When the residual variance is zero, both sides of
\eqref{eq:conditional-product} vanish.
The literal extension of top-$s$ sorting starts at $J_0=\emptyset$ and
selects the largest current product $-a_{j\mid J_{t-1}}b_{j\mid
J_{t-1}}$ at each step.  It is exact when $C$ is diagonal, but under
correlation an early choice changes every later product.  The
certifiable construction below instead selects a polarized projection
gain and bounds the regret of its resulting product path.

For disjoint $S,R\subseteq[p]$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-control-block}
        C_{R\mid S}
        =C_{RR}-C_{RS}C_{SS}^{-1}C_{SR}.
\end{equation}
The scale-invariant projection ratio is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-ratio}
        \mu_s(C)
        =\inf_{S,R,u}
        \frac{u^\top\operatorname{diag}(C_{R\mid S})^{-1}u}
        {u^\top C_{R\mid S}^{-1}u},
\end{equation}
where the infimum is over disjoint sets with $|S|\le s$,
$1\le|R|\le s$, and nonzero
$u\in\operatorname{range}(C_{R\mid S})$; pairs with
$C_{R\mid S}=0$ are omitted, and an empty infimum is one.  Equivalently,
\eqref{eq:residual-ratio} is the infimum of the least positive
eigenvalues of the conditional correlation matrices obtained from
\eqref{eq:conditional-control-block} after zero-variance coordinates are
removed.  This definition allows singular control blocks.

This parameter is the largest covariance-only constant in the
joint-to-coordinate projection inequality used below.  Indeed, for every
admissible $u$, the compatible score
$v=C_{\cdot R}C_{R\mid S}^{-1}u$ has conditional residual $u$.  Thus every
direction in the infimum is attained.  The
parameter also connects directly to sparse restricted conditioning.
When
every $C_{JJ}$ with $|J|\le2s$ is nonsingular, put
\begin{equation*}
        \kappa_s(C)=\sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \frac{\lambda_{\max}(C_{JJ})}{\lambda_{\min}(C_{JJ})}.
\end{equation*}
Because the controls have unit variance, this is the usual sparse
restricted condition number.  For any admissible $S,R$,
$C_{R\mid S}^{-1}$ is a principal block of
$C_{S\cup R,S\cup R}^{-1}$.  Hence the eigenvalues of $C_{R\mid S}$ lie
between those of its parent block.  Its diagonal entries are bounded by
the same upper eigenvalue, so diagonal normalization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-ratio-conditioning}
        \mu_s(C)\ge\kappa_s(C)^{-1}.
\end{equation}

Fix a supplied lower bound $0<\mu\le\mu_s(C)$.  For positive
$\sigma_a,\sigma_b$, define the active and passive contrasts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-polarization}
        z_{\mathrm a}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
        \left(\frac a{\sigma_a}-\frac b{\sigma_b}\right),
        \qquad
        z_{\mathrm p}=\frac1{\sqrt2}
        \left(\frac a{\sigma_a}+\frac b{\sigma_b}\right).
\end{equation}
Starting from $J_0=\emptyset$, forward projection chooses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-projection-path}
        j_t\in\mathop{\arg\max}_{j\notin J_{t-1}}
        (z_{\mathrm a})_{j\mid J_{t-1}}^2,
        \qquad
        J_t=J_{t-1}\cup\{j_t\},
        \qquad t=1,\ldots,s.
\end{equation}
For a finite family, braces with subscript $(\ell)$ denote its
$\ell$th largest member.  Along this path, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-certificate}
\begin{split}
        \mathcal U_{C,\mu}(v;J_\bullet)
        =\min_{0\le t\le s}\bigg\{
        v_{J_t}^\top C_{J_tJ_t}^{-1}v_{J_t}
        +\frac1\mu
        \sum_{\ell=1}^{s\wedge(p-t)}
        \{v_{j\mid J_t}^2:j\notin J_t\}_{(\ell)}
        \bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Write the corresponding product-gap certificate as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-gap-certificate}
\begin{split}
        \mathcal D_{C,\mu}(z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p};J_\bullet)
        ={}&\mathcal U_{C,\mu}(z_{\mathrm a};J_\bullet)
        -(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}\\
        &+(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Forward product-search guarantee]
\label{thm:projection-products}
Let $a,b$ be score-compatible, form $z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p}$ by
\eqref{eq:score-polarization}, and let $J_\bullet$ follow
\eqref{eq:forward-projection-path}.  For every score-compatible $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-upper-bound}
        \mathcal U_{C,\mu}(v;J_\bullet)\ge\norm{v}_{C,s}^2.
\end{equation}
The active gap obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-projection-gap}
\begin{split}
        0&\le\norm{z_{\mathrm a}}_{C,s}^2
        -(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_t}^\top C_{J_tJ_t}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_t}\\
        &\le\left(1-\frac\mu s\right)^t\norm{z_{\mathrm a}}_{C,s}^2,
        \qquad 0\le t\le s.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The returned conditional-product sum satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:forward-product-certificate}
\begin{split}
        0&\le T_C(a,b)-Q_{J_s}(a,b)\\
        &\le\frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b}{2}
        \mathcal D_{C,\mu}(z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p};J_\bullet).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Incremental factorization computes the path and the certificates in
$O(ps^2)$ arithmetic operations and $O(ps)$ working memory beyond access
to $C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For disjoint $S,R$, write
\begin{equation*}
        u=v_R-C_{RS}C_{SS}^{-1}v_S.
\end{equation*}
Block inversion on the compressed range and
\eqref{eq:residual-ratio} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:joint-projection-gain}
\begin{split}
        v_{S\cup R}^\top C_{S\cup R,S\cup R}^{-1}v_{S\cup R}
        -v_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}v_S
        &=u^\top C_{R\mid S}^{-1}u,\\
        \sum_{j\in R}v_{j\mid S}^2
        &=u^\top\operatorname{diag}(C_{R\mid S})^{-1}u\\
        &\ge\mu u^\top C_{R\mid S}^{-1}u.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Let $A$ maximize $v_A^\top C_{AA}^{-1}v_A$ over $|A|=s$ and take
$R=A\setminus J_t$.  Projection energy is monotone, so
\eqref{eq:joint-projection-gain} yields
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        \norm{v}_{C,s}^2
        -v_{J_t}^\top C_{J_tJ_t}^{-1}v_{J_t}
        \le\frac1\mu
        \sum_{\ell=1}^{s\wedge(p-t)}
        \{v_{j\mid J_t}^2:j\notin J_t\}_{(\ell)}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
This proves \eqref{eq:projection-upper-bound}.  With $v=z_{\mathrm a}$, the largest
residual gain is at least $\mu/s$ times the current active gap, and
iteration proves \eqref{eq:forward-projection-gap}.

Residualization commutes with \eqref{eq:score-polarization}, and
polarization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-product-polarization}
\begin{split}
        -a_{j\mid S}b_{j\mid S}
        &=\frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b}{2}
        \{(z_{\mathrm a})_{j\mid S}^2-(z_{\mathrm p})_{j\mid S}^2\},\\
        Q_S(a,b)
        &=\frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b}{2}\left\{
        (z_{\mathrm a})_{S}^\top C_{SS}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm a})_{S}
        -(z_{\mathrm p})_{S}^\top C_{SS}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm p})_{S}\right\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        &\{T_C(a,b)-Q_{J_s}(a,b)\}
        +\left\{\frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b}{2}
        \norm{z_{\mathrm a}}_{C,s}^2-T_C(a,b)\right\}\\
        &\qquad=\frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b}{2}\left\{
        \norm{z_{\mathrm a}}_{C,s}^2
        -(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}
        +(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}\right\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Both terms on the left are nonnegative.  Dropping the second and applying
\eqref{eq:projection-upper-bound} proves
\eqref{eq:forward-product-certificate}.
\end{proof}

The path does not require $\mu$; the supplied lower bound makes its
realized regret certificate valid.  The best sparse value of $\mu$ can
be combinatorial, but a structural or conservative spectral bound is
enough.  Relative restricted isometry transfers such a population bound
to the empirical Gram matrix.

\begin{lemma}[Relative RIP transfer]
\label{lem:projection-rip-transfer}
Suppose positive semidefinite $C$ and $\widetilde C$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-rip-loewner}
        (1-\epsilon)C_{JJ}
        \preceq\widetilde C_{JJ}
        \preceq(1+\epsilon)C_{JJ},
        \qquad |J|\le2s,
\end{equation}
for some $0\le\epsilon<1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection-rip-transfer}
        \mu_s(\widetilde C)
        \ge\frac{1-\epsilon}{1+\epsilon}\mu_s(C).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Schur complement has the variational representation
\begin{equation*}
        u^\top C_{R\mid S}u
        =\inf_v
        \begin{pmatrix}v\\u\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}
        C_{S\cup R,S\cup R}
        \begin{pmatrix}v\\u\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation*}
Thus \eqref{eq:relative-rip-loewner} gives
\begin{equation*}
        (1-\epsilon)C_{R\mid S}
        \preceq\widetilde C_{R\mid S}
        \preceq(1+\epsilon)C_{R\mid S}.
\end{equation*}
The two conditional matrices have the same range and zero diagonal
coordinates.  On their common range, their inverse quadratic forms obey
the reversed inequalities, while the diagonal inverse forms obey the
corresponding coordinatewise inequalities.  Hence every ratio in
\eqref{eq:residual-ratio} for $\widetilde C$ is at least
$(1-\epsilon)/(1+\epsilon)$ times its counterpart for $C$.  Taking the
infimum proves \eqref{eq:projection-rip-transfer}.
\end{proof}

In particular, \eqref{eq:relative-rip} and
\eqref{eq:projection-ratio-conditioning} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-projection-ratio}
        \mu_s(\hat C)
        \ge\{1-O_{\Prob}(\delta)\}\mu_s(C)
        \ge\frac{1-O_{\Prob}(\delta)}{\kappa_s(C)}
\end{equation}
when the sparse population blocks are nonsingular.  Thus
$\kappa_s(C)$ is a familiar sufficient condition, whereas $\mu_s(C)$
is the weaker condition tailored to forward projection and remains
meaningful with exact dependencies.  Under the unit-variance
normalization, define the local-isotropy coefficient by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-isotropy}
        \rho_s(C)=\sup_{|J|\le2s}\norm{C_{JJ}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
It compares $C$ with $I$ and is needed only to reduce conditional
products to a static list.

The same construction gives a finite-sample certificate for the
regression search itself.  When $x^\top x>0$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-forward-scores}
\begin{gathered}
        \hat r=y-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}x,
        \qquad
        \hat Z_{2n}=\frac{X^\top\hat r}{\sqrt n},\\
        \hat\sigma_0^2=\frac{x^\top x}{n},
        \qquad
        \hat\sigma_r^2=\frac{\hat r^\top\hat r}{n}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-products} with $C=\hat C$,
$a=Z_{1n}$, $b=\hat Z_{2n}$, any positive scales
$\sigma_a,\sigma_b$, and a supplied
$0<\mu\le\mu_s(\hat C)$.  Denote the resulting path by
$J_\bullet$, form $\hat z_{\mathrm a},\hat z_{\mathrm p}$ as in
\eqref{eq:score-polarization}, and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-forward-certificate}
\begin{aligned}
        \hat{\mathcal D}_n
        =\mathcal D_{\hat C,\mu}
        (\hat z_{\mathrm a},\hat z_{\mathrm p};J_\bullet),\\
        \mathcal U_{1n}
        &=\mathcal U_{\hat C,\mu}(Z_{1n};J_\bullet),
        \qquad
        \mathcal U_{2n}
        =\mathcal U_{\hat C,\mu}(\hat Z_{2n};J_\bullet).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
On $\hat\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r>0$, the sample standard deviations
in \eqref{eq:observable-forward-scores} give a canonical choice of the
two polarization scales.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample regression certificate]
\label{thm:observable-forward-search}
If $\mathcal U_{1n}<n\hat\sigma_0^2$, then every searched denominator
is positive and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-forward-regret}
\begin{split}
        0&\le
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat\beta_{0,S}
        -\hat\beta_{0,J_s}\\
        &\le
        \frac{\sigma_a\sigma_b\hat{\mathcal D}_n}
        {2n\hat\sigma_0^2}
        +\frac{2\mathcal U_{1n}
        \sqrt{\mathcal U_{1n}\mathcal U_{2n}}}
        {n\hat\sigma_0^2
        (n\hat\sigma_0^2-\mathcal U_{1n})}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Every quantity on the right is computed from $(x,y,X)$, the chosen
scales, and the supplied design bound $\mu$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The score vectors $Z_{1n}$ and $\hat Z_{2n}$ are compatible with
$\hat C$.  Since $x^\top\hat r=0$, partitioned
regression gives, for every support,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-observable-ratio}
        \hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}
        =
        \frac{-Z_{1n,S}^\top\hat C_{SS}^{-1}
        \hat Z_{2n,S}}
        {n\hat\sigma_0^2
        -Z_{1n,S}^\top\hat C_{SS}^{-1}Z_{1n,S}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:projection-upper-bound} bounds the two score energies
by $\mathcal U_{1n}$ and $\mathcal U_{2n}$.  Cauchy--Schwarz on the
compressed range therefore bounds the absolute numerator in
\eqref{eq:exact-observable-ratio} by
$\sqrt{\mathcal U_{1n}\mathcal U_{2n}}$.  Replacing its denominator by
$n\hat\sigma_0^2$ changes every support value by at most
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\mathcal U_{1n}
        \sqrt{\mathcal U_{1n}\mathcal U_{2n}}}
        {n\hat\sigma_0^2
        (n\hat\sigma_0^2-\mathcal U_{1n})}.
\end{equation*}
Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-products} bounds the common-denominator
regret of $J_s$ by
$\sigma_a\sigma_b\hat{\mathcal D}_n/
(2n\hat\sigma_0^2)$.  Applying the denominator bound once to the
maximizing support and once to $J_s$ proves
\eqref{eq:observable-forward-regret}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:observable-forward-search} is deterministic and retains
the support-dependent denominator, so it also applies when the outcome
signal is nonnegligible and in observational designs.  For the matched
score field, form $z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p}$
from $(Z_1,Z_2)$ with
$(\sigma_a,\sigma_b)=(\sigma_0,\sigma_r)$.

\begin{corollary}[Stochastic forward-product approximation]
\label{cor:covariance-aware-stochastic-products}
Suppose the assumptions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} hold and
$0<\mu\le\mu_s(C)$.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-forward-smallness}
        \E\min\left\{
        \frac{\sigma_r
        \mathcal D_{C,\mu}(z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p};J_\bullet)}
        {n\sigma_0a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},1\right\}=o(1),
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:covariance-aware-stochastic-products}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        &\frac{\max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})}
        {a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},
        -\frac1{n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}}}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        (Z_1)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}(Z_2)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}
        \bigg)\\
        &\lesssim
        \left(\frac mn\right)^{1/6}+\delta+o(1).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-products} bounds the matched product error by
$\sigma_0\sigma_r\mathcal D_{C,\mu}(z_{\mathrm a},z_{\mathrm p};J_\bullet)/2$.  Condition
\eqref{eq:matched-forward-smallness} makes its normalized truncated first
moment vanish.  The triangle inequality for $d_3$ and
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} prove the claim.
\end{proof}

Condition \eqref{eq:matched-forward-smallness} controls the forward gap
under the full covariance-matched Gaussian score law.  The following
Kronecker covariance gives a useful sufficient condition for its passive
term.  If, for $-1<\gamma<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kronecker-score-law}
        \begin{pmatrix}Z_1/\sigma_0\\Z_2/\sigma_r\end{pmatrix}
        -\E\begin{pmatrix}Z_1/\sigma_0\\Z_2/\sigma_r\end{pmatrix}
        \sim N\!\left(
        0,
        \begin{pmatrix}1&\gamma\\\gamma&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes C
        \right),
\end{equation}
then $z_{\mathrm a}$ and $z_{\mathrm p}$ are independent with covariances
$(1-\gamma)C$ and $(1+\gamma)C$.  Along every $z_{\mathrm a}$-measurable path,
conditionally on $z_{\mathrm a}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-passive-law}
        \frac{(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}}
        {1+\gamma}
        \sim\chi^2_{\operatorname{rank}(C_{J_sJ_s})}
        \left(
        \frac{(\E z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}
        (\E z_{\mathrm p})_{J_s}}{1+\gamma}
        \right).
\end{equation}
If $\E Z_1=\E Z_2=0$, the passive energy is $O_{\Prob}(s)$ for every
covariance.

The forward path is first-order accurate for a concrete class that need
not approach local isotropy.

\begin{corollary}[Forward search under block-diagonal covariance]
\label{cor:block-anisotropic-products}
Suppose \eqref{eq:kronecker-score-law} holds with
$\E Z_1=\E Z_2=0$ and, after a permutation, $C$ is block diagonal with
$q$ positive-rank blocks.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-anisotropic-regime}
        s=o(q),
        \qquad
        \log(p/q)=o(L),
\end{equation}
then the forward path in \eqref{eq:forward-projection-path}, which does
not require $\mu$, satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-anisotropic-gap}
        \norm{z_{\mathrm a}}_{C,s}^2
        -(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}^\top C_{J_sJ_s}^{-1}(z_{\mathrm a})_{J_s}
        =o_{\Prob}(m)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-anisotropic-product}
\begin{split}
        T_C(Z_1,Z_2)
        &=-\sum_{t=1}^s
        (Z_1)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}(Z_2)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}
        +o_{\Prob}(\sigma_0\sigma_rm)\\
        &=\sigma_0\sigma_r(1-\gamma)m
        +o_{\Prob}(\sigma_0\sigma_rm).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
No conditioning or full-rank assumption is imposed within the blocks.
If Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} also holds and $m=o(n)$, the
empirical maximum and the matched scores admit a coupling on which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-anisotropic-regression}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        ={}&-\frac1{n\sigma_0^2}\sum_{t=1}^s
        (Z_1)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}(Z_2)_{j_t\mid J_{t-1}}\\
        &+o_{\Prob}\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\frac mn\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Put $\widetilde z=z_{\mathrm a}/(1-\gamma)^{1/2}$, so
$\widetilde z\sim N(0,C)$.  For every fixed size-$s$ support,
$\widetilde z_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}\widetilde z_S$ is chi-square with at
most $s$ degrees of freedom.  A chi-square tail bound and a union over the
at most $\binom ps$ supports give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-active-upper}
        \norm{\widetilde z}_{C,s}^2
        \le\{2+o_{\Prob}(1)\}m.
\end{equation}
Choose one coordinate from each block.  Their standardized scores are
independent standard Gaussians, and Gaussian tail and binomial
concentration bounds give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-active-lower}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        \left\{\frac{\widetilde z_j^2}{C_{jj}}:
        j\text{ is a block representative}\right\}_{(t)}
        \ge\{2-o_{\Prob}(1)\}s\log(eq/s)
        =\{2-o_{\Prob}(1)\}m.
\end{equation}
At the $t$th forward step, at most $t-1$ blocks have been touched.  One
of the blocks carrying the $t$ largest representative squares remains
untouched, so its residual gain is unchanged.  The $t$th forward gain is
therefore at least the $t$th largest representative square.  Telescoping
\eqref{eq:block-active-lower} and comparing with
\eqref{eq:block-active-upper} proves
\eqref{eq:block-anisotropic-gap}.

Equation~\eqref{eq:conditional-passive-law} makes the passive energy
$O_{\Prob}(s)=o_{\Prob}(m)$.  The exact polarized error budget in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:projection-products} proves
\eqref{eq:block-anisotropic-product}.  Centering in
\eqref{eq:kronecker-score-law} gives $d=h=0$, so
$a_n^{\mathrm{score}}=(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)m/n$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} and Strassen's coupling
theorem then give \eqref{eq:block-anisotropic-regression}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Separable top-s reduction}

When $C=I$, the products in \eqref{eq:conditional-product} no longer
depend on $S$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-s-products}
        T_I(a,b)
        =\sum_{t=1}^s\{-a_jb_j\}_{(t)},
\end{equation}
where braces denote decreasing order statistics.  The departure from
this static product list is measured by $\rho_s(C)$ in
\eqref{eq:local-isotropy}.
\begin{theorem}[Local-isotropy reduction]\label{thm:static-products}
If $\rho_s(C)<1$, then, for every $a,b\in\R^p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-product-bound}
        |T_C(a,b)-T_I(a,b)|
        \le\rho_s(C)\norm{a}_{C,s}\norm{b}_{C,s}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $S$, put
$\widetilde a=C_{SS}^{-1/2}a_S$ and
$\widetilde b=C_{SS}^{-1/2}b_S$.  Then
\begin{equation*}
        Q_S(a,b)+a_S^\top b_S
        =\widetilde a^\top(C_{SS}-I)\widetilde b,
\end{equation*}
whose absolute value is at most the right side of
\eqref{eq:static-product-bound}.  Taking the uniform bound over $S$ and
using $|\max_S x_S-\max_S y_S|\le\max_S|x_S-y_S|$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Empirical top-$s$ approximation]
\label{thm:empirical-top-s}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments},
\eqref{eq:local-treatment}, $\delta\le\delta_0$, and
$\rho_s(C)<1$ hold.  Use the empirical scores in
\eqref{eq:empirical-scores} and the observable quantities in
\eqref{eq:observable-forward-scores}.
Then, with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-top-s}
\begin{split}
\bigg|\max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        -\frac1{n\hat\sigma_0^2}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        \{-Z_{1n,j}\hat Z_{2n,j}:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}\bigg|
        \le C\{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Consequently the approximation error is
$o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$ whenever
$m=o(n)$ and $\rho_s(C)=o(1)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The empirical part of Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration} changes the
searched coefficient by at most $C\delta a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$ on the
sparse score event.  On the same event,
\begin{align*}
        \norm{Z_{1n}}_{C,s}&\le C\sigma_0\sqrt m,\\
        \norm{Z_{2n}}_{C,s}&\le
        C\{\sqrt n\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_r\sqrt m\}.
\end{align*}
The first bound uses
$\sqrt n\norm{d}_{C,s}\lesssim\sigma_0\sqrt m$, which follows from
\eqref{eq:local-treatment}; the second follows from
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-score-moments}.  Theorem~\ref{thm:static-products}
therefore bounds the difference between $T_C(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})$ and
$T_I(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})$ by
$Cn\sigma_0^2\rho_s(C)a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:top-s-products} identifies the latter with the
top-$s$ sum formed with $Z_{2n}$.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
        \hat Z_{2n}
        =Z_{2n}-(\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}-\beta_\emptyset^*)Z_{1n}.
\end{equation*}
On the same event,
$|\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset}-\beta_\emptyset^*|\le
C(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta$ and
$\max_{|S|=s}\norm{Z_{1n,S}}^2
\le C\{1+\rho_s(C)\}\sigma_0^2m$.  The deterministic inequality
$|\max_S a_S-\max_S b_S|\le\max_S|a_S-b_S|$ therefore bounds the
normalized change in the top-$s$ sum by
$C\{1+\rho_s(C)\}(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^3$, which is bounded by
$C\{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$.
The same event has
$|\hat\sigma_0^2/\sigma_0^2-1|\le C\delta$.  Cauchy--Schwarz and
the two sparse score bounds show that the top-$s$ sum divided by
$n\sigma_0^2$ is at most
$C\{1+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}}$ in absolute value.  Replacing
$\sigma_0^2$ by $\hat\sigma_0^2$ therefore stays within the stated
error.  The fixed-$L_q$ versions of the same sparse-moment and score
bounds give, without requiring $m\to\infty$,
\begin{equation*}
        O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}}\right)
\end{equation*}
for the total approximation error.  This proves the final assertion.
\end{proof}

The coordinatewise conditions used by a separable approximation imply
the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:empirical-top-s} and identify its
scale.  The following corollary records this implication explicitly.

\begin{corollary}[Observable separable approximation]
\label{cor:observable-separable}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds,
$m=o(n)$, $\rho_s(C)=o(1)$, and, for a fixed constant $K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coordinate-score-cap}
        \max_{j\le p}\frac{\sqrt n\,|d_j|}{\sigma_0\sqrt L}
        +\max_{j\le p}\frac{\sqrt n\,|h_j|}{\sigma_r\sqrt L}
        \le K.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observable-separable}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        ={}&\frac1{n\hat\sigma_0^2}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        \{-Z_{1n,j}\hat Z_{2n,j}:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}\\
        &+o_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\frac{m}{n}\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For $|J|\le2s$, local isotropy and
\eqref{eq:coordinate-score-cap} give
\begin{equation*}
        d_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}d_J
        \lesssim\sigma_0^2\frac{|J|L}{n},
        \qquad
        h_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}h_J
        \lesssim\sigma_r^2\frac{|J|L}{n}.
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
$\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta$,
$\widetilde\Psi_s\lesssim\sigma_r\delta$, and
\begin{equation*}
        a_n^{\mathrm{score}}\asymp
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{equation*}
Theorem~\ref{thm:empirical-top-s} now has error
$O_{\Prob}(\{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$, which is
$o_{\Prob}\{(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)m/n\}$ under the stated regime.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Top-$s$ stochastic approximation]
\label{cor:top-s-stochastic}
Under the assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}, if
$\rho_s(C)=o(1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-s-stochastic}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        &\frac{\max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})}
        {a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},
        \frac1{n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}}}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\{-Z_{1j}Z_{2j}\}_{(t)}
        \bigg)=o(1).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the two variables admit a coupling under which their
unnormalized difference is $o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Gaussian sparse-score bounds give
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{Z_1}_{C,s}=O_{\Prob}(\sigma_0\sqrt m),
        \qquad
        \norm{Z_2}_{C,s}
        =O_{\Prob}(\sqrt n\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_r\sqrt m).
\end{equation*}
Theorem~\ref{thm:static-products} therefore replaces $T_C(Z_1,Z_2)$ by
$T_I(Z_1,Z_2)$ with error $o_{L_1}(n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} and
\eqref{eq:top-s-products} prove the smooth-distance statement.  The
normalized variables are tight by the same sparse-score bounds, so
Prokhorov--Strassen coupling gives the scalar assertion.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Independent-coordinate comparison}

The products in \eqref{eq:top-s-stochastic} need not be independent.
Their dependence is governed by the covariance of the two score
channels, which can be separated from the covariance $C$ of the controls.
Define the standardized centered row score and its covariance by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-covariance}
        \chi^\circ=
        \begin{pmatrix}
        (Xx-d)/\sigma_0\\
        (Xr-h)/\sigma_r
        \end{pmatrix},
        \qquad \Omega=\Cov(\chi^\circ),
\end{equation}
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-isotropy}
        \rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}
        =\sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \norm{\Omega_{(1,2)J,(1,2)J}-I_{2|J|}}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}
Here $(1,2)J$ includes both score coordinates associated with the
controls in $J$.

For centered variables, write
\begin{equation*}
        \kappa_4(a,b,c,d)
        =\E[abcd]-\E[ab]\E[cd]-\E[ac]\E[bd]-\E[ad]\E[bc].
\end{equation*}
Define the standardized cumulant matrices by
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        K^{xx}_{jk}&=\frac{\kappa_4(X_j,x,X_k,x)}{\sigma_0^2},
        \qquad
        K^{rr}_{jk}=\frac{\kappa_4(X_j,r,X_k,r)}{\sigma_r^2},\\
        K^{xr}_{jk}&=\frac{\kappa_4(X_j,x,X_k,r)}
        {\sigma_0\sigma_r}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The score covariance follows from
\begin{align*}
\Cov(X_jx-d_j,X_kx-d_k)
&=\sigma_0^2C_{jk}+d_jd_k+\sigma_0^2K^{xx}_{jk},\\
\Cov(X_jr-h_j,X_kr-h_k)
&=\sigma_r^2C_{jk}+h_jh_k+\sigma_r^2K^{rr}_{jk},\\
\Cov(X_jx-d_j,X_kr-h_k)
&=h_jd_k+\sigma_0\sigma_rK^{xr}_{jk}.
\end{align*}
The sparse mean cap used below is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-mean-cap}
        \sup_{|J|\le s}\left\{
        \frac{n\norm{d_J}^2}{\sigma_0^2}
        +\frac{n\norm{h_J}^2}{\sigma_r^2}
        \right\}\lesssim m.
\end{equation}
When $m=o(n)$, local isotropy, \eqref{eq:score-mean-cap}, and
\begin{equation*}
        \sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \max_{K\in\{K^{xx},K^{rr},K^{xr}\}}
        \norm{K_{JJ}}_{\mathrm{op}}=o(1)
\end{equation*}
imply $\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}=o(1)$.  Local isotropy alone does not:
fourth cumulants govern the covariance of the score products, and
entrywise cumulant bounds need not control their sparse operator norms.

\begin{theorem}[Independent-coordinate Gaussian comparison]
\label{thm:gaussian-top-s-decoupling}
Suppose $m\to\infty$, $\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}=o(1)$, and
\eqref{eq:score-mean-cap} holds.
Let $(\xi_1,\xi_2)$ have mutually independent coordinates with
\begin{equation*}
        \xi_{1j}\sim N(\sqrt n\,d_j,\sigma_0^2),
        \qquad
        \xi_{2j}\sim N(\sqrt n\,h_j,\sigma_r^2).
\end{equation*}
The two top-$s$ functionals admit a coupling for which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-score-decoupling}
\begin{split}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\{-Z_{1j}Z_{2j}\}_{(t)}
        -\sum_{t=1}^s\{-\xi_{1j}\xi_{2j}\}_{(t)}
        =o_{\Prob}(\sigma_0\sigma_r m).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In standardized coordinates write
\begin{equation*}
        F(z)=\max_{|S|=s}-z_{1S}^\top z_{2S}
\end{equation*}
and smooth this maximum by
\begin{equation*}
        F_\tau(z)=\frac1\tau
        \log\sum_{|S|=s}\exp\{-\tau z_{1S}^\top z_{2S}\}.
\end{equation*}
Since $\log\binom ps\le m$, the smoothing error is at most $m/\tau$.
Gaussian interpolation between the laws with covariance $I_{2p}$ and
$\Omega$, followed by integration by parts, expresses the derivative of
$\E F_\tau$ as one half of the inner product of $\Omega-I_{2p}$ with
$\E\nabla^2F_\tau$.  The supportwise quadratic Hessians are supported on
one set of size $s$.  The softmax covariance of two supportwise gradients
is supported on their union, whose size is at most $2s$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:score-isotropy} and the sparse Gaussian moment bound
therefore give, uniformly along the interpolation,
\begin{equation*}
        \left|\frac{d}{dt}\E F_\tau\right|
        \lesssim\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}\{s+\tau m\},
\end{equation*}
where \eqref{eq:score-mean-cap} bounds the deterministic part of the
sparse score norm.  If $\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}=0$, the two Gaussian
laws agree.  Otherwise, taking
$\tau=(\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}})^{-1/2}$ shows that the expectations of
the two unsmoothed maxima differ by $o(m)$.

The almost-everywhere gradient of $F$ is supported on a maximizing set.
On that set,
\(\nabla F^\top\Omega\nabla F
\le\{1+\rho_s^{\mathrm{score}}\}\norm{\nabla F}^2\).
Gaussian Poincar\'e and the sparse moment bound therefore give variance
$O(m)$ under both laws.  Each maximum is within
$O_{\Prob}(\sqrt m)=o_{\Prob}(m)$ of its expectation.  Placing the two
score pairs independently on the same probability space and restoring
the two marginal scales proves
\eqref{eq:gaussian-score-decoupling}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Independent top-$s$ approximation]
\label{cor:independent-top-s}
Suppose the assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:top-s-stochastic} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-top-s-decoupling} hold.
Then the independent top-$s$ functional in
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-top-s-decoupling} can be coupled to the
searched coefficient so that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:independent-top-s}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        =\frac1{n\sigma_0^2}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\{-\xi_{1j}\xi_{2j}\}_{(t)}
        +o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}}).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:top-s-stochastic} couples the searched coefficient to
the matched top-$s$ sum at error $o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-top-s-decoupling}, divided by
$n\sigma_0^2$, contributes
$o_{\Prob}\{(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^2\}$, which is
$o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}})$.  Realizing the two couplings on one
extension proves the statement.
\end{proof}

Gaussian controls carrying no population outcome or treatment score
permit score replacement throughout $m=o(n)$: the observable products
are conditionally Gaussian exactly.

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian-control top-$s$ approximation]
\label{thm:gaussian-control-separable}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and, independently of
$(x^{(i)},r^{(i)})$, each control row has distribution $N(0,C)$.  Suppose
$m\to\infty$, $m=o(n)$, and $\rho_s(C)=o(1)$.  On an extension of the
probability space, let $\zeta_1,\zeta_2\sim N(0,I_p)$ be independent.
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-control-separable}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        ={}&\frac{\sigma_r}{n\sigma_0}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        \{-\zeta_{1j}\zeta_{2j}:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}\\
        &+o_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\frac mn\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Here $d=h=0$.  Use the sample standard deviations in
\eqref{eq:observable-forward-scores}.
On the event
$\mathcal A_n=\{\hat\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r>0\}$, define
\begin{equation*}
        U=\frac{Z_{1n}}{\hat\sigma_0},
        \qquad
        V=\frac{\hat Z_{2n}}{\hat\sigma_r}.
\end{equation*}
Empirical second-moment concentration gives
$\Prob(\mathcal A_n)\to1$; define $(U,V)$ arbitrarily on its complement.
Conditional on $(x,r)$, the pair $(U,V)$ is jointly Gaussian.  Its two
marginal covariance matrices are $C$, while
\begin{equation*}
        \Cov(U,V\mid x,r)
        =\frac{x^\top\hat r}
        {n\hat\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r}C=0.
\end{equation*}
Thus $U$ and $V$ are independent $N(0,C)$ vectors, and their conditional
law does not depend on $(x,r)$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:empirical-top-s} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-gaussian-products}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        ={}&\frac{\hat\sigma_r}
        {n\hat\sigma_0}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\{-U_jV_j:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}\\
        &+O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \delta^2\{\delta+\rho_s(C)\}\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The standardized score covariance of $(U,V)$ is
$\operatorname{diag}(C,C)$; hence its sparse score-isotropy coefficient
is $\rho_s(C)$ and its mean cap is zero.  Applied to this pair,
Theorem~\ref{thm:gaussian-top-s-decoupling} couples
the sum in \eqref{eq:conditional-gaussian-products} to its independent
coordinate counterpart with error $o_{\Prob}(m)$.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\hat\sigma_0}{\sigma_0}=1+O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2}),
        \qquad
        \frac{\hat\sigma_r}{\sigma_r}=1+O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2}),
\end{equation*}
and each top-$s$ sum is $O_{\Prob}(m)$.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:conditional-gaussian-products} proves the result.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fixed-budget calibration}

The preceding theorem resolves the Gaussian-control case throughout
$m=o(n)$ at the $m/n$ envelope.  A fixed search budget requires a sharper
comparison because
centering the extreme order statistics leaves fluctuations of order
$1/n$.  The needed extreme-value result follows directly from the same
conditional Gaussian representation.

\begin{lemma}[Weakly correlated Gaussian product extremes]
\label{lem:gaussian-product-extremes}
Let $U,V$ be independent $N(0,C_p)$ vectors, where
$(C_p)_{jj}=1$, and suppose
\begin{equation*}
        \rho_{\max}(C_p)\log p\to0,
        \qquad
        \rho_{\max}(C_p)=\max_{j\ne k}|(C_p)_{jk}|.
\end{equation*}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-centering}
        b_p=\log p-\frac12\log\log p-\frac12\log(2\pi).
\end{equation}
Then the point process
\begin{equation*}
        \sum_{j=1}^p\delta_{-U_jV_j-b_p}
\end{equation*}
converges to a Poisson point process on $\R$ with intensity
$e^{-z}\,dz$.  If $W_{(1)}\ge\cdots\ge W_{(p)}$ are the ordered values
of $-U_jV_j$, then, for every fixed $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-order-limit}
        \sum_{t=1}^s(W_{(t)}-b_p)
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \sum_{t=1}^s(-\log H_t),
        \qquad
        H_t=\mathcal E_1+\cdots+\mathcal E_t,
\end{equation}
where the $\mathcal E_t$ are independent standard exponential variables.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For independent standard normals $G_1,G_2$, the density of $G_1G_2$ is
$K_0(|z|)/\pi$, where $K_0$ is the modified Bessel function.  Its
large-argument expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-product-tail}
        \Prob(G_1G_2>z)
        =\frac{e^{-z}}{\sqrt{2\pi z}}
        \{1+O(z^{-1})\}.
\end{equation}
Consequently,
$p\Prob(-G_1G_2>b_p+x)\to e^{-x}$ for each fixed $x$.

It remains to verify that finitely many exceedances factorize.  Fix $k$
and distinct indices $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)$.  The covariance of
$(U_J,V_J)$ is $\operatorname{diag}(C_{JJ},C_{JJ})$, and
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{C_{JJ}-I_k}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \le(k-1)\rho_{\max}(C_p).
\end{equation*}
Thus $C_{JJ}$ is nonsingular for all sufficiently large $p$.
On the set where every coordinate has magnitude at most
$R\sqrt{\log p}$, the ratio of this Gaussian density to the standard
$2k$-variate Gaussian density is
\begin{equation*}
        \exp\{O_k(\rho_{\max}(C_p)\log p)\}=1+o(1)
\end{equation*}
uniformly in $J$.  Choose the fixed $R$ so that $R^2/2>k+1$.
A marginal Gaussian union bound shows, under both covariance laws, that
the probability of truncation failure is $o(p^{-k})$.
Together with \eqref{eq:gaussian-product-tail}, this proves, uniformly
for fixed $x_1,\ldots,x_k$,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
 &\Prob\bigl(-U_{j_\ell}V_{j_\ell}>b_p+x_\ell,
             \ 1\le\ell\le k\bigr)\\
 &\hspace{25mm}=\{1+o(1)\}
   \prod_{\ell=1}^k
   \Prob(-U_{j_\ell}V_{j_\ell}>b_p+x_\ell).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Inclusion--exclusion gives the same factorization for mixed counts on
disjoint bounded intervals.  Their factorial moment measures therefore
converge to those of a Poisson process with intensity $e^{-z}\,dz$,
which proves the point-process convergence.  Moreover,
\eqref{eq:gaussian-product-tail} and a union bound give
\begin{equation*}
        \limsup_{p\to\infty}
        \Prob\left(\max_{j\le p}(-U_jV_j-b_p)>R\right)
        \le e^{-R}.
\end{equation*}
Letting $R\to\infty$ rules out escape at the upper boundary and permits
passage to the ordered atoms.  Their representation is
$-\log H_1,-\log H_2,\ldots$, which gives
\eqref{eq:product-order-limit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gaussian-control coefficient extremes]
\label{lem:gaussian-control-coefficient-extremes}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and, independently of
$(x^{(i)},r^{(i)})$, each control row has distribution $N(0,C)$.  Let
$s$ be fixed and let $p\to\infty$.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-coefficient-extreme-regime}
        (\log p)^2=o(n),
        \qquad
        \rho_s(C)\log p=o(1),
\end{equation}
then, on $\{\hat\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r>0\}$, define
\begin{equation*}
        U=\frac{X^\top x}{\sqrt n\hat\sigma_0},
        \qquad
        V=\frac{X^\top\hat r}{\sqrt n\hat\sigma_r},
\end{equation*}
On the complementary event, let $U,V$ be fresh independent $N(0,C)$
vectors, independent of the data.
Conditionally on $(x,r)$, the vectors $U,V$ are independent $N(0,C)$ and
their conditional law does not depend on $(x,r)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-coefficient-extreme-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \frac{n\hat\sigma_0}{\hat\sigma_r}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        =\sum_{t=1}^s\{-U_jV_j:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}+o_{\Prob}(1).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work on $\{\hat\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r>0\}$, whose probability tends to
one, and condition on $(x,r)$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        e_1=\frac{x}{\sqrt n\hat\sigma_0},
        \qquad
        e_2=\frac{\hat r}{\sqrt n\hat\sigma_r},
\end{equation*}
so $e_1^\top e_2=0$.  Let $O_\perp$ have orthonormal columns spanning
their orthogonal complement, and define
\begin{equation*}
        X_\perp=O_\perp^\top X.
\end{equation*}
Row-orthogonal invariance makes $U,V$ independent $N(0,C)$ vectors and
makes the $n-2$ rows of $X_\perp$ independent $N(0,C)$ vectors,
independently of $(U,V)$.  For every $S$, write
\begin{equation*}
        A_S=X_{\perp,S}^\top X_{\perp,S}+V_SV_S^\top,
        \qquad
        X_S^\top X_S=A_S+U_SU_S^\top.
\end{equation*}
The rate condition makes every size-$s$ block of $C$ positive definite,
so $A_S$ is almost surely invertible for all sufficiently large $n$.
Partitioned regression and the Sherman--Morrison formula give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-conditional-gaussian-regression}
\begin{split}
        \frac{n\hat\sigma_0}{\hat\sigma_r}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        =-nU_S^\top A_S^{-1}V_S.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Indeed, before the Sherman--Morrison cancellation, the right side
without the factor $n$ is
\begin{equation*}
        -\frac{U_S^\top
        (A_S+U_SU_S^\top)^{-1}V_S}
        {1-U_S^\top(A_S+U_SU_S^\top)^{-1}U_S}
        =-U_S^\top A_S^{-1}V_S.
\end{equation*}

It remains to compare \eqref{eq:exact-conditional-gaussian-regression}
only where its maximum can occur.  Write
\begin{equation*}
        Q_S^I(U,V)=-U_S^\top V_S,
        \qquad
        \Theta_S=-nU_S^\top A_S^{-1}V_S,
\end{equation*}
where the superscript $I$ denotes the identity-covariance objective, and
let
\begin{equation*}
        \mathcal I(w)=\{j:-U_jV_j\ge b_p-w\}.
\end{equation*}
Choose deterministic $c_n\to\infty$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localization-slack}
        c_n=o(\log p),
        \qquad
        (\log p)^2c_n/n=o(1).
\end{equation}
Since $\rho_{\max}(C)\le\rho_s(C)$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-product-extremes} implies, with probability
tending to one,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:raw-product-window}
        \{-U_jV_j:j\in[p]\}_{(1)}\le b_p+c_n,
        \qquad
        \{-U_jV_j:j\in[p]\}_{(s)}\ge b_p-c_n.
\end{equation}
A Gaussian union bound also gives
$\max_j(|U_j|\vee|V_j|)\le K\sqrt{\log p}$ with probability tending
to one.

Two Gram bounds localize the comparison.  First, a union over the
fixed-size supports and a Gaussian Wishart bound give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-perp-gram}
        \sup_{|S|\le s}
        \left\|\frac1nX_{\perp,S}^\top X_{\perp,S}-C_{SS}
        \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
        \le K\left\{\sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}}+\frac{\log p}{n}\right\}
\end{equation}
with probability tending to one.  Second, there is an event of
probability tending to one on which, simultaneously for
$c_n\le w\le(\log p)/2$,
\begin{align}
        |\mathcal I(w)|&\le\exp(Kw),
        \label{eq:localized-product-count}\\
        \sup_{\substack{S\subseteq\mathcal I(w)\\|S|\le s}}
        \left\|\frac1nX_{\perp,S}^\top X_{\perp,S}-C_{SS}
        \right\|_{\mathrm{op}}
        &\le K\left(\sqrt{\frac wn}+\frac wn\right).
        \label{eq:localized-perp-gram}
\end{align}
To verify this event, the marginal tail in
\eqref{eq:gaussian-product-tail} gives
$\E|\mathcal I(w)|\le Ke^w$ for $w\le(\log p)/2$.
Markov's inequality at the dyadic values $w=2^kc_n$ proves
\eqref{eq:localized-product-count} simultaneously.  Conditional on
$(U,V)$, $X_\perp$ is independent of these sets.  A fixed-size Wishart
bound over at most $\exp(Ksw)$ supports, followed by the same dyadic
union, proves \eqref{eq:localized-perp-gram}.  Monotonicity of
$\mathcal I(w)$ extends both bounds between adjacent dyadic values.  The
factor $(n-2)/n$ in the Wishart mean contributes $O(n^{-1})$, which is
absorbed by the displayed remainders.

On these events, inverse perturbation gives, for
$S\subseteq\mathcal I(w)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-field-error}
\begin{split}
        |\Theta_S-Q_S^I(U,V)|
        \le \mathsf e_n(w)
        :=K\log p\left\{
        \rho_s(C)+\frac{\log p}{n}+\sqrt{\frac wn}+\frac wn
        \right\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here $\|C_{SS}-I\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\rho_s(C)$,
$\|V_S\|^2\le K\log p$, and the latter term contributes at most
$K(\log p)/n$ to $A_S/n$.  The global Gram bound yields the analogous
uniform error
\begin{equation*}
        \mathsf e_n^\circ
        =K\log p\left\{
        \rho_s(C)+\sqrt{\frac{\log p}{n}}+\frac{\log p}{n}
        \right\}
        =o(\log p).
\end{equation*}

Let $S^\star$ maximize $\Theta_S$ and let $S_0$ contain the $s$
largest products $-U_jV_j$.  The global bound and
\eqref{eq:raw-product-window} imply
\begin{equation*}
        S^\star\subseteq\mathcal I(w_0),
        \qquad
        w_0=(2s-1)c_n+2\mathsf e_n^\circ=o(\log p).
\end{equation*}
Indeed, a support containing $-U_jV_j<b_p-w$ has identity-covariance
objective at most $s b_p+(s-1)c_n-w$, whereas
$Q_{S_0}^I(U,V)\ge s(b_p-c_n)$.
The same comparison sharpens itself: whenever
$S^\star\subseteq\mathcal I(w_k)$,
\begin{equation*}
        S^\star\subseteq\mathcal I(w_{k+1}),
        \qquad
        w_{k+1}=(2s-1)c_n+2\mathsf e_n(w_k).
\end{equation*}
For all sufficiently large $n$, Young's inequality and
\eqref{eq:gaussian-coefficient-extreme-regime} give
\begin{equation*}
        w_{k+1}\le A'c_n+\frac12w_k.
\end{equation*}
With
\begin{equation*}
        k_n=\left\lceil
        \log_2\{(w_0/c_n)\vee1\}\right\rceil,
\end{equation*}
the simultaneous local event therefore gives
$S^\star\subseteq\mathcal I(A''c_n)$.  Finally,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        \mathsf e_n(A''c_n)
        &\le K\left\{
        (\log p)\rho_s(C)+\frac{(\log p)^2}{n}
        +\sqrt{\frac{(\log p)^2c_n}{n}}
        +\frac{c_n\log p}{n}
        \right\}
        =o(1).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The same bound applies to $S_0\subseteq\mathcal I(c_n)$, so
\begin{equation*}
        \left|\max_{|S|=s}\Theta_S-Q_{S_0}^I(U,V)\right|
        =o_{\Prob}(1).
\end{equation*}
Combining this relation with
\eqref{eq:exact-conditional-gaussian-regression} proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Experimental product extremes]
\label{lem:experimental-product-extremes}
Let $(x^{(i)},\varepsilon^{(i)},X^{(i)})$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, be independent
rows from the model in \eqref{eq:model}.  Suppose $x^{(i)}$ is independent
of $X^{(i)}$, $\sigma_\varepsilon>0$, and recall that
$\varepsilon^{(i)}$ is independent of the design.  Define
\begin{equation*}
        A_{n,j}=\frac1{\sigma_0\sqrt n}
        \sum_{i=1}^n X_j^{(i)}x^{(i)},
        \qquad
        E_{n,j}=\frac1{\sigma_\varepsilon\sqrt n}
        \sum_{i=1}^n X_j^{(i)}\varepsilon^{(i)}.
\end{equation*}
If $p\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-extreme-regime}
        \log p=o(n^{1/3}),
        \qquad
        \rho_{\max}(C)\log p=o(1),
        \qquad
        \rho_{\max}(C)=\max_{j\ne k}|C_{jk}|,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-product-process}
        \sum_{j=1}^p
        \delta_{-A_{n,j}E_{n,j}-b_p}
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \operatorname{PPP}(e^{-z}\,dz).
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every fixed $s$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-product-order-limit}
        \sum_{t=1}^s
        \{-A_{n,j}E_{n,j}:j\in[p]\}_{(t)}-s b_p
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \sum_{t=1}^s(-\log H_t),
\end{equation}
where $H_t=\mathcal E_1+\cdots+\mathcal E_t$ for independent standard
exponential variables $\mathcal E_1,\mathcal E_2,\ldots$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $k$ and distinct indices $J=(j_1,\ldots,j_k)$.  The standardized
row score
\begin{equation*}
        W_{i,J}
        =\left(\frac{x^{(i)}X_J^{(i)}}{\sigma_0},
        \frac{\varepsilon^{(i)}X_J^{(i)}}{\sigma_\varepsilon}\right)
        \in\R^{2k}
\end{equation*}
is centered and has covariance
$\Sigma_J=\operatorname{diag}(C_{JJ},C_{JJ})$.  Independence and the
sub-Gaussian bounds imply, uniformly in $J$, that
$\E\exp(t^\top W_{i,J})<\infty$ on a fixed neighborhood of the origin.
Indeed, conditioning on $X_J^{(i)}$ first bounds this moment by the
expectation of
\begin{equation*}
        \exp\{K(|a^\top X_J^{(i)}|^2+|b^\top X_J^{(i)}|^2)\},
        \qquad t=(a,b),
\end{equation*}
which is uniformly finite for $\norm{t}$ small.  Since
$\rho_{\max}(C)\to0$, $\Sigma_J$ is uniformly nonsingular for each
fixed $k$.

Put $S_{n,J}=n^{-1/2}\sum_iW_{i,J}$.  The uniform analytic neighborhood,
the covariance floor, and fixed dimension place the whitened row laws in
Zaitsev's class $\mathcal A_{2k}(\tau_k)$ for a common $\tau_k$.
Theorem~1.2 of the multidimensional KMT construction\footnote{A.~Yu.\
Zaitsev, \emph{Multidimensional version of the results of
Koml\'os, Major and Tusn\'ady for vectors with finite exponential
moments}, ESAIM: Probability and Statistics 2 (1998), 41--108.}
therefore places $S_{n,J}$ and $G_J\sim N(0,\Sigma_J)$ on one probability
space so that, for every fixed $D>0$, uniformly in $J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-dimensional-score-coupling}
        \Prob\left(\norm{S_{n,J}-G_J}_\infty>\epsilon_n\right)
        \le p^{-D},
        \qquad
        \epsilon_n=\frac{C_{k,D}(\log n+\log p)}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}
The constants are uniform because $k$ is fixed, the eigenvalues of
$\Sigma_J$ stay in a common compact subset of $(0,\infty)$, and the
whitened vectors have a common analytic neighborhood.

Fix bounded $z_1,\ldots,z_k$ and choose $D>k+2$ and $R$ with
$R^2/2>k+2$.  On the coupling event and the Gaussian cube
$\norm{G_J}_\infty\le R\sqrt{\log p}$,
\begin{equation*}
 \max_{\ell\le k}
 \left|A_{n,j_\ell}E_{n,j_\ell}
       -G_{j_\ell,1}G_{j_\ell,2}\right|
 \le\Delta_n,
 \qquad
 \Delta_n=2R\sqrt{\log p}\,\epsilon_n+\epsilon_n^2=o(1).
\end{equation*}
The last relation follows from $\log p=o(n^{1/3})$; the term containing
$\log n$ is smaller than $n^{-1/2}\log n\sqrt{\log p}$.  The coupling
failure and the complement of the cube are both $o(p^{-k})$.  Hence the
score excursion probability is sandwiched between the matched Gaussian
probabilities with thresholds $b_p+z_\ell\pm\Delta_n$, up to
$o(p^{-k})$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-product-extremes} and
$\rho_{\max}(C)\log p=o(1)$ show that shifting all thresholds by $o(1)$
changes either Gaussian probability by a $1+o(1)$ factor.  Therefore, uniformly
in distinct $J$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-score-comparison}
\begin{split}
 &\Prob\left(-A_{n,j_\ell}E_{n,j_\ell}>b_p+z_\ell,
             \ 1\le\ell\le k\right)\\
 &\qquad=\{1+o(1)\}
 \Prob\left(-G_{j_\ell,1}G_{j_\ell,2}>b_p+z_\ell,
             \ 1\le\ell\le k\right),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $(G_{J,1},G_{J,2})$ has covariance
$\operatorname{diag}(C_{JJ},C_{JJ})$.

The Gaussian density comparison
in Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-product-extremes}, applied to the fixed set
$J$, then gives, uniformly for bounded $z_1,\ldots,z_k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-factorial-tail}
\begin{split}
 &\Prob\left(-A_{n,j_\ell}E_{n,j_\ell}>b_p+z_\ell,
             \ 1\le\ell\le k\right)\\
 &\hspace{30mm}=\{1+o(1)\}p^{-k}
        \exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^kz_\ell\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Inclusion--exclusion extends this relation to disjoint bounded
intervals.  Summing over distinct coordinate tuples proves convergence
of every mixed factorial moment to that of a Poisson process with
intensity $e^{-z}\,dz$.  The case $k=1$ and a union bound rule out escape
at the upper boundary.  This proves \eqref{eq:experimental-product-process};
the ordered-atom argument from Lemma~\ref{lem:gaussian-product-extremes}
gives \eqref{eq:experimental-product-order-limit}.
\end{proof}

The range in \eqref{eq:experimental-extreme-regime} is the natural
uniform range over the relative sub-Gaussian class for the stated
Gaussian centering.  At a product excursion $t\asymp\log p$, the
cubic term in the fixed-dimensional Cram\'er expansion has size
$t^{3/2}/\sqrt n$.  The two product saddles give opposite cubic terms,
but their probabilities add rather than cancel.  Thus a nonzero third
cumulant can alter the limiting exceedance intensity when
$\log p\asymp n^{1/3}$.  A wider uniform range would require vanishing
sparse third cumulants together with a fourth-order comparison, or a
distribution-dependent correction to $b_p$.

\begin{corollary}[Fixed-budget calibration]
\label{cor:fixed-budget-calibration}
Let $s$ be fixed, let $p\to\infty$, and suppose
$\rho_s(C)\log p=o(1)$.  Suppose either
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds, independently of
$(x^{(i)},r^{(i)})$ each control row has distribution $N(0,C)$, and
$(\log p)^2=o(n)$; or
\item[(ii)] in the model \eqref{eq:model}, $\beta=0$ and $x^{(i)}$ is
independent of $X^{(i)}$, and $(\log p)^3=o(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fixed-budget-limit}
\begin{split}
        \frac{n\sigma_0}{\sigma_r}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        -s b_p
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \sum_{t=1}^s(-\log H_t),
\end{split}
\end{equation}
with $b_p$ and $H_t$ defined in
\eqref{eq:product-centering} and \eqref{eq:product-order-limit}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In case \textup{(i)}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:gaussian-control-coefficient-extremes}
and \ref{lem:gaussian-product-extremes} give
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        \frac{n\hat\sigma_0}{\hat\sigma_r}
        \max_{|S|=s}(\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        -s b_p
        \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
        \sum_{t=1}^s(-\log H_t).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Moreover,
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\sigma_0\hat\sigma_r}
        {\sigma_r\hat\sigma_0}=1+O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2}),
\end{equation*}
while the uncentered statistic is $s b_p+O_{\Prob}(1)$.  Replacing the
empirical scales by the population scales therefore costs
$O_{\Prob}(\log p/\sqrt n)=o_{\Prob}(1)$, proving
\eqref{eq:fixed-budget-limit}.

In case \textup{(ii)}, $r=\varepsilon$, $d=h=0$, and
$\sigma_r=\sigma_\varepsilon$.  With the score coordinates
$A_{n,j},E_{n,j}$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:experimental-product-extremes},
the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:empirical-top-s} gives
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{n\sigma_0}{\sigma_r}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        =\sum_{t=1}^s\{-A_{n,j}E_{n,j}\}_{(t)}+o_{\Prob}(1).
\end{equation*}
Indeed, its normalized separability remainder is
$O_{\Prob}\{m\delta+m\rho_s(C)\}=o_{\Prob}(1)$.
Residualizing $\varepsilon$ on $x$ changes a product by a common
$O_{\Prob}(n^{-1/2})A_{n,j}^2$ term, while
$\max_jA_{n,j}^2=O_{\Prob}(\log p)$; replacing the empirical treatment
scale has the same $O_{\Prob}(\log p/\sqrt n)$ cost.  These terms vanish
under $(\log p)^3=o(n)$.  Since
$\rho_{\max}(C)\le\rho_s(C)$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:experimental-product-extremes}
proves the result.
\end{proof}

The Gaussian rate $(\log p)^2=o(n)$ is also natural under population
scaling: a relative sample-scale error of order $n^{-1/2}$ multiplies
the leading $s\log p$ term.  Without $\log p=o(\sqrt n)$, this generally
produces a nonnegligible random shift.  The stronger cubic restriction
in case \textup{(ii)} comes instead from non-Gaussian score tails.

\section{Bounds and packing geometry}

\subsection{Upper bounds and the observational landscape}

The moment approximation separates the size of the fluctuations from the
geometry of the population landscape.  The quantities $\phi_s$ and
$\widetilde\Psi_s$ in \eqref{eq:mean-sizes} measure the treatment and
residual-outcome score energies on the searched supports.

\begin{lemma}[Influence envelope]\label{lem:influence-envelope}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-envelope}
        \max_{|S|\le s}
        \norm{DB_S(\Gamma)
        [v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma]}_{\psi_1}
        \lesssim
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\sqrt{\phi_s}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand $u_Se_S/\eta(S)-xr/\sigma_0^2$ using
\eqref{eq:influence-residuals}.  The common $xr$ term cancels when
$d=h=0$, and every remaining summand contains at least one factor of
$C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S$ or $C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S$.  Relative
sub-Gaussianity, H\"older's inequality for Orlicz norms, and overlap bound
its $\psi_1$ norm by a constant times
$\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}/\sigma_0$ or
$\widetilde\Psi_s/\sigma_0$.  Summing these terms proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficient upper bound]\label{cor:coefficient-upper}
Let $S^\star\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$.  Suppose
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and $\delta\le\delta_0$ for a
sufficiently small constant.  With probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-upper}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat\beta_{0,S}
        -\hat\beta_{0,S^\star}
        \le C\bigg(
        &\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        \bigg).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Bernstein's inequality, \eqref{eq:influence-envelope}, and a union over the
supports give the first two terms of \eqref{eq:coefficient-upper}; the
linear Bernstein remainder has order
$(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^2$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} give the quadratic term.  The
population difference $B_S(\Gamma)-B_{S^\star}(\Gamma)$ is nonpositive, so
centering at $S^\star$ completes the argument.
\end{proof}

When $d=0$, \eqref{eq:coefficient-upper} is the experimental scale in
\eqref{eq:local-treatment-upper-scale}.  When
$\phi_s\lesssim\delta^2$, its treatment-side term is absorbed by the
bilinear term, which gives the observational rate under sparse treatment
alignment.  For arbitrary $d$, the $\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta$ term is
first-order and cannot be omitted.  Equation~\eqref{eq:score-translation}
expresses the bound in the original model parameters
$(\Psi_s,\beta,d)$.

The critical empirical field identifies the general lower object without
discarding either the linear or the quadratic fluctuation.  Fix
$S^\star\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$.  For $\epsilon\ge0$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-maximal-family}
        \mathcal N_\epsilon
        =\{S:|S|=s,\ B_S(\Gamma)\ge B_{S^\star}(\Gamma)-\epsilon\},
\end{equation}
and let $\mathcal P$ be any subfamily of $\mathcal N_\epsilon$.

\begin{theorem}[Empirical lower reduction]
\label{thm:empirical-lower-reduction}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and
$\delta\le\delta_0$.  With probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
simultaneously for every $\epsilon\ge0$ and every nonempty
$\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal N_\epsilon$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-lower-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat\beta_{0,S}
        -\hat\beta_{0,S^\star}
        \ge{}&
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}
        \{\mathcal H_{n,S}-\mathcal H_{n,S^\star}\}-\epsilon\\
        &-C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
In particular, if the maximum on the right exceeds $q$ with probability
at least $1-\alpha$, then the displayed coefficient contrast exceeds
$q-\epsilon-C(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^3$ with probability at least
$1-\alpha-Ce^{-cm}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the event in Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-empirical-expansion}, write
\begin{equation*}
        B_S(\hat\Gamma)
        =B_S(\Gamma)+\mathcal H_{n,S}+R_{n,S},
        \qquad
        \max_{|S|\le s}|R_{n,S}|
        \le C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3.
\end{equation*}
Every $S\in\mathcal P$ has population deficit at most $\epsilon$.
Subtracting the expansion at $S^\star$ and maximizing over $\mathcal P$
proves \eqref{eq:empirical-lower-reduction}.
\end{proof}

The matched moment field makes this lower object explicit in the
subcritical Gaussian-approximation regime.  Write
\begin{equation*}
        W_S=B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)-B_S(\Gamma).
\end{equation*}
Then the Gaussianized moment field obeys the deterministic inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landscape-lower-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        -B_{S^\star}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge
        \max_{S\in\mathcal N_\epsilon}
        \{W_S-W_{S^\star}\}-\epsilon.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus a lower bound is governed by a quantile of the matched field on the
near-maximal supports.  To reduce this statement to a Gaussian width,
define the linear field
\begin{equation*}
        W_S^{(1)}=\frac1{\sqrt n}DB_S(\Gamma)[G].
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-field-remainder}
        \max_{|S|=s}|W_S-W_S^{(1)}|
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-lower-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        -B_{S^\star}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge{}&\max_{S\in\mathcal N_\epsilon}
        (W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^\star}^{(1)})-\epsilon\\
        &-2\max_{|S|=s}|W_S-W_S^{(1)}|.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The canonical distance of $W_S^{(1)}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-distance}
        d_G(S,T)^2
        =\frac1n\E\left[
        DB_S(\Gamma)[G]-DB_T(\Gamma)[G]
        \right]^2.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Packing lower bound]\label{prop:packing-lower}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds,
$\delta\le\delta_0$, $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal N_\epsilon$
has cardinality $M\ge2$, and $d_G(S,T)\ge\lambda$ for distinct
$S,T\in\mathcal P$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}^2
        =\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\Var(W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^\star}^{(1)}).
\end{equation*}
For every $u\ge0$, with probability at least
$1-e^{-u^2/2}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        -B_{S^\star}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge{}&c\lambda\sqrt{\log M}-u\bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}-\epsilon\\
        &-C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sudakov minoration gives
$\E\max_{S\in\mathcal P}(W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^\star}^{(1)})
\ge c\lambda\sqrt{\log M}$.
The Gaussian lower-tail inequality subtracts at most
$u\bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}$ with probability $e^{-u^2/2}$.  The Gaussian
case of \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} bound the final term in
\eqref{eq:gaussian-lower-reduction}, proving \eqref{eq:packing-lower}.
\end{proof}

The smooth moment comparison transfers this packing bound to the
empirical field at every fixed probability level, with an explicit loss
when the target probability varies with $n$.

\begin{corollary}[Empirical packing lower bound]
\label{cor:empirical-packing-lower}
Suppose the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:packing-lower} hold, and put
\begin{equation*}
        \omega_n=\left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{equation*}
For every $u\ge0$ and $0<\zeta\le1$, with probability at least
\begin{equation*}
        1-e^{-u^2/2}-Ce^{-cm}-C\omega_n\zeta^{-3},
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-packing-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat\beta_{0,S}
        -\hat\beta_{0,S^\star}
        \ge{}&c\lambda\sqrt{\log M}-u\bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}-\epsilon\\
        &-C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2-\zeta a_n.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The empirical and matched anchored maxima are normalized by $a_n$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:centered-observational-field} bounds their $d_3$
distance by $C\omega_n$, while Proposition~\ref{prop:packing-lower}
supplies the lower quantile of the matched maximum.  Applying
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-lower-transfer} at buffer $\zeta$ proves the
display.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional and experimental lower bounds}

The proposition is informative when its Gaussian width dominates the
quadratic remainder.  In a degenerate experiment that remainder is part
of the leading scale, so the correct lower object is instead a quantile
of the full linear--bilinear chaos in
Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration}.

Write the covariance of the matched score pair in blocks as
\begin{equation*}
        \Sigma_{ab}=\Cov(Z_a,Z_b),\qquad a,b\in\{1,2\},
\end{equation*}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-score-innovation}
\begin{split}
        \Sigma_{1\mid2}
        &=\Sigma_{11}
        -\Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{\dagger}\Sigma_{21},\\
        \mu_{1\mid2}(z)
        &=\sqrt n\,d+
        \Sigma_{12}\Sigma_{22}^{\dagger}(z-\sqrt n\,h).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The generalized inverse is harmless because
$Z_2-\sqrt n\,h$ lies in the range of $\Sigma_{22}$ almost surely.  Let
$\iota_S:\R^S\to\R^p$ be coordinate embedding, and write
$\norm{v}_A=(v^\top Av)^{1/2}$ for $A\succeq0$.  For $z\in\R^p$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-embedded-score}
        q_S(z)=\iota_SC_{SS}^{-1}z_S.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional matched-score packing]
\label{prop:conditional-matched-chaos}
Let $\mathcal P=\mathcal P(Z_2)$ be a measurable family of size-$s$
supports with fixed cardinality $M\ge2$, let $S_0=S_0(Z_2)$ be a
measurable size-$s$ anchor, and fix $K\ge1$.
Suppose that, on a $Z_2$-measurable event of probability at least
$1-\pi_0$, there are measurable $\lambda_n>0$ and $\Delta_n\ge0$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-matched-chaos-geometry}
\begin{split}
        \min_{\substack{S,T\in\mathcal P\\S\ne T}}
        \norm{q_S(Z_2)-q_T(Z_2)}_{\Sigma_{1\mid2}}
        &\ge\lambda_n,\\
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}
        \norm{q_S(Z_2)-q_{S_0}(Z_2)}_{\Sigma_{1\mid2}}
        &\le K\lambda_n,\\
        \min_{S\in\mathcal P}
        \{-\mu_{1\mid2}(Z_2)^\top
        [q_S(Z_2)-q_{S_0}(Z_2)]\}
        &\ge-\Delta_n.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-matched-chaos-lower}
\begin{split}
        T_C(Z_1,Z_2)-Q_{S_0}(Z_1,Z_2)
        \ge c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}-\Delta_n.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
If Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments},
\eqref{eq:local-treatment}, and $\delta\le\delta_0$ also hold, then,
outside an additional event of probability $Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-chaos-coefficient-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        -B_{S_0}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge{}&
        \frac{c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}-\Delta_n}
        {n\sigma_0^2}\\
        &-C\left\{
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3
        \right\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $Z_2=z$,
$Z_1\sim N\{\mu_{1\mid2}(z),\Sigma_{1\mid2}\}$.  Hence
\begin{equation*}
        \E[Q_S(Z_1,z)\mid Z_2=z]
        =-\mu_{1\mid2}(z)^\top q_S(z),
\end{equation*}
and the canonical distance between two centered support values is
$\norm{q_S(z)-q_T(z)}_{\Sigma_{1\mid2}}$.  Conditional Sudakov
minoration gives expectation at least
$c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}$ for the anchored centered maximum.  Its
conditional Lipschitz radius is at most $K\lambda_n$, so Gaussian
lower-tail concentration at half this expectation has failure probability
$Ce^{-c\log M}$.  The last line of
\eqref{eq:conditional-matched-chaos-geometry} accounts for the
conditional mean and proves \eqref{eq:conditional-matched-chaos-lower}.
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration} bounds its remainder
uniformly in $S$.  Applying that bound to the maximum and the anchor
proves \eqref{eq:matched-chaos-coefficient-lower}.
\end{proof}

The same result transfers to the empirical field in the score-comparison
regime.  The anchored formulation is important: it preserves the
population-optimal specification rather than replacing it by an
uncontrolled common term.

\begin{corollary}[Empirical conditional-chaos lower bound]
\label{cor:empirical-conditional-chaos-lower}
Suppose the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}
hold.  Let $S_0$ be deterministic, and suppose the event in
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-matched-chaos} also has deterministic
bounds $\lambda_n\ge\underline\lambda_n$ and
$\Delta_n\le\overline\Delta_n$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \omega_n^{\mathrm{score}}
        =\left(\frac{m}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{equation*}
For every $0<\zeta\le1$, with probability at least
\begin{equation*}
        1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}
        -C\omega_n^{\mathrm{score}}\zeta^{-3},
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-conditional-chaos-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma)-B_{S_0}(\hat\Gamma)
        \ge{}&
        \frac{c\underline\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}
        -\overline\Delta_n}{n\sigma_0^2}
        -\zeta a_n^{\mathrm{score}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply the score replacement in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} to the smooth maximum
minus the fixed support value.  Its derivative bounds are unchanged, so
the anchored empirical coefficient and
$\{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)-Q_{S_0}(Z_1,Z_2)\}/(n\sigma_0^2)$ have normalized
$d_3$ distance at most $C\omega_n^{\mathrm{score}}$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-matched-chaos} supplies the lower
quantile of the second variable.  Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-lower-transfer}
at buffer $\zeta$ proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The conditional metric has a top-$s$ construction of its own.

In the coordinate-subset results below, $c_0>0$ denotes a sufficiently
small constant depending only on the fixed sub-Gaussian and overlap
constants.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional product packing]
\label{cor:conditional-product-packing}
In Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-matched-chaos}, fix a deterministic
$N\ge2s$, fixed constants $K_0,K_1\ge1$, and fixed
$0<c_V\le C_V<\infty$.  Suppose that, on a
$Z_2$-measurable event of probability at least $1-\pi_0$, there are
$Z_2$-measurable $b_n>0$ and
$\mathcal J\subseteq[p]$ with $|\mathcal J|\ge N$ such
that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-product-packing}
        b_n\le |Z_{2j}|\le K_0b_n,\qquad j\in\mathcal J.
\end{equation}
Put $L_N=\log(eN/s)$.  Suppose $\rho_s(C)\le c_0/K_0$ and, for every
$2s$-sparse $v$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-score-overlap}
        c_V\sigma_0^2\norm{v}_C^2
        \le\norm{v}_{\Sigma_{1\mid2}}^2
        \le C_V\sigma_0^2\norm{v}_C^2.
\end{equation}
Let $S_0$ be a measurable anchor satisfying
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{q_{S_0}(Z_2)}_C\le K_1b_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
Select $N$ coordinates from $\mathcal J$ by a deterministic ordering,
and let $\mathcal P$ be a deterministic constant-weight
Varshamov--Gilbert family on those coordinates.  If, for a
$Z_2$-measurable $\Delta_n\ge0$, this family satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-landscape-retention}
        \min_{S\in\mathcal P}
        \{-\mu_{1\mid2}(Z_2)^\top
        [q_S(Z_2)-q_{S_0}(Z_2)]\}\ge-\Delta_n,
\end{equation}
then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-csL_N}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-product-packing-lower}
\begin{split}
        T_C(Z_1,Z_2)-Q_{S_0}(Z_1,Z_2)
        \ge c\sigma_0b_ns\sqrt{L_N}-\Delta_n.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The constant-weight construction gives
$\log|\mathcal P|\ge csL_N$ and
$|S\mathbin\triangle T|\ge cs$ for distinct supports.
For $z=Z_2$, local isotropy gives
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{q_S(z)-\iota_Sz_S}_2
        \le\frac{\rho_s(C)}{1-\rho_s(C)}K_0b_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
The raw truncations satisfy
$\norm{\iota_Sz_S-\iota_Tz_T}_2\ge cb_n\sqrt s$.
Taking $c_0$ sufficiently small and comparing the $C$- and Euclidean
norms on $S\cup T$ therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{q_S(Z_2)-q_T(Z_2)}_C\ge cb_n\sqrt s,
        \qquad
        \norm{q_S(Z_2)}_C\le Cb_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
Condition~\eqref{eq:conditional-score-overlap} and the anchor bound turn
these into \eqref{eq:conditional-matched-chaos-geometry} with
$\lambda_n=c\sigma_0b_n\sqrt s$.  The mean condition is
\eqref{eq:conditional-landscape-retention}, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-matched-chaos} proves the result.
\end{proof}

If $b_n\asymp\sigma_r\sqrt{L_N}$, division by $n\sigma_0^2$ makes the
leading coefficient scale in
\eqref{eq:conditional-product-packing-lower}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:observational-quadratic-lower-scale}
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\frac{sL_N}{n}.
\end{equation}
Thus, when $sL_N\asymp\log M$, the conditional product packing has the
$\sigma_r\log M/(n\sigma_0)$ observational scale.  If additionally
$L_N\asymp L$, $\delta=o(1)$,
$\widetilde\Psi_s\lesssim\sigma_r\delta$, and the packing event includes
$\Delta_n\le\varepsilon_n\sigma_0\sigma_rm$ for a deterministic
$\varepsilon_n\to0$, the losses in
\eqref{eq:matched-chaos-coefficient-lower} are lower order, so the
coefficient field attains the same scale.  One square-root
entropy factor comes from the realized outcome-score subset and the
other from conditional Gaussian minoration.  Under random assignment
with $x$ independent of $(X,r)$,
$\Sigma_{12}=0$, $\Sigma_{1\mid2}=\sigma_0^2C$, and
$\mu_{1\mid2}=0$, so
\eqref{eq:conditional-score-overlap} and
\eqref{eq:conditional-landscape-retention} hold automatically.  In an
observational design they are respectively a score-level overlap
condition and a genuine landscape-retention condition.

At the critical boundary, the needed lower-tail property can be stated
directly for the randomized treatment.  A sequence of centered vectors
$x_n\in\R^n$ with covariance $\sigma_0^2I_n$ has uniform exponential
multiplier minoration if, for each fixed $K$, there are constants
$c_K,C_K>0$, independent of $n$, such that every finite family
$a_1,\ldots,a_M\in\R^n$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier-minoration-geometry}
        \min_{k\ne\ell}\sigma_0\norm{a_k-a_\ell}_2\ge\lambda,
        \qquad
        \max_k\sigma_0\norm{a_k}_2\le K\lambda
\end{equation}
obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier-minoration}
        \Prob\left(
        \max_{k\le M}a_k^\top x_n\ge c_K\lambda\sqrt{\log M}
        \right)
        \ge1-C_Ke^{-c_K\log M}.
\end{equation}
Gaussian treatment satisfies this property by Sudakov minoration and
Gaussian lower-tail concentration.  General sub-Gaussian treatment need
not satisfy it.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional experimental packing]
\label{prop:conditional-experimental-packing}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds, $d=0$,
$\delta\le\delta_0$, the sample treatment vector $x$ is independent
of $(X,r)$, and its sequence of laws has uniform exponential multiplier
minoration.
For $|S|=s$, put
\begin{equation*}
        \mathcal C_{n,S}
        =\frac{Q_S(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})}{n\sigma_0^2},
        \qquad \mathcal F=\sigma(X,r).
\end{equation*}
Let $\mathcal P\subseteq\{S\subseteq[p]:|S|=s\}$ be an
$\mathcal F$-measurable family of a fixed cardinality $M\ge2$, and
define its conditional distance and radius by
\begin{align*}
        d_n(S,T)^2
        &=\E[(\mathcal C_{n,S}-\mathcal C_{n,T})^2\mid\mathcal F],\\
        \bar v_n^2
        &=\max_{S\in\mathcal P}
        \E[\mathcal C_{n,S}^2\mid\mathcal F].
\end{align*}
Suppose that, on an $\mathcal F$-measurable event of probability at
least $1-\pi_0$, the family satisfies, for an
$\mathcal F$-measurable $\lambda_n>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-packing-geometry}
        \min_{S\ne T\in\mathcal P}d_n(S,T)\ge\lambda_n,
        \qquad \bar v_n\le K\lambda_n.
\end{equation}
Then, for constants depending only on $K$ and the standing assumptions,
with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-experimental-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \ge{}&c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}\\
        &-C\left(
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Conditional on $\mathcal F$, the field
$\{\mathcal C_{n,S}:S\in\mathcal P\}$ is a centered linear process in
$x$.  Its coefficient vectors satisfy
\eqref{eq:multiplier-minoration-geometry} by
\eqref{eq:conditional-packing-geometry}.  Independence and
\eqref{eq:multiplier-minoration} give the first term in
\eqref{eq:conditional-experimental-lower} with conditional failure
probability $Ce^{-c\log M}$.  The high-probability empirical statement in
Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration}, obtained from the full sparse-moment
and score tails at $t=m$, contributes the final remainder and failure
probability.
\end{proof}

The packing geometry has an exact score representation.  Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-outcome-score}
        q_{n,S}=\iota_SC_{SS}^{-1}Z_{2n,S},
\end{equation}
and recall $\hat C=X^\top X/n$.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional score geometry]
\label{lem:conditional-score-geometry}
Under the sampling conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-experimental-packing},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-score-metric}
\begin{split}
        \Cov(\mathcal C_{n,S},\mathcal C_{n,T}\mid\mathcal F)
        &=\frac{q_{n,S}^\top\hat Cq_{n,T}}
        {n^2\sigma_0^2},\\
        d_n(S,T)&=\frac{\norm{q_{n,S}-q_{n,T}}_{\hat C}}
        {n\sigma_0},
        \qquad
        \bar v_n=\frac1{n\sigma_0}
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{q_{n,S}}_{\hat C}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
On the relative-RIP event with distortion $\rho_{\mathrm{RIP}}<1$, every
$2s$-sparse $q$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rip-score-metric}
        (1-\rho_{\mathrm{RIP}})\norm{q}_C^2
        \le\norm{q}_{\hat C}^2
        \le(1+\rho_{\mathrm{RIP}})\norm{q}_C^2.
\end{equation}
These identities use the range inverse when a block is singular.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:embedded-outcome-score} gives
\begin{equation*}
        \mathcal C_{n,S}
        =-\frac{Z_{1n}^\top q_{n,S}}{n\sigma_0^2}
        =-\frac{x^\top Xq_{n,S}}{n^{3/2}\sigma_0^2}.
\end{equation*}
Conditional independence and $\Cov(x)=\sigma_0^2I_n$ prove
\eqref{eq:conditional-score-metric}.  A difference of two embedded
scores is supported on at most $2s$ coordinates, so
\eqref{eq:rip-score-metric} is the quadratic-form version of
\eqref{eq:relative-rip}.  Support compression handles the null space.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Embedded-score packing]
\label{cor:embedded-score-packing}
Suppose the sampling conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-experimental-packing} hold.  Suppose
that, on an $\mathcal F$-measurable event of probability at least
$1-\pi_0$, a family
$\mathcal P\subseteq\{S\subseteq[p]:|S|=s\}$ of fixed cardinality
$M\ge2$ and an
$\mathcal F$-measurable $\Lambda_n>0$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-score-packing}
        \min_{S\ne T\in\mathcal P}
        \norm{q_{n,S}-q_{n,T}}_C\ge\Lambda_n,
        \qquad
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{q_{n,S}}_C\le K\Lambda_n.
\end{equation}
Then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-score-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \ge{}&c\frac{\Lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}}{n\sigma_0}\\
        &-C\left(
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The relative-RIP event has failure probability $Ce^{-cm}$ and
$\rho_{\mathrm{RIP}}\le C\delta<1/2$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional-score-geometry}
turns \eqref{eq:embedded-score-packing} into
\eqref{eq:conditional-packing-geometry} with
$\lambda_n=\sqrt{1-\rho_{\mathrm{RIP}}}\Lambda_n/(n\sigma_0)$ and a
radius-to-separation
ratio bounded in terms of $K$.  Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-experimental-packing}
then proves \eqref{eq:embedded-score-lower}.
\end{proof}

In particular, suppose the event in
\eqref{eq:embedded-score-packing} has probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$
and its family satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-matching-packing}
        \log M\ge c m,
        \qquad
        \Lambda_n\ge c\sqrt n
        \{\Psi_s^2+\sigma_r^2\delta^2\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
For a sufficiently small $\delta_0$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:embedded-score-packing} then gives, with probability
at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-lower-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \ge c\frac{\delta}{\sigma_0}
        \{\Psi_s^2+\sigma_r^2\delta^2\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:experimental-matching-packing} is a sufficient bridge to
the matching experimental scale.  Its separation condition supplies
multiplicity that the scalar energy $\Psi_s$ does not encode.

Under local isotropy, a coordinate subset supplies this packing and
gives a construction parallel to the top-$s$ approximation.

\begin{corollary}[Top-$s$ score packing]
\label{cor:score-packing-lower}
Suppose the sampling conditions of
Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional-experimental-packing} hold.  Fix
$K_0\ge1$ and a deterministic $N\ge2s$, and put
$L_N=\log(eN/s)$.  The constants below may depend on the fixed $K_0$.
Suppose $\rho_s(C)\le c_0/K_0$.  If, on an
$\mathcal F$-measurable event of probability at least $1-\pi_0$,
there are an $\mathcal F$-measurable $b_n>0$ and a set
$\mathcal J_n\subseteq[p]$ with $|\mathcal J_n|\ge N$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-packing}
        b_n\le |Z_{2n,j}|\le K_0b_n,
        \qquad j\in\mathcal J_n,
\end{equation}
then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-csL_N}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-packing-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \ge{}&c\frac{b_ns\sqrt{L_N}}{n\sigma_0}\\
        &-C\left(
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3\right).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A deterministic ordering selects $N$ coordinates from $\mathcal J_n$.  The
constant-weight Varshamov--Gilbert construction gives a measurable
family $\mathcal P$ of $s$-subsets of these coordinates such that
\begin{equation*}
        \log|\mathcal P|\ge csL_N,
        \qquad |S\mathbin\triangle T|\ge cs
        \quad(S\ne T).
\end{equation*}
For $z=Z_{2n}$, local isotropy gives
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{q_{n,S}-\iota_Sz_S}_2
        \le\frac{\rho_s(C)}{1-\rho_s(C)}K_0b_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
The raw truncations obey
$\norm{\iota_Sz_S-\iota_Tz_T}_2\ge cb_n\sqrt s$.
For a sufficiently small $c_0$, comparison on $S\cup T$ therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{q_{n,S}-q_{n,T}}_C\ge cb_n\sqrt s,
        \qquad
        \norm{q_{n,S}}_C\le CK_0b_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
Corollary~\ref{cor:embedded-score-packing} applies with
$\Lambda_n=cb_n\sqrt s$ and proves the claim.
\end{proof}

The coordinate-subset condition can itself be verified in a Gaussian
experiment.
The following representation separates its common radial fluctuation from
its coordinate innovations.

\begin{lemma}[Conditional Gaussian outcome scores]
\label{lem:conditional-gaussian-outcome-scores}
Suppose $(X^{(i)},r^{(i)})$ is jointly Gaussian.  For a deterministic
$A\subseteq[p]$, put
\begin{equation*}
        R_n=\frac{\norm{r}_2^2}{n\sigma_r^2}.
\end{equation*}
Conditional on $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-gaussian-outcome-score-law}
        Z_{2n,A}\sim N\!\left(
        \sqrt n\,R_n h_A,
        \sigma_r^2R_n\left\{C_{AA}
        -\frac{h_Ah_A^\top}{\sigma_r^2}\right\}\right).
\end{equation}
Equivalently, there are jointly Gaussian $(G_A,G_0)$, independent of $r$,
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-gaussian-outcome-score-representation}
\begin{gathered}
        G_A\sim N(0,C_{AA}),
        \qquad G_0\sim N(0,1),
        \qquad \Cov(G_A,G_0)=h_A/\sigma_r,\\
        Z_{2n,j}=h_j(\sqrt n\,R_n-\sqrt{R_n}\,G_0)
        +\sigma_r\sqrt{R_n}\,G_j,
        \qquad j\in A.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Gaussian conditioning gives
\begin{equation*}
        X_A^{(i)}\mid r^{(i)}\sim
        N\!\left(\frac{h_A}{\sigma_r^2}r^{(i)},
        C_{AA}-\frac{h_Ah_A^\top}{\sigma_r^2}\right).
\end{equation*}
Summing $X_A^{(i)}r^{(i)}/\sqrt n$ proves
\eqref{eq:conditional-gaussian-outcome-score-law}.  The covariance of
$\sigma_rG_A-h_AG_0$ is
$\sigma_r^2C_{AA}-h_Ah_A^\top$, which proves the representation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian experimental matching bound]
\label{cor:gaussian-experimental-recovery}
Suppose $x^{(i)}\sim N(0,\sigma_0^2)$ is independent of $(X,r)$,
$(X^{(i)},r^{(i)})$ is jointly Gaussian, and
$\delta\le\delta_0$.  Fix constants $c_L,a,A>0$.  Let $C_0,K$ be
sufficiently large constants and $\epsilon_0$ a sufficiently small constant.
Suppose one of the following two conditions holds.

\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Psi_s\le K\sigma_r\delta$, there is a deterministic set
      $A_0$ such that, with $P_0=|A_0|$ and
      $L_0=\log(eP_0/s)$,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-noise-pool}
              P_0\ge C_0s,\qquad C_{A_0A_0}=I,\qquad L_0\ge c_LL,
              \max_{j\in A_0}\sqrt n\,|h_j|
              \le\epsilon_0\sigma_r\sqrt{L_0}.
      \end{equation}

\item If $\Psi_s>K\sigma_r\delta$, there is a deterministic set
      $A_1$ such that, with $P_1=|A_1|$ and
      $L_1=\log(eP_1/s)$,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-signal-pool}
      \begin{gathered}
              P_1\ge C_0s,\qquad C_{A_1A_1}=I,\qquad L_1\ge c_LL,\\
              a\frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt s}
              \le |h_j|\le
              A\frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt s},\qquad j\in A_1.
      \end{gathered}
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Then, for constants depending only on the displayed fixed constants and
the standing assumptions, with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-experimental-matching-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \ge c\frac{\delta}{\sigma_0}
        \{\Psi_s^2+\sigma_r^2\delta^2\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional-gaussian-outcome-scores} applies on either
pool.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-radial-event}
        \Prob\left(R_n\notin[3/4,5/4]\right)
        +\Prob(|G_0|>\sqrt n/8)\le Ce^{-cn}.
\end{equation}
On the noise pool, the coordinates of $G_{A_0}$ are independent standard
normals.  Choose fixed $0<a_0<b_0$ with $b_0^2/2<1$.  The expected number
of indices satisfying
\begin{equation*}
        a_0\sqrt{L_0}\le |G_j|\le b_0\sqrt{L_0}
\end{equation*}
is at least
\begin{equation*}
        \mu_0=cP_0e^{-b_0^2L_0/2}
        =cs\exp\{(1-b_0^2/2)L_0\}.
\end{equation*}
Because $L_0\ge c_LL$ and $C_0$ is sufficiently large,
$\mu_0\ge\max\{cm,4s\}$ and
$\log(e\mu_0/s)\ge cL$.  A Chernoff bound has failure probability at
most $Ce^{-cm}$ and leaves at least $N_0=\lfloor\mu_0/2\rfloor$
such coordinates.
On the event in \eqref{eq:gaussian-radial-event}, the mean cap in
\eqref{eq:gaussian-noise-pool} then leaves a set $\mathcal J_0$ of size
at least $N_0$ for which
\begin{equation*}
        c\sigma_r\sqrt{L_0}
        \le |Z_{2n,j}|\le
        C\sigma_r\sqrt{L_0},\qquad j\in\mathcal J_0,
\end{equation*}
and $\log(eN_0/s)\ge cL$.

On the signal pool, \eqref{eq:gaussian-radial-event} makes the first term
in \eqref{eq:conditional-gaussian-outcome-score-representation}
comparable to $\sqrt n\,|h_j|$.  Since
$\Psi_s>K\sigma_r\delta$, the constant $K$ can be chosen so that the
innovation is smaller on each coordinate with probability at least
$7/8$.  A second Chernoff bound, using $P_1\ge cm$, leaves a set
$\mathcal J_1$ of size at least $3P_1/4$
such that
\begin{equation*}
        c\frac{\sqrt n\,\Psi_s}{\sqrt s}
        \le |Z_{2n,j}|\le
        C\frac{\sqrt n\,\Psi_s}{\sqrt s},\qquad j\in\mathcal J_1.
\end{equation*}
Its entropy also satisfies $\log(e|\mathcal J_1|/s)\ge cL$.

Every support constructed inside either pool has $C_{SS}=I$.  The
constant-weight construction in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:score-packing-lower}, followed by
Corollary~\ref{cor:embedded-score-packing}, therefore gives respectively
\begin{align*}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        &\ge c\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        -C\left(
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3\right),\\
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\hat\beta_{0,S}-\hat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        &\ge c\frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        -C\left(
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3\right).
\end{align*}
The corresponding condition and a sufficiently small $\delta_0$ absorb the
remainders and give \eqref{eq:gaussian-experimental-matching-lower}.
\end{proof}

The two experimental scales arise from two score subsets.  If
$L_N\asymp L$ and $b_n\asymp\sigma_r\sqrt L$, the leading term in
\eqref{eq:score-packing-lower} is
$(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^2$.  If instead
$b_n\asymp\sqrt n\,\Psi_s/\sqrt s$, it is
$(\Psi_s/\sigma_0)\delta$.  The second case requires the signal energy
to occur on many coordinates; the scalar $\Psi_s$ does not imply this
multiplicity.

Cardinality alone does not control either metric.  For example, take
$s=1$, $C=I$, $d=0$, and $h=\mu e_1$.  Then
$\widetilde\Psi_1=\mu$, but only one support carries signal.  Its linear
contribution is of order $\mu/\sqrt n$, not
$\mu\sqrt{\log p/n}$; the remaining coordinates contribute only the
bilinear noise maximum.  A scalar signal lower bound of order
$\widetilde\Psi_s\delta$ therefore requires a packing or multiplicity
condition.
The matched field records that condition through its covariance instead
of imposing it implicitly.

The cardinality of a near-optimal family can be equally misleading.  Let
$C=I$, $y=X_1$, and $x=-aX_1+u$, where
$u\sim N(0,1-a^2)$ is independent of $X$.  Every support containing
coordinate $1$ is population-optimal and gives
$\hat\beta_{0,S}=0$ exactly.  With probability tending to one, the
supports excluding it remain below zero, so the anchored searched
increment is zero although the optimal family has cardinality
$\binom{p-1}{s-1}$.  Its increment metric is zero, as the lower
reduction requires.

\section{Studentized search}

Studentization is another smooth function of an augmented second-moment
matrix.  Let
\begin{equation*}
        v_y^{(i)}=
        \begin{pmatrix}x^{(i)}\\y^{(i)}\\X^{(i)}\end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        \Gamma_y=\E[v_y^{(i)}(v_y^{(i)})^\top],
        \qquad
        \hat\Gamma_y=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        v_y^{(i)}(v_y^{(i)})^\top.
\end{equation*}
For a second-moment matrix indexed by $(x,y,X)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-functional}
        \tau_S(A)
        =\frac{A_{xy\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        {\{A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}A_{yy\mathbin{\cdot}S}\}^{1/2}}.
\end{equation}
For $|S|=s$, define the scaled partial correlation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-moment-identity}
        \hat z_{0,S}
        =\sqrt{n-s-1}\,\tau_S(\hat\Gamma_y).
\end{equation}
The conventional least-squares statistic uses the empirical control
rank:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conventional-t-definition}
\begin{split}
        \hat t_{0,S}
        &=\sqrt{n-\operatorname{rank}(X_S)-1}\,
        \frac{\tau_S(\hat\Gamma_y)}
        {\{1-\tau_S(\hat\Gamma_y)^2\}^{1/2}}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus $\hat t_{0,S}
=\hat z_{0,S}/\{1-\tau_S(\hat\Gamma_y)^2\}^{1/2}$
whenever $\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=s$.
For the baseline support,
\begin{equation*}
        \hat z_{0,\emptyset}
        =\sqrt{n-1}\,\tau_\emptyset(\hat\Gamma_y),
        \qquad
        \hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        =\frac{\hat z_{0,\emptyset}}
        {\{1-\tau_\emptyset(\hat\Gamma_y)^2\}^{1/2}}.
\end{equation*}
Write $\mathfrak S_s=\{S\subseteq[p]:|S|=s\}$.  For a nonempty family
$\mathcal P\subseteq\mathfrak S_s$, put
\begin{equation*}
        r_{t,n}(\mathcal P)=\max\left\{
        |\tau_\emptyset(\hat\Gamma_y)|,\,
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}|\tau_S(\hat\Gamma_y)|\right\}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[Transfer to the conventional statistic]
\label{lem:conventional-t-transfer}
Let $\mathcal P$ be a nonempty family of size-$s$ supports.  On the
event that $\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=s$ for every $S\in\mathcal P$ and
$r_{t,n}(\mathcal P)\le r_0<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conventional-t-transfer}
\left|
\left\{\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\hat t_{0,S}
      -\hat t_{0,\emptyset}\right\}
-\left\{\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\hat z_{0,S}
      -\hat z_{0,\emptyset}\right\}
\right|
\le C_{r_0}\sqrt n\,r_{t,n}(\mathcal P)^3.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $|u|\le r_0$,
\begin{equation*}
        \left|\frac{u}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}-u\right|
        \le C_{r_0}|u|^3.
\end{equation*}
Apply this inequality to every searched support and to the baseline,
then use $|\max_S a_S-\max_S b_S|\le\max_S|a_S-b_S|$.
\end{proof}

Let $G_y$ be the covariance-matched Gaussian moment matrix for $v_y$, and
put $\Gamma_y^G=\Gamma_y+G_y/\sqrt n$.  Assume that the treatment and
outcome Schur complements are bounded below uniformly over $|S|\le s$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-floors}
\begin{split}
        (\Gamma_y)_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        &\ge c_x\Var(x),\\
        (\Gamma_y)_{yy\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        &\ge c_y\Var(y).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
When $\tau_S$ or its smooth transform is evaluated at $\Gamma_y^G$, it
denotes the global extension obtained from the reciprocal and spectral
cutoffs of Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-localization}.  This extension agrees
with \eqref{eq:t-functional} on the sparse neighborhood of $\Gamma_y$.
Let $|H|_{y,s}$ denote \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} with $r$ replaced by
$y$ and with $|H_{yy}|/\Var(y)$ included.  Define the anchored functional
and its first-derivative envelope by
\begin{align*}
        c_{s,n}&=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-s-1}},
        \qquad
        \Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)=\tau_S(A)-c_{s,n}\tau_\emptyset(A),\\
        \alpha_{t,s}
        & =\max_{|S|=s}\sup_{|H|_{y,s}\le1}
        |D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H]|,\\
        a_{t,n}&=\alpha_{t,s}\delta+\delta^2.
\end{align*}

\begin{corollary}[Critical studentized expansion]
\label{cor:critical-studentized}
Suppose the relative sub-Gaussian condition of
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds for $v_y$, the floors in
\eqref{eq:t-floors} hold, and $\delta\le\delta_0$.  Put
$H_{y,n}=\hat\Gamma_y-\Gamma_y$.  With probability at least
$1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{align}
\sup_{|S|=s}\big|
        \Delta\tau_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
        -\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)
        -D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H_{y,n}]
        \big|
&\le C\delta^2,
\label{eq:critical-studentized-first}\\
\sup_{|S|=s}\big|
        \Delta\tau_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
        -\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)
        -D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H_{y,n}]\notag\\
        \hspace{20mm}
        -\tfrac12D^2\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H_{y,n},H_{y,n}]
        \big|
&\le C\delta^3.
\label{eq:critical-studentized-second}
\end{align}
In particular, if
$\Delta\tau_*=\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-studentized-upper}
        \max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
        \le\Delta\tau_*+C\{\alpha_{t,s}\delta+\delta^2\}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Support compression and the sparse moment tail give
$|H_{y,n}|_{y,s}\le C\delta$ with the stated probability.  The two
Schur-complement floors keep every reciprocal derivative bounded on this
neighborhood.  The inverse expansion used in
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} therefore gives
\eqref{eq:critical-studentized-first} and
\eqref{eq:critical-studentized-second}.  The definition of
$\alpha_{t,s}$ bounds the linear term by $\alpha_{t,s}\delta$, which
proves \eqref{eq:critical-studentized-upper}.
\end{proof}

The same expansion gives the studentized lower reduction without a new
probabilistic argument.  Define
\begin{equation*}
        \mathcal T_{n,S}
        =D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H_{y,n}]
        +\frac12D^2\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H_{y,n},H_{y,n}],
\end{equation*}
fix $S_t^\star\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}
\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)$, and put
\begin{equation*}
        \mathcal N_{t,\epsilon}
        =\{S:|S|=s,\quad
        \Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)
        \ge\Delta\tau_{S_t^\star,n}(\Gamma_y)-\epsilon\}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{corollary}[Critical studentized lower reduction]
\label{cor:critical-studentized-lower}
Under the conditions of Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-studentized}, with
probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$, simultaneously for every
$\epsilon\ge0$ and every nonempty
$\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal N_{t,\epsilon}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-studentized-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat z_{0,S}-\hat z_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge\sqrt{n-s-1}\bigg\{&
        \Delta\tau_{S_t^\star,n}(\Gamma_y)
        +\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\mathcal T_{n,S}\\
        &-\epsilon-C\delta^3\bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The anchored form is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:critical-studentized-anchored-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat z_{0,S}-\hat z_{0,S_t^\star}
        \ge\sqrt{n-s-1}\bigg\{&
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}
        (\mathcal T_{n,S}-\mathcal T_{n,S_t^\star})\\
        &-\epsilon-C\delta^3\bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:critical-studentized-second} gives
$\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
=\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)+\mathcal T_{n,S}+R_{n,S}$ uniformly, with
$\max_S|R_{n,S}|\le C\delta^3$.  The definition of
$\mathcal N_{t,\epsilon}$, together with
\eqref{eq:studentized-moment-identity} and the definition of $c_{s,n}$,
proves \eqref{eq:critical-studentized-lower}.  Subtracting the expansion
at $S_t^\star$ proves
\eqref{eq:critical-studentized-anchored-lower}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Studentized moment approximation]
\label{cor:studentized-moments}
Suppose the relative sub-Gaussian condition
of Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds for $v_y$, the floors in
\eqref{eq:t-floors} hold, and $m^7=o(n)$.  If
$\Delta\tau_*=\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-gaussian-field}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
        -\Delta\tau_*}{a_{t,n}},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y^G)
        -\Delta\tau_*}{a_{t,n}}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:studentized-gaussian-field} approximates the anchored
maximum of the scaled partial correlations.

For the conventional statistic, suppose in addition that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-partial-correlation-floor}
\begin{gathered}
        \inf_{|S|\le s}\{1-\tau_S(\Gamma_y)^2\}\ge c_\tau,\\
        \operatorname{rank}(C_{SS})=|S|,
        \qquad |S|\le s.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Define
\begin{equation*}
        \vartheta_S(A)
        =\frac{\tau_S(A)}{\{1-\tau_S(A)^2\}^{1/2}},
        \qquad
        \Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(A)
        =\vartheta_S(A)-c_{s,n}\vartheta_\emptyset(A),
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
        \alpha_{\vartheta,s}
        =\max_{|S|=s}\sup_{|H|_{y,s}\le1}
        |D\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H]|,
        \qquad
        a_{\vartheta,n}=\alpha_{\vartheta,s}\delta+\delta^2,
\end{equation*}
and
$\Delta\vartheta_*=\max_{|S|=s}
\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conventional-t-gaussian-field}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y)
        -\Delta\vartheta_*}{a_{\vartheta,n}},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(\Gamma_y^G)
        -\Delta\vartheta_*}{a_{\vartheta,n}}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Moreover, on the relative restricted-isometry event,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conventional-t-moment-identity}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        =\sqrt{n-s-1}
        \max_{|S|=s}\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}(\hat\Gamma_y).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the two Schur moments in \eqref{eq:t-functional} and
using \eqref{eq:t-floors} gives
\begin{align*}
        |D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)[H]|&\lesssim
        \{\alpha_{t,s}+|A-\Gamma_y|_{y,s}\}|H|_{y,s},\\
        |D^j\Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
        &\lesssim\prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_{y,s},
        \qquad j=2,3,
\end{align*}
on a fixed sparse neighborhood.  Smooth reciprocal and spectral cutoffs
give a localized global version.  The hybrid-moment, leave-one-row, and
smooth-maximum calculation in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments} applies
with $a_{t,n}$ in place of $a_n$ and proves
\eqref{eq:studentized-gaussian-field}.

Under \eqref{eq:population-partial-correlation-floor}, the map
$u\mapsto u/\sqrt{1-u^2}$ and its first three derivatives are bounded on
the same neighborhood.  The identical calculation with
$\Delta\vartheta_{S,n}$ proves
\eqref{eq:conventional-t-gaussian-field}.  The definitions of
$c_{s,n}$ and $\vartheta_S$ give
\eqref{eq:conventional-t-moment-identity}.
\end{proof}

The lower packing argument has subcritical canonical distance
\begin{equation*}
        d_{t,G}(S,T)^2
        =\frac1n\E\left[
        D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[G_y]
        -D\Delta\tau_{T,n}(\Gamma_y)[G_y]
        \right]^2.
\end{equation*}
A packing whose Gaussian width dominates the quadratic scale controls
the anchored increment through the same smooth transfer used for the
coefficient field.  The next proposition states the corresponding
critical affine-slice condition without discarding the empty-support
anchor.  When the first derivative degenerates, the full
linear--quadratic field $\mathcal T_{n,S}$ remains the lower object.

\begin{proposition}[Anchored studentized packing]
\label{prop:anchored-studentized-packing}
Suppose the conditions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-studentized-lower} hold.  Let
$\mathcal F$ be a sigma-field and let
$\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal N_{t,\epsilon}$ be an
$\mathcal F$-measurable family of fixed cardinality $M\ge2$.  Suppose
that, on an $\mathcal F$-measurable event of probability at least
$1-\pi_0$, there are $\mathcal F$-measurable
$\lambda_n>0$ and $r_n\ge0$ and a fixed $K\ge1$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-affine-slice}
        \mathcal T_{n,S}=g_{n,S}^\top\xi_n+e_{n,S},
        \qquad S\in\mathcal P,
\end{equation}
where the vectors $g_{n,S}$ and residuals $e_{n,S}$ are
$\mathcal F$-measurable and, conditionally on $\mathcal F$, $\xi_n$
satisfies \eqref{eq:multiplier-minoration} with $\sigma_0=1$ for every
finite family of coefficient vectors, with constants uniform in the
realization of $\mathcal F$.  Suppose also that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-affine-packing}
\begin{split}
        \min_{\substack{S,T\in\mathcal P\\S\ne T}}
        \norm{g_{n,S}-g_{n,T}}_2&\ge\lambda_n,\\
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{g_{n,S}}_2&\le K\lambda_n,
        \qquad
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}|e_{n,S}|\le r_n.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:anchored-studentized-packing}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat z_{0,S}-\hat z_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge\sqrt{n-s-1}\bigg\{&
        \Delta\tau_{S_t^\star,n}(\Gamma_y)
        +c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}\\
        &-\epsilon-r_n-C\delta^3\bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vectors $g_{n,S}$ already represent the empty-anchored functional
$\Delta\tau_{S,n}$.  Conditional multiplier minoration and
\eqref{eq:studentized-affine-packing} give
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}g_{n,S}^\top\xi_n
        \ge c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}
\end{equation*}
except with conditional probability $Ce^{-c\log M}$.
Substitution in \eqref{eq:critical-studentized-lower} proves the result.
\end{proof}

Suppose $\beta_0=0$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-testing-condition}
        \max_{|S|\le s}|\tau_S(\Gamma_y)|\le K_\tau n^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Write $\theta_s=\Psi_s^2/\Var(y)$.  Then
$\Delta\tau_{S_t^\star,n}(\Gamma_y)\ge-C/\sqrt n$.  Hence
Proposition~\ref{prop:anchored-studentized-packing} gives the general
lower scale
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-studentized-lower-scale}
        c\sqrt m\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta\}-C
\end{equation}
whenever $\log M\ge cm$,
\begin{equation*}
        \lambda_n\ge
        \frac{c}{\sqrt n}
        \{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta\},
        \qquad
        \epsilon+r_n\le
        c\delta\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta\},
\end{equation*}
with constants chosen so that the packing term dominates the losses.
If $\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=s$ for every $S\in\mathcal P$, then
sparse moment concentration, the Schur-complement floors, and
\eqref{eq:local-testing-condition} give
$r_{t,n}(\mathcal P)\le C\delta$ on the same sparse-moment event.
Lemma~\ref{lem:conventional-t-transfer} costs at most
$C\sqrt m\,\delta^2$, so \eqref{eq:general-studentized-lower-scale}
also holds for the conventional statistic after adjusting constants.
The condition retains the treatment channel rather than absorbing it
into an unspecified anchor.

In a randomized experiment the full second-order field has a more
concrete conditional representation.  Put $P_S=I-M_S$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-directions}
\begin{gathered}
        u_{n,S}=\frac{M_Sy}{\norm{M_Sy}_2},
        \qquad
        u_{n,\emptyset}=\frac{y}{\norm{y}_2},\\
        \nu_{n,s}=\sqrt{\frac{n-s-1}{n-s}},
        \qquad
        \nu_{n,0}=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n}},
        \qquad
        u_{n,S}^{\Delta}=\nu_{n,s}u_{n,S}
        -\nu_{n,0}u_{n,\emptyset}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Experimental studentized packing]
\label{thm:experimental-studentized-packing}
Suppose $\beta_0=0$, $x$ is independent of
$\mathcal F=\sigma(X,y)$, and the entries of $x$ are independent,
centered, uniformly sub-Gaussian with variance $\sigma_0^2$ and uniform
exponential multiplier minoration.  Suppose that, outside an
$\mathcal F$-measurable event of probability $\pi_0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-outcome-floor}
        \inf_{|S|=s}
        \frac{\norm{M_Sy}_2^2}{\norm{y}_2^2}\ge c_y.
\end{equation}
Let $\mathcal P$ be an $\mathcal F$-measurable family of fixed
cardinality $M\ge2$.  Suppose that, on the same event,
$\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=s$ for every $S\in\mathcal P$, and there are an
$\mathcal F$-measurable $\lambda_n>0$ and a fixed $K\ge1$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-direction-packing}
        \min_{\substack{S,T\in\mathcal P\\S\ne T}}
        \norm{u_{n,S}^{\Delta}-u_{n,T}^{\Delta}}_2\ge\lambda_n,
        \qquad
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{u_{n,S}^{\Delta}}_2\le K\lambda_n.
\end{equation}
If $\delta\le\delta_0$, then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-c\log M}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:experimental-studentized-packing-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge c\lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}
        -C\sqrt m\,\delta^2.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put
\begin{equation*}
        D_{n,S}
        =\frac{x^\top M_Sx/(n-s)}{x^\top x/n}-1.
\end{equation*}
The partial-correlation identity gives exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-studentized-direction}
\begin{split}
        \hat z_{0,S}
        &=\nu_{n,s}\frac{x^\top u_{n,S}}{\hat\sigma_0}
        (1+D_{n,S})^{-1/2},\\
        \hat z_{0,\emptyset}
        &=\nu_{n,0}\frac{x^\top u_{n,\emptyset}}
        {\hat\sigma_0}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Conditional quadratic and linear-form bounds, followed by a union over
the supports, give, outside an event of probability $Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation*}
        \hat\sigma_0\asymp\sigma_0,\qquad
        \max_{|S|=s}x^\top P_Sx\le C\sigma_0^2m,\qquad
        \max_{|S|=s}|x^\top u_{n,S}|
        \le C\sigma_0\sqrt m.
\end{equation*}
Since
\begin{equation*}
        D_{n,S}
        =\frac{s\norm{x}_2^2-nx^\top P_Sx}
        {(n-s)\norm{x}_2^2},
\end{equation*}
the same event has
$\max_S|D_{n,S}|\le C\delta^2$.  Expanding the last factor in
\eqref{eq:exact-studentized-direction} therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-direction-remainder}
        \max_{|S|=s}\left|
        \hat z_{0,S}-\hat z_{0,\emptyset}
        -\frac{x^\top u_{n,S}^{\Delta}}{\hat\sigma_0}
        \right|
        \le C\sqrt m\,\delta^2.
\end{equation}
Conditional multiplier minoration applied to
$\{u_{n,S}^{\Delta}/\sigma_0:S\in\mathcal P\}$, together with
$\hat\sigma_0\le C\sigma_0$, gives the claimed lower bound for
$\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\hat z_{0,S}
-\hat z_{0,\emptyset}$.  The preceding
linear-form bound and \eqref{eq:exact-studentized-direction} also give
$r_{t,n}(\mathcal P)\le C\delta$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:conventional-t-transfer} changes
the anchored maximum by at most
$C\sqrt n\,\delta^3=C\sqrt m\,\delta^2$ and proves
\eqref{eq:experimental-studentized-packing-lower}.
\end{proof}

If the relative sub-Gaussian condition holds for $v_y$ and the
population outcome floor in \eqref{eq:t-floors} holds, sparse moment
concentration gives \eqref{eq:empirical-outcome-floor} with
$\pi_0\le Ce^{-cm}$.  Thus the empirical premises of
Theorem~\ref{thm:experimental-studentized-packing} follow from the same
moment, isotropy, and outcome-floor conditions used above.

The direction metric is an observable score geometry.  Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-embedded-score}
        z_n=\frac{X^\top y}{\sqrt n},
        \qquad
        q_{n,S}^y=\iota_S\hat C_{SS}^{\dagger}z_{n,S}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-projection-geometry}
\begin{gathered}
        P_Sy=\frac{Xq_{n,S}^y}{\sqrt n},\\
        \norm{P_Sy-P_Ty}_2
        =\norm{q_{n,S}^y-q_{n,T}^y}_{\hat C},
        \qquad
        \norm{P_Sy}_2=\norm{q_{n,S}^y}_{\hat C}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Embedded studentized packing]
\label{cor:embedded-studentized-packing}
Suppose the sampling and outcome-floor conditions of
Theorem~\ref{thm:experimental-studentized-packing} hold.  Let
$\mathcal P$ be an $\mathcal F$-measurable family of fixed cardinality
$M\ge2$.  Suppose that, on the event in
\eqref{eq:empirical-outcome-floor}, $\operatorname{rank}(X_S)=s$ for every
$S\in\mathcal P$ and an $\mathcal F$-measurable
$\Lambda_n>0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-studentized-geometry}
\begin{split}
        \min_{\substack{S,T\in\mathcal P\\S\ne T}}
        \norm{q_{n,S}^y-q_{n,T}^y}_{\hat C}
        &\ge\Lambda_n,\\
        \max_{S\in\mathcal P}\norm{q_{n,S}^y}_{\hat C}
        &\le K\Lambda_n,
        \qquad
        \frac{\Lambda_n}{\norm{y}_2}\ge C\frac{s}{n^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Then, with the same probability as in that theorem,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:embedded-studentized-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge c\frac{\Lambda_n\sqrt{\log M}}{\norm{y}_2}
        -C\sqrt m\,\delta^2.
\end{equation}
In particular, if $\log M\ge cm$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-matching-packing}
        \frac{\Lambda_n}{\norm{y}_2}
        \ge c\{\theta_s+\delta^2\}^{1/2},
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge c\sqrt m\,\{\theta_s+\delta^2\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $u_0=y/\norm{y}_2$ and $v_S=P_Sy/\norm{y}_2$.  Orthogonality gives
\begin{equation*}
        u_{n,S}
        =\frac{u_0-v_S}{\sqrt{1-\norm{v_S}_2^2}},
        \qquad
        v_S=u_0-\langle u_0,u_{n,S}\rangle u_{n,S}.
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{u_{n,S}-u_{n,T}}_2
        \ge\frac12\norm{v_S-v_T}_2,
        \qquad
        \norm{u_{n,S}-u_0}_2
        \le C_{c_y}\norm{v_S}_2.
\end{equation*}
Equations~\eqref{eq:studentized-projection-geometry} and
\eqref{eq:embedded-studentized-geometry} therefore imply
\eqref{eq:studentized-direction-packing} with
$\lambda_n=c\Lambda_n/\norm{y}_2$.  Here
$|\nu_{n,s}-\nu_{n,0}|\lesssim s/n^2$ is absorbed by the
last condition in \eqref{eq:embedded-studentized-geometry}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:experimental-studentized-packing} proves
\eqref{eq:embedded-studentized-lower}; the final assertion follows by
absorbing its $O(\sqrt m\,\delta^2)$ remainder.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Studentized score packing]
\label{cor:studentized-score-packing}
Suppose $\beta_0=0$, $x$ is independent of
$\mathcal F=\sigma(X,y)$, and the entries of $x$ are independent,
centered, uniformly sub-Gaussian with variance $\sigma_0^2$ and uniform
exponential multiplier minoration.  Fix $K_0\ge1$ and a deterministic
$N\ge2s$.  Suppose that, on one $\mathcal F$-measurable event of
probability at least $1-\pi_0$,
\eqref{eq:empirical-outcome-floor} holds,
\begin{equation*}
        \hat\rho_s
        =\sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \norm{\hat C_{JJ}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \le c_0/K_0
\end{equation*}
and there are an $\mathcal F$-measurable $b_n>0$ and an
$\mathcal F$-measurable $\mathcal J\subseteq[p]$ with
$|\mathcal J|\ge N$ such that
\begin{equation*}
        b_n\le |z_{n,j}|\le K_0b_n,\qquad j\in\mathcal J,
        \qquad
        \frac{b_n\sqrt s}{\norm{y}_2}\ge C\frac{s}{n^2},
\end{equation*}
then, with probability at least
$1-\pi_0-Ce^{-csL_N}-Ce^{-cm}$, where $L_N=\log(eN/s)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-score-packing-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge c\frac{b_ns\sqrt{L_N}}{\norm{y}_2}
        -C\sqrt m\,\delta^2.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The constant-weight construction and the argument in
Corollary~\ref{cor:score-packing-lower}, now applied to $\hat C$,
give a family of cardinality at least $\exp(csL_N)$ with
\begin{equation*}
        \min_{S\ne T}
        \norm{q_{n,S}^y-q_{n,T}^y}_{\hat C}
        \ge cb_n\sqrt s,
        \qquad
        \max_S\norm{q_{n,S}^y}_{\hat C}
        \le Cb_n\sqrt s.
\end{equation*}
Corollary~\ref{cor:embedded-studentized-packing} proves the claim.
\end{proof}

When $\norm{y}_2\asymp\sqrt n\,\sigma_y$, where
$\sigma_y^2=\Var(y)$, and $L_N\asymp L$, the choices
$b_n\asymp\sigma_y\sqrt L$ and
$b_n\asymp\sqrt n\,\Psi_s/\sqrt s$ give respectively
$\sqrt m\,\delta$ and $\sqrt m\,\sqrt{\theta_s}$.  Thus the same
observable top-$s$ construction recovers both studentized
regimes whenever the corresponding score multiplicity is present.

\begin{corollary}[Gaussian experimental studentized bound]
\label{cor:gaussian-experimental-studentized}
Suppose $\beta_0=0$, $x^{(i)}\sim N(0,\sigma_0^2)$ is independent of
$(X,y)$, and $(X^{(i)},y^{(i)})$ is jointly Gaussian.  Suppose the
population outcome floor in \eqref{eq:t-floors} holds and
$\delta\le\delta_0$.  Fix constants $c_L,a,A>0$, take $C_0,K$
sufficiently large, and take $\epsilon_0$ sufficiently small.  Suppose
one of the following conditions holds, where
$g=\Cov(X^{(i)},y^{(i)})$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If $\Psi_s\le K\sigma_y\delta$, there is a deterministic
      $\mathcal A_0\subseteq[p]$ such that, with
      $P_0=|\mathcal A_0|$ and $L_0=\log(eP_0/s)$,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-gaussian-noise-pool}
              P_0\ge C_0s,\qquad C_{\mathcal A_0\mathcal A_0}=I,
              \qquad L_0\ge c_LL,\qquad
              \max_{j\in\mathcal A_0}\sqrt n\,|g_j|
              \le\epsilon_0\sigma_y\sqrt{L_0}.
      \end{equation}
\item If $\Psi_s>K\sigma_y\delta$, there is a deterministic
      $\mathcal A_1\subseteq[p]$ such that, with
      $P_1=|\mathcal A_1|$ and $L_1=\log(eP_1/s)$,
      \begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-gaussian-signal-pool}
      \begin{gathered}
              P_1\ge C_0s,\qquad C_{\mathcal A_1\mathcal A_1}=I,
              \qquad L_1\ge c_LL,\\
              a\frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt s}
              \le |g_j|\le A\frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt s},
              \qquad j\in\mathcal A_1.
      \end{gathered}
      \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Then, with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-experimental-studentized-lower}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \ge c\sqrt m\,\{\theta_s+\delta^2\}^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Sparse moment concentration turns the population outcome floor into
\eqref{eq:empirical-outcome-floor}.  On either identity-covariance pool,
relative RIP gives the required empirical local isotropy.
Lemma~\ref{lem:conditional-gaussian-outcome-scores} and the two count
arguments in Corollary~\ref{cor:gaussian-experimental-recovery} produce,
with failure probability $Ce^{-cm}$, a score subset with
$L_N\asymp L$ and respectively
\begin{equation*}
        b_n\asymp\sigma_y\sqrt L,
        \qquad\text{or}\qquad
        b_n\asymp\frac{\sqrt n\,\Psi_s}{\sqrt s}.
\end{equation*}
The proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:studentized-score-packing}, restricted
to that pool, gives $\sqrt m\,\delta$ in the first regime and
$\sqrt m\,\sqrt{\theta_s}$ in the second.  Its
$O(\sqrt m\,\delta^2)$ remainder is absorbed for sufficiently small
$\delta_0$.
\end{proof}

To connect the upper bound with the testing regime, retain the null and
the local testing condition \eqref{eq:local-testing-condition}, and
suppose $\operatorname{rank}(C_{SS})=s$ for every
$S\in\mathfrak S_s$.
Indeed, put $g=\Cov(X^{(i)},y^{(i)})=C\beta$ and
$K_S=C_{SS}^{-1}$.  The derivative of the numerator Schur moment is
\begin{equation*}
        H_{xy}-H_{xS}K_Sg_S-d_S^\top K_SH_{Sy}
        +d_S^\top K_SH_{SS}K_Sg_S.
\end{equation*}
The analogous denominator derivatives, the two floors, and the local
population-correlation bound therefore give
\begin{equation*}
        \alpha_{t,s}\lesssim
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt{\Var(y)}}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta.
\end{equation*}
Corollary~\ref{cor:critical-studentized} and the local
population-correlation bound first give the following display with
$\hat z$ in place of $\hat t$, as well as
$r_{t,n}(\mathfrak S_s)\le C\delta$,
with probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:conventional-t-transfer} adds only
$C\sqrt m\,\delta^2$, which is absorbed in the displayed right side.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-studentized-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \le C\left[
        \sqrt m\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta\}+1\right].
\end{equation}
Under the sparse-treatment condition \eqref{eq:local-treatment}, the
treatment term is absorbed by the quadratic scale, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}\hat t_{0,S}-\hat t_{0,\emptyset}
        \le C\left[
        \sqrt m\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\delta\}+1\right].
\end{equation}

The treatment term in \eqref{eq:general-studentized-upper-scale} is
necessary.  For example, let $p$ be even, $s=p/2$, $n=p^2$, and take
$C=I_p$, $d_j=a/\sqrt p$ for a fixed $a\in(0,1)$.  Let
$X\sim N(0,I_p)$, $y\sim N(0,1)$, and $u\sim N(0,1-a^2)$ be independent,
and set $x=d^\top X+u$.  Every population partial correlation vanishes,
$\theta_s=0$, and overlap holds uniformly, but the top-$s$ treatment--outcome
score term moves the searched statistic by order $\sqrt p$.  The
treatment-free right side of \eqref{eq:studentized-upper-scale} would be
of constant order as $p\to\infty$.

The lower results therefore match this upper scale whenever
the full anchored studentized field has the separation in
\eqref{eq:studentized-affine-packing}.  Scalar $\theta_s$ alone cannot
provide that separation.

\section{Scope of the reduction}

With $B_*$ as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments}, put
\begin{equation*}
        M_n=\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\hat\Gamma),
        \qquad
        M_n^G=\max_{|S|=s}B_S^{\mathrm{loc}}(\Gamma+G/\sqrt n).
\end{equation*}
The support-compressed critical expansion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:critical-empirical-expansion} contains the observational
upper bound, including its $\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta$ term, and the empirical
lower reduction.  The same expansion gives the critical
studentized upper and lower reductions.

The distributional and computational reductions have distinct sample
size requirements:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-gaussian-reduction}
\begin{aligned}
 m^7=o(n)
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_3\!\left(\frac{M_n-B_*}{a_n},\frac{M_n^G-B_*}{a_n}\right)=o(1),\\
 m=o(n),\ \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 d_3\!\left(
 \frac{M_n}{a_n^{\mathrm{score}}},
 \frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{score}}}
 \right)=o(1),\\
 m=o(n),\ \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta,\ \rho_s(C)=o(1)
 &\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 M_n=\frac1{n\hat\sigma_0^2}
 \sum_{t=1}^s\{-Z_{1n,j}\hat Z_{2n,j}\}_{(t)}
 +o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{score}}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
When $m\to\infty$, sparse score isotropy and the mean cap further reduce
the last line of \eqref{eq:unified-gaussian-reduction} to independent
coordinates.  Gaussian controls give that law throughout $m=o(n)$.
At fixed $s$, their coefficient extremes require only
$(\log p)^2=o(n)$ under population scaling.  The sub-Gaussian
experimental null instead requires $(\log p)^3=o(n)$ because of the
cubic Cram\'er correction.  Neither restriction belongs to the matched
Gaussian score process itself.

The same two fields also carry the lower bounds.  At the critical scale,
the empirical linear--quadratic field is reduced by anchored packing.  In
the subcritical regime, conditioning on one matched score channel yields
the $C$-norm geometry of the other.  Relative RIP transfers that geometry
to the sample, and score multiplicity supplies explicit top-$s$ packings.
The studentized field uses the same expansion and retains the
$\sqrt{\phi_s}$ treatment channel.  Thus the core upper, lower, and
studentized claims require no additional approximation object.

The remaining limitations separate genuine geometry from approximation
technology.  Relative sub-Gaussianity alone does not imply exponential
multiplier minoration, signal multiplicity, conditional landscape
retention, or score anti-clustering.  In the experimental null,
treatment--control independence makes the two score channels
asymptotically covariance-matched, and
$\rho_s(C)\log p=o(1)$ supplies the anti-clustering used by the
fixed-budget limit.  Covariance control alone does not supply this bridge
for an arbitrary observational score law.  For arbitrary $C$, exact
maximization can remain combinatorial.  The forward path has a
deterministic certificate once a lower bound on $\mu_s(C)$ is supplied,
but first-order accuracy still requires a small realized product gap or
additional covariance structure, as in the block-diagonal result.
Searches not indexed by sparse principal blocks lie outside this moment
field.
