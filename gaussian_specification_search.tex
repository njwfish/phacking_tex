\title{A Unified Gaussian Moment Field for Specification Search}
\author{Th\'eo Voldoire and Nic Fishman}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Searching over sparse regression specifications is a search over Schur
complements of one empirical second-moment matrix.  Replacing the centered
matrix by a Gaussian matrix with the same covariance gives a single
stochastic approximation for experimental and observational designs.  Its
population value is the observational specification landscape, and its
linear and quadratic terms give the influence process and the matched
Gaussian chaos.  When the treatment score is local, a condition that
includes random assignment, this field becomes a linear--bilinear score
chaos that allows arbitrary outcome signal.  Under exact random assignment,
a vanishing outcome score leaves only the centered chaos.  Local isotropy
reduces the chaos to the top-$s$ sum of matched score products.  A
single deterministic marginal-increment bound gives a recursive search for
the general moment field and becomes conditional-product search after the
local-treatment reduction.  The same moment field yields coefficient
bounds and applies directly to the searched $t$-statistic.  Its
studentized derivative exposes the same treatment-score term, which local
population correlations alone do not remove.  Lower bounds are governed
by the metric geometry of the near-optimal specifications.
\end{abstract}

\section{The sparse moment field}

For independent and identically distributed observations
$i=1,\ldots,n$, consider the centered linear model
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model}
        y^{(i)}
        =\beta_0x^{(i)}+(X^{(i)})^\top\beta+\varepsilon^{(i)},
        \qquad X^{(i)}\in\R^p.
\end{equation}
To keep the moment formulas light, we abbreviate the focal regressor
$x_0$, the controls $X_{-0}$, and their coefficient vector $\beta_{-0}$
as $x$, $X$, and $\beta$.
The design is jointly sub-Gaussian relative to its covariance, and the
centered sub-Gaussian noise is independent of the design.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \beta_\emptyset^*=\frac{\E[x^{(i)}y^{(i)}]}{\E[(x^{(i)})^2]},
        \qquad
        r^{(i)}=y^{(i)}-\beta_\emptyset^*x^{(i)}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\E[x^{(i)}r^{(i)}]=0$.  We write
\begin{equation*}
        \E[(x^{(i)})^2]=\sigma_0^2,
        \qquad
        \E[(r^{(i)})^2]=\sigma_r^2,
        \qquad
        \E[X^{(i)}(X^{(i)})^\top]=C,
\end{equation*}
and assume $\sigma_0\sigma_r>0$.  Rescaling the controls changes none of
their selected spans, so $C_{jj}=1$ throughout.

The augmented row and its second-moment matrix are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment-matrix}
        v^{(i)}=
        \begin{pmatrix}x^{(i)}\\ r^{(i)}\\ X^{(i)}\end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        \Gamma=\E[v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top]
        =\begin{pmatrix}
        \sigma_0^2&0&d^\top\\
        0&\sigma_r^2&h^\top\\
        d&h&C
        \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
where $d=\E[X^{(i)}x^{(i)}]$ and $h=\E[X^{(i)}r^{(i)}]$.  Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-moment-matrix}
        \widehat\Gamma
        =\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top.
\end{equation}

For a symmetric matrix $A$, two indices $u,v\in\{x,r\}$, and a control
set $S$, define the residual moment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual-moment}
        A_{uv\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        =A_{uv}-A_{uS}A_{SS}^{-1}A_{Sv}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient contrast associated with $S$ is the function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-functional}
        B_S(A)
        =\frac{A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}}{A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        -\frac{A_{xr}}{A_{xx}},
        \qquad B_\emptyset(A)=0.
\end{equation}
This function carries both the observational population landscape and the
stochastic search around it.  It is invariant under replacing $r$ by
$r+c x$ for any scalar $c$, because both ratios in
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} then increase by $c$.  The empirical
field can therefore be computed from $(x,y,X)$ even though the orthogonal
residual $r$ makes its population geometry clearer.

For $S\subseteq[p]$, let $M_S$ project orthogonally off the columns of
$X_S$, and write
\begin{equation*}
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}
        =\frac{x^\top M_Sy}{x^\top M_Sx}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[Exact moment representation]\label{lem:exact-moment}
For every support on which the displayed inverses exist,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-moment-representation}
        \widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\emptyset}
        =B_S(\widehat\Gamma).
\end{equation}
At the population matrix,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:population-landscape}
        B_S(\Gamma)
        =-\frac{d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}h_S}
        {\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $y=\beta_\emptyset^*x+r$, the coefficient on $x$ after controlling
for $X_S$ is $\beta_\emptyset^*$ plus the coefficient on $x$ in the
regression of $r$ on $(x,X_S)$.  The partitioned-regression formula makes
the latter coefficient
$\widehat\Gamma_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}/
\widehat\Gamma_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}$.  Subtracting the corresponding
coefficient with $S=\emptyset$ proves
\eqref{eq:exact-moment-representation}.  Substitution of
\eqref{eq:moment-matrix} into \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} proves
\eqref{eq:population-landscape}.
\end{proof}

If a selected control block is singular, the same statement holds with
the Moore--Penrose inverse, equivalently after redundant columns are
removed.  The exact moment field therefore depends only on selected
column spans.  Differentiable Gaussian approximation requires the
nonsingular sparse blocks imposed below.

The population residual treatment variance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:overlap}
        \eta(S)
        =\Gamma_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        =\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}d_S.
\end{equation}
\begin{assumption}[Sparse moment regime]\label{ass:sparse-moments}
The augmented row $v^{(i)}$ is sub-Gaussian relative to $\Gamma$, with a
uniformly bounded constant.  The block $C_{SS}$ is positive definite for
every $|S|\le2s$, and
\begin{equation*}
        \eta(S)\ge c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2
\end{equation*}
for a constant $c_{\mathrm{ov}}>0$.
\end{assumption}

Overlap and sparse nonsingularity are exactly the domain conditions of
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional}; relative sub-Gaussianity supplies
uniform moment concentration on that domain.
Under the model above, the relative sub-Gaussian condition on $v^{(i)}$
follows from the stated design and noise conditions.  Indeed,
\begin{equation*}
        r^{(i)}=(X^{(i)})^\top\beta+\varepsilon^{(i)}
        -\frac{d^\top\beta}{\sigma_0^2}x^{(i)},
\end{equation*}
so every linear form in $v^{(i)}$ is the sum of an independent design
form and a multiple of $\varepsilon^{(i)}$; its sub-Gaussian norm is
therefore bounded by a constant times its own standard deviation.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:search-scale}
        L=\log(ep/s),
        \qquad m=sL,
        \qquad \delta=\delta_{s,n,p}=\sqrt{m/n}.
\end{equation}
The regime $m=o(n)$ makes the empirical sparse blocks a vanishing relative
perturbation of their population values.  The stronger condition used for
the Gaussian replacement will be stated where it enters.

\section{Matched Gaussian moments}

The central result replaces the searched empirical moment field by the
same smooth functional of a covariance-matched Gaussian matrix.  The
empirical matrix in \eqref{eq:empirical-moment-matrix} is an average of
row outer products, so its matched Gaussian perturbation is a
centered symmetric matrix $G$ defined by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-moment-matrix}
        \Cov(G_{ab},G_{cd})
        =\Cov(v_a^{(i)}v_b^{(i)},v_c^{(i)}v_d^{(i)}),
        \qquad a,b,c,d\in\{x,r,1,\ldots,p\}.
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussianized-moment-matrix}
        \Gamma^G=\Gamma+\frac{G}{\sqrt n}.
\end{equation}
The Gaussian perturbation need not be positive semidefinite outside the
sparse neighborhood of $\Gamma$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-localization}
constructs smooth global versions of $B_S$ that agree with
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} on that neighborhood; the same notation
is used for the localized functions.  Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments}
and the moment bound below put both endpoint matrices in the unmodified
region with probability tending to one.
The covariance in \eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix} retains the fourth
moments of the original rows.  The Gaussian field
$\{B_S(\Gamma^G):|S|=s\}$ therefore preserves the population landscape,
the random treatment and outcome scores, the control Gram fluctuations,
and the support-dependent denominators.

For a symmetric perturbation $H$, define its relative sparse size by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-size}
\begin{split}
        \lvert H\rvert_s
        ={}&\frac{|H_{xx}|}{\sigma_0^2}
        +\frac{|H_{xr}|}{\sigma_0\sigma_r}\ +
        \sup_{|J|\le2s}\bigg\{
        \frac{\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{Jx}}}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{Jr}}}{\sigma_r}\\
        &\hspace{43mm}
        +\norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}H_{JJ}C_{JJ}^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Sparse hybrid moments]\label{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments}
Let $Y_i=v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$, and let $Y_i^G$ be independent
centered Gaussian matrices with the covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.  Uniformly over every deterministic
hybrid pattern $\widetilde Y_i\in\{Y_i,Y_i^G\}$ and each fixed
$1\le q\le6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-concentration}
        \left\|
        \left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde Y_i\right|_s
        \right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim\delta,
        \qquad sL=o(n).
\end{equation}
For $m+t\lesssim n$, the corresponding tail bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sparse-moment-tail}
\Prob\left(
\left|\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde Y_i\right|_s
>C\left\{\sqrt{\frac{m+t}{n}}+\frac{m+t}{n}\right\}
\right)
\le 2e^{-ct}.
\end{equation}
Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-row-moment}
        \max_i
        \left\|\lvert\widetilde Y_i\rvert_s\right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim sL.
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation*}
        \lvert\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma\rvert_s
        +\frac{\lvert G\rvert_s}{\sqrt n}
        =O_{\Prob}(\delta).
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed support and unit directions, every entry and quadratic form in
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} is a centered product of two standardized
sub-Gaussian linear forms and therefore has uniformly bounded
$\psi_1$ norm.  A $1/4$-net for each sparse sphere, followed by a union over
the supports, has logarithmic size $O(sL)$.  Bernstein's inequality on
this net gives \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail}; tail integration gives
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-concentration}.  Gaussian net directions have
sub-Gaussian tails with the same variance bound, so the same conclusions
hold for every deterministic hybrid pattern.  For one original row, the
control-block term is bounded by the squared norm of the largest $2s$
standardized coordinates, whose fixed $L_q$ norm is $O(sL)$.  The vector
terms are smaller by Cauchy--Schwarz.  For $Y_i^G$, a union over the same
Gaussian net gives an $O(\sqrt{sL})$ bound and hence the weaker stated
$O(sL)$ bound.  This proves \eqref{eq:one-row-moment}.
\end{proof}

The control-block component of \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} is the
relative restricted-isometry event
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-rip}
        \sup_{|J|\le2s}
        \norm{C_{JJ}^{-1/2}\widehat C_{JJ}C_{JJ}^{-1/2}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}
        \lesssim\delta,
        \qquad \widehat C=\frac{X^\top X}{n}.
\end{equation}
It is centered at the population covariance $C$.  The local-isotropy
condition used later instead compares $C$ with $I$; the former stabilizes
the empirical field, while the latter makes its search separable.

The next lemma records the deterministic part of the approximation.  Let
$DB_S(\Gamma)[H]$ and $D^2B_S(\Gamma)[H,H]$ denote the first two
derivatives of \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} along $H$.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform moment expansion]\label{lem:uniform-expansion}
There is a constant $c>0$, depending only on the overlap constant, such
that $|H|_s\le c$ implies
\begin{align}
\sup_{|S|\le s}
\left|B_S(\Gamma+H)-B_S(\Gamma)-DB_S(\Gamma)[H]\right|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}|H|_s^2,
\label{eq:first-order-remainder}\\
\sup_{|S|\le s}
\left|B_S(\Gamma+H)-B_S(\Gamma)-DB_S(\Gamma)[H]
-\frac12D^2B_S(\Gamma)[H,H]\right|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}|H|_s^3.
\label{eq:second-order-remainder}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $S$, write
$K_S=C_{SS}^{-1}$, $\alpha=K_Sd_S$, $\gamma=K_Sh_S$, and
$\eta=\sigma_0^2-d_S^\top\alpha$.  The inverse identity
\begin{equation*}
        (C_{SS}+H_{SS})^{-1}
        =K_S-K_SH_{SS}K_S+K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}K_S
        -K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}K_SH_{SS}(C_{SS}+H_{SS})^{-1}
\end{equation*}
expands both Schur complements in
\eqref{eq:coefficient-functional}.  The relative norm in
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} bounds every linear term by its population
standard deviation, while Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S}\le\sigma_0$ and
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S}\le\sigma_r$.  Overlap bounds the reciprocal
denominators by $(c_{\mathrm{ov}}\sigma_0^2)^{-1}$.  Expanding each scalar
reciprocal through second order and collecting terms of degree at least
two proves \eqref{eq:first-order-remainder}.  Retaining the quadratic
terms leaves products of degree at least three and proves
\eqref{eq:second-order-remainder}, uniformly in $S$.
\end{proof}

For $z\in\R^p$, define
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{z}_{C,k}
        =\sup_{|J|\le k}
        \left(z_J^\top C_{JJ}^{-1}z_J\right)^{1/2},
\end{equation*}
and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean-sizes}
        \phi_k=\frac{\norm{d}_{C,k}^2}{\sigma_0^2},
        \qquad
        \widetilde\Psi_k=\norm{h}_{C,k},
        \qquad
        a_n=\frac1{\sigma_0}
        \{(\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s)\delta
        +\sigma_r\delta^2\}.
\end{equation}
The first two terms in $a_n$ bound the treatment and outcome parts of the
linear field; the last is the size of the quadratic field when that
derivative degenerates.  The tilde distinguishes the residual score $h$
from the outcome score $C\beta$; they agree under random assignment.
Indeed, the model gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-translation}
\begin{split}
        h&=C\beta-\frac{d^\top\beta}{\sigma_0^2}d,
        \qquad
        \sigma_r^2
        =\beta^\top C\beta+\sigma_\varepsilon^2
        -\frac{(d^\top\beta)^2}{\sigma_0^2},\\
        \widetilde\Psi_k
        &\le \Psi_k
        +\frac{|d^\top\beta|}{\sigma_0^2}\norm{d}_{C,k},
        \qquad
        \Psi_k=\norm{C\beta}_{C,k}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Under random assignment, the residual-score quantities equal their
outcome-score counterparts.  Away from random assignment,
\eqref{eq:score-translation} records the exact alignment correction.

The same calculation gives the derivative bounds used in the Gaussian
replacement.

\begin{lemma}[Sparse derivatives]\label{lem:sparse-derivatives}
If $|A-\Gamma|_s\le c$ for a sufficiently small constant $c$, then for
$j=1,2,3$ and symmetric
directions $H_1,\ldots,H_j$,
\begin{align}
|DB_S(A)[H_1]|
&\lesssim \frac1{\sigma_0}
\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
+\sigma_r|A-\Gamma|_s\}|H_1|_s,
\label{eq:first-derivative-bound}\\
|D^jB_S(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
&\lesssim \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
\prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_s,
\qquad j=2,3.
\label{eq:higher-derivative-bound}
\end{align}
The constants are uniform over $|S|\le s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $K_A=A_{SS}^{-1}$, direct differentiation gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        D A_{uv\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]
        ={}&H_{uv}-H_{uS}K_AA_{Sv}-A_{uS}K_AH_{Sv}\\
        &+A_{uS}K_AH_{SS}K_AA_{Sv}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        DB_S(A)[H]
        ={}&\frac{D A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]}
        {A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        -\frac{A_{xr\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        D A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}[H]}
        {A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}^2}\\
        &-\frac{H_{xr}}{A_{xx}}
        +\frac{A_{xr}H_{xx}}{A_{xx}^2}.
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
At $A=\Gamma$, every noncancelling term contains one factor of either
$C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S$ or $C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S$.  Cauchy--Schwarz gives the
first two terms in \eqref{eq:first-derivative-bound}.  Every further
derivative uses
\begin{equation*}
        D(A_{SS}^{-1})[H]
        =-A_{SS}^{-1}H_{SS}A_{SS}^{-1}
\end{equation*}
and inserts another relative perturbation in one of these products.
Overlap bounds the reciprocal treatment Schur complement.  The second
derivative along the segment from $\Gamma$ to $A$ adds at most
$\sigma_r|A-\Gamma|_s$ to the first derivative.  The bounds
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S}\le\sigma_0$ and
$\norm{C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S}\le\sigma_r$ control every second- and
third-order term, proving \eqref{eq:higher-derivative-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Smooth localization]\label{lem:smooth-localization}
The functions $B_S$ admit three times continuously differentiable
extensions that agree with \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} when
$|A-\Gamma|_s\le c$ and satisfy, for every symmetric $A$ and $H$,
\begin{align*}
        |DB_S(A)[H]|
        &\lesssim\frac1{\sigma_0}
        \left\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
        +\sigma_r(|A-\Gamma|_s\wedge1)\right\}|H|_s,\\
        |D^jB_S(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
        &\lesssim\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_s,
        \qquad j=2,3.
\end{align*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
In population-standardized coordinates, first apply a smooth radial clip
to the full non-Gram moment tuple, including $A_{xx}-\sigma_0^2$,
$A_{xr}$, $A_{Sx}-d_S$, and $A_{Sr}-h_S$.  Replace each scalar reciprocal
by a smooth bounded function that equals $u\mapsto u^{-1}$ on the overlap
interval.  For a control block, use
\begin{equation*}
        C_{SS}^{-1/2}
        f(C_{SS}^{-1/2}A_{SS}C_{SS}^{-1/2})
        C_{SS}^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
where the fixed matrix function $f$ equals inversion near $I$ and has
bounded Fr\'echet derivatives through order three in operator norm.  Such
an $f$ follows from a smooth spectral cutoff on a fixed neighborhood of
the positive cone.  The chain rule and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-derivatives} give the displayed bounds.  All
thresholds are fixed by the overlap constant, so the constants are
uniform in $S$ and $p$.
\end{proof}

The first derivative has the regression influence form.  In a population
row, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-residuals}
\begin{aligned}
        \alpha_S&=C_{SS}^{-1}d_S,
        &u_S&=x-X_S^\top\alpha_S,\\
        \gamma_S&=C_{SS}^{-1}h_S,
        &e_S&=r-X_S^\top\gamma_S-B_S(\Gamma)u_S.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Then, for the centered rank-one direction
$v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-function}
        DB_S(\Gamma)
        [v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma]
        =\frac{u_S^{(i)}e_S^{(i)}}{\eta(S)}
        -\frac{x^{(i)}r^{(i)}}{\sigma_0^2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the observational first-order field is already a part of the same
moment expansion; no separate drift or denominator decomposition is
needed.

For real random variables $U,V$, let
\begin{equation*}
        d_3(U,V)
        =\sup_f|\E f(U)-\E f(V)|,
\end{equation*}
where the supremum is over three times continuously differentiable $f$
with $\max_{0\le j\le3}\norm{f^{(j)}}_\infty\le1$.

\begin{theorem}[Matched moment approximation]\label{thm:matched-moments}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and $m^7=o(n)$.  If
\begin{equation*}
        b_* = \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma),
\end{equation*}
then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-moment-approximation}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\widehat\Gamma)-b_*}{a_n},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)-b_*}{a_n}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
If $S^*\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$, then also
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centered-observational-field}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        &\frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\widehat\Gamma)-B_{S^*}(\widehat\Gamma)}{a_n},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)-B_{S^*}(\Gamma^G)}{a_n}
        \bigg)\\
        &\hspace{45mm}\lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $Y_i=v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma$, and replace the $Y_i$ one at a
time by independent centered Gaussian matrices with the covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.  The first two Taylor terms cancel at
each replacement.  Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} applies to every
deterministic partially replaced sum.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        g_S(A)=\frac{B_S(A)-b_*}{a_n}.
\end{equation*}
Since
$a_n\ge\sigma_0^{-1}\delta
\{\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}+\widetilde\Psi_s
+\sigma_r\delta\}$,
Lemma~\ref{lem:smooth-localization} gives, along a replacement path with
leave-one-row base $A_i$ and row direction $Y/n$,
\begin{align*}
        |Dg_S(A)[Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s}{\sqrt{mn}}\,\Lambda_i(Y),\\
        |D^2g_S(A)[Y/n,Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s^2}{mn},\\
        |D^3g_S(A)[Y/n,Y/n,Y/n]|
        &\lesssim\frac{|Y|_s^3}{mn^2}.
\end{align*}
Here
\begin{equation*}
        \Lambda_i(Y)
        =1+\frac{|A_i-\Gamma|_s\wedge1}{\delta}
        +\frac{|Y|_s}{n\delta}.
\end{equation*}
The base $A_i$ is independent of the replacement row; the hybrid-moment
lemma applies to it after inserting one deterministic zero term.  The $L_6$
bounds in Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments}, H\"older's inequality,
and $n\delta=\sqrt{mn}$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localized-row-moment}
        \E\{|Y|_s^3\Lambda_i(Y)^3\}\lesssim m^3.
\end{equation}

Smooth the maximum of the $g_S$ by
\begin{equation*}
        F_\lambda(A)
        =\frac1{\lambda m}
        \log\sum_{|S|=s}\exp\{\lambda m g_S(A)\}.
\end{equation*}
Because $\log\binom ps\le m$, this changes the maximum by at most
$1/\lambda$ at each endpoint, for a total smoothing cost at most
$2/\lambda$.  Softmax differentiation and the preceding display bound
the third derivative in one row direction by
\begin{equation*}
        C|Y|_s^3\Lambda_i(Y)^3\left\{
        \frac1{mn^2}
        +\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt m\,n^{3/2}}
        +\frac{\lambda^2\sqrt m}{n^{3/2}}
        \right\}.
\end{equation*}
For the choice of $\lambda$ below, $\lambda m\ge1$.  The terms produced
by composing with a test function in the definition of $d_3$, including
$(F_\lambda')^3$ and $F_\lambda'F_\lambda''$, are then absorbed by the
same display.  Taylor's formula, cancellation of the first two moments,
and \eqref{eq:localized-row-moment} therefore bound the sum of the
replacement errors by
\begin{equation*}
        C\left\{
        \frac{m^2}{n}
        +\frac{\lambda m^{5/2}}{\sqrt n}
        +\frac{\lambda^2m^{7/2}}{\sqrt n}
        \right\}.
\end{equation*}
Smooth localization makes this calculation global, so no simultaneous
good event over the $n$ hybrids is used.  At the two endpoints, the
localized and original statistics differ only if the relative sparse
distance exceeds $c$.  The $L_6$ endpoint bound makes the contribution to
$d_3$ at most $C\delta^6\le C\delta$.  Choosing
$\lambda=(\sqrt n/m^{7/2})^{1/3}$ proves
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-approximation}.  Subtracting one fixed support
changes neither the entropy nor the derivative bounds, which proves the
final statement.
\end{proof}

The seventh power in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments} comes from the
rank-one control Gram term, whose one-row sparse operator norm is of order
$m$.  The local-treatment reduction removes this term from the leading field
and permits a sharper score replacement.

\section{Degeneration to matched chaos}

The general field simplifies when the treatment has little population
projection on the controls.  For $a,b\in\R^p$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chaos-functional}
        Q_S(a,b)=-a_S^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b_S,
        \qquad
        T_C(a,b)=\max_{|S|=s}Q_S(a,b).
\end{equation}
The Gaussian score pair contained in $G$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:matched-scores}
        Z_1=\sqrt n\,d+G_{Xx},
        \qquad
        Z_2=\sqrt n\,h+G_{Xr}.
\end{equation}
Its covariance is matched to the two centered row scores
$X^{(i)}x^{(i)}-d$ and $X^{(i)}r^{(i)}-h$; it is the corresponding
subvector of the full moment covariance in
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.

\begin{theorem}[Local-treatment degeneration]\label{thm:chaos-degeneration}
Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment}
        \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta.
\end{equation}
No restriction is imposed on $h$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-chaos-degeneration}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)
        =\frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3
        \right).
\end{equation}
The empirical scores
\begin{equation*}
        Z_{1n}=\frac{X^\top x}{\sqrt n},
        \qquad
        Z_{2n}=\frac{X^\top r}{\sqrt n}
\end{equation*}
satisfy the same deterministic reduction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\widehat\Gamma)
        =\frac{T_C(Z_{1n},Z_{2n})}{n\sigma_0^2}
        +O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^3
        \right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a perturbation $H$, write
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
        u=H_{xx},\qquad w=H_{xr},\qquad
        a=d_S+H_{Sx},\qquad b=h_S+H_{Sr},\qquad K=H_{SS},\\
        \widetilde q=\sigma_0^2+u,
        \qquad \widetilde C=C_{SS}+K.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
Direct substitution into \eqref{eq:coefficient-functional} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-local-treatment-perturbation}
        B_S(\Gamma+H)
        =\frac{
        -a^\top\widetilde C^{-1}b
        +(w/\widetilde q)a^\top\widetilde C^{-1}a}
        {\widetilde q-a^\top\widetilde C^{-1}a}.
\end{equation}
The leading term is
$-a^\top C_{SS}^{-1}b/\sigma_0^2$.
The relative inverse bound and \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} bound the
uniform difference, on $|H|_s\le c$, by a constant times
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)
        \left\{\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_r}+|H|_s\right\}
        \left\{|H|_s+\left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)^2\right\}
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}
        \left(\sqrt{\phi_{2s}}+|H|_s\right)^2|H|_s.
\end{equation*}
Under \eqref{eq:local-treatment}, taking $H=G/\sqrt n$ and using
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-concentration} gives
\eqref{eq:gaussian-chaos-degeneration}.  Taking
$H=\widehat\Gamma-\Gamma$ gives
\eqref{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration}.
\end{proof}

Condition \eqref{eq:local-treatment} includes random assignment, where
$d=0$.  The natural envelope of the searched displacement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-scale}
        a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}
        =\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{equation}
The two summands bound the linear score process produced by $h$ and the
bilinear chaos.  The remainder in
Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration} is
$O_{\Prob}(\delta a_n^{\mathrm{loc}})$, so no upper bound on the signal
energy is needed for an approximation at this envelope when
$\delta\to0$.

The full moment theorem can be used after this reduction, but replacing
the two score channels directly gives the sharper rate associated with
their smaller row norm.

For a score pair $z=(z_1,z_2)$, write
\begin{equation*}
        |z|_{C,s}
        =\frac{\norm{z_1}_{C,s}}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\norm{z_2}_{C,s}}{\sigma_r}.
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse score moments]\label{lem:sparse-score-moments}
Let
\begin{equation*}
        U_i=
        \begin{pmatrix}X^{(i)}x^{(i)}-d\\X^{(i)}r^{(i)}-h\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation*}
and let $U_i^G$ be independent centered Gaussian vectors with the same
covariance.  Uniformly over deterministic hybrid patterns
$\widetilde U_i\in\{U_i,U_i^G\}$ and fixed $1\le q\le6$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-hybrid-moments}
        \left\|
        \left|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde U_i
        \right|_{C,s}\right\|_{L_q}
        \lesssim\sqrt m,
        \qquad
        \max_i\norm{|\widetilde U_i|_{C,s}}_{L_q}\lesssim\sqrt m.
\end{equation}
For $m+t\lesssim n$, the corresponding tail bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score-hybrid-tail}
\Prob\left(
\left|\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{i=1}^n\widetilde U_i\right|_{C,s}
>C\left\{\sqrt{m+t}+\frac{m+t}{\sqrt n}\right\}
\right)
\le2e^{-ct}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed support and unit sparse direction, each score is a centered
product of standardized sub-Gaussian linear forms and has bounded
$\psi_1$ norm.  Nets over the sparse spheres and supports have logarithmic
size $O(m)$, so Bernstein's inequality gives
\eqref{eq:score-hybrid-tail}, and tail integration gives the first bound.
For one row, the largest sparse norm of $X^{(i)}$ has $L_{2q}$ norm
$O(\sqrt m)$, while $x^{(i)}$ and $r^{(i)}$ have bounded standardized
$L_{2q}$ norms.  Hölder's inequality gives the second bound; $q\le6$
requires moments no higher than twelve.
The Gaussian case follows from the same net calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local-treatment stochastic approximation]
\label{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}
Suppose Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} and
\eqref{eq:local-treatment} hold, and $m^4=o(n)$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-stochastic-approximation}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}
        (\widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\emptyset})}
        {a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}},
        \frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}
        {n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^4}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use \eqref{eq:empirical-chaos-degeneration} and replace the centered
score sums $(Z_{1n}-\sqrt n d,Z_{2n}-\sqrt n h)$ one row at a time by Gaussian
scores with the same covariance.  Normalize each support value by
\begin{equation*}
        n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}
        =\sigma_0\{\sqrt{nm}\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_rm\}.
\end{equation*}
If $W_i$ is the centered leave-one-row hybrid score sum, the directional
derivative of the normalized support value in a row direction
$U_i/\sqrt n$ is bounded by
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{C|U_i|_{C,s}}{\sqrt{mn}}
        \left(1+\frac{|W_i|_{C,s}}{\sqrt m}
        +\frac{|U_i|_{C,s}}{\sqrt{mn}}\right),
\end{equation*}
and its second derivative is bounded by
$C|U_i|_{C,s}^2/(mn)$.  The leave-one-row sum is independent of $U_i$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-score-moments} and H\"older's inequality therefore
give the same integrated derivative bounds as if the parenthetical
factor were constant.  Smoothing the maximum with inverse temperature
$\lambda m$, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments}, therefore bounds the
third derivative in one row direction by
\begin{equation*}
        C|U_i|_{C,s}^3\left\{
        \frac1{m^{3/2}n^{3/2}}
        +\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt m\,n^{3/2}}
        +\frac{\lambda^2\sqrt m}{n^{3/2}}
        \right\}.
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-score-moments} makes the sum of the Lindeberg
remainders
\begin{equation*}
        O\left(\frac1{\sqrt n}
        +\frac{\lambda m}{\sqrt n}
        +\frac{\lambda^2m^2}{\sqrt n}\right).
\end{equation*}
Balancing the last term with the smoothing error $1/\lambda$ using
$\lambda=(\sqrt n/m^2)^{1/3}$ gives $(m^4/n)^{1/6}$.  Writing $\mathcal E_n$ for
the degeneration remainder, its deterministic bound and
the $L_3$ sparse-moment bound give
\begin{equation*}
        \E\min\left\{\frac{|\mathcal E_n|}{a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}},1\right\}
        \lesssim\delta,
\end{equation*}
which supplies the final term in the smooth distance.
\end{proof}

The score tail \eqref{eq:score-hybrid-tail} and relative-RIP tail
\eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} also give the following direct result.  If
$\delta\le\delta_0$ for a
sufficiently small constant, then, with probability at least
$1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-treatment-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}
        (\widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\emptyset})
        \le C\left(
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        \right).
\end{equation}
The bound follows from
$T_C(a,b)\le\norm{a}_{C,s}\norm{b}_{C,s}$.  Under random assignment,
$\widetilde\Psi_s=\Psi_s$ and
$\sigma_r^2=\beta^\top C\beta+\sigma_\varepsilon^2$, so the right side is
equivalent to the standard experimental coordinate-search scale
\begin{equation*}
        \frac{\delta}{\sigma_0}
        \sqrt{\Psi_s^2+\sigma_r^2\delta^2}.
\end{equation*}
A matching scalar lower
bound cannot depend on $\widetilde\Psi_s$ alone: the linear maximum also
depends on how many separated supports carry that energy.  The Gaussian
field retains this geometry rather than discarding it.

\section{Recursive and product search}

The same recursive argument applies to the general Gaussian moment field
and to its matched-chaos reduction.  Let $f$ be any real set function on
the subsets of $[p]$ of size at most $s$, with $f(\emptyset)=0$, and
define its marginal increment by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-marginal}
        \Delta_jf(S)=f(S\cup\{j\})-f(S),
        \qquad j\notin S.
\end{equation}
Starting from $S_0=\emptyset$, choose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-greedy}
        j_t\in\mathop{\arg\max}_{j\notin S_{t-1}}
        \Delta_jf(S_{t-1}),
        \qquad
        S_t=S_{t-1}\cup\{j_t\},
        \qquad t=1,\ldots,s,
\end{equation}
with ties broken by a fixed order.

For distinct $k_1,\ldots,k_s$, define the aggregate equal-depth
variation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-variation}
\begin{split}
        V_s(f)
        =\max_{\substack{k_1,\ldots,k_s\\\text{distinct}}}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\bigg\{
        &\max_{\substack{|R|=t-1\\k_t\notin R}}\Delta_{k_t}f(R)\\
        &-\min_{\substack{|R|=t-1\\k_t\notin R}}\Delta_{k_t}f(R)
        \bigg\}.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This quantity measures only how much the marginal of a fixed coordinate
changes across histories of the same length.

\begin{theorem}[Recursive-search regret]\label{thm:general-greedy}
For every set function $f$, the support in \eqref{eq:general-greedy}
satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-greedy-regret}
        0\le \max_{|S|=s}f(S)-f(S_s)\le V_s(f).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix an optimal support $J$.  Its elements admit an ordering
$k_1,\ldots,k_s$ such that $k_t\notin S_{t-1}$.  A backwards construction
works because $J\setminus S_{t-1}$ has at least $s-t+1$ elements while
only $s-t$ later positions have been filled.  Put
$J_t=\{k_1,\ldots,k_t\}$.  Greedy choice gives
\begin{equation*}
        \Delta_{j_t}f(S_{t-1})
        \ge\Delta_{k_t}f(S_{t-1}).
\end{equation*}
Telescoping along the optimal and recursive paths therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
        f(J)-f(S_s)
        &\le\sum_{t=1}^s
        \{\Delta_{k_t}f(J_{t-1})
        -\Delta_{k_t}f(S_{t-1})\}\\
        &\le V_s(f).
\end{split}
\end{equation*}
The lower bound follows from optimality of $J$.
\end{proof}

For $f^G(S)=B_S(\Gamma^G)$, Theorem~\ref{thm:general-greedy} gives a
recursive rule for the general Gaussian field.  Every marginal can be
evaluated from two Schur
complements and updated by a rank-one inverse formula, so
\eqref{eq:general-greedy} takes polynomial time.  Whenever
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-gaussian-variation}
        V_s(f^G)=o_{\Prob}(a_n),
\end{equation}
the recursive value can replace the exact Gaussian maximum in
Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments}.  This deterministic reduction requires
no distributional assumption beyond the one used to obtain the Gaussian
field.

For the matched chaos, the marginal has a product form.  If $j\notin S$,
define the standardized residual score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-score}
        a_{j\mid S}
        =\frac{a_j-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}a_S}
        {\{1-C_{jS}C_{SS}^{-1}C_{Sj}\}^{1/2}},
\end{equation}
and define $b_{j\mid S}$ in the same way.  Block inversion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-product}
        Q_{S\cup\{j\}}(a,b)-Q_S(a,b)
        =-a_{j\mid S}b_{j\mid S}.
\end{equation}
Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:general-greedy} becomes conditional-product search
after the moment field degenerates to $T_C$.
Define
\begin{equation*}
        V_C(a,b)=V_s\big(S\mapsto Q_S(a,b)\big).
\end{equation*}
If $S_t$ follows \eqref{eq:general-greedy} for this set function, then
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-greedy} and \eqref{eq:conditional-product} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-greedy-regret}
        0\le T_C(a,b)
        +\sum_{t=1}^s a_{j_t\mid S_{t-1}}b_{j_t\mid S_{t-1}}
        \le V_C(a,b).
\end{equation}
Consequently, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-variation-condition}
        \E\min\left\{
        \frac{V_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}},1
        \right\}=o(1),
\end{equation}
the conditional-product value can replace $T_C(Z_1,Z_2)$ in
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}.  This is the stochastic
greedy approximation; \eqref{eq:product-greedy-regret} is its exact
deterministic certificate.

When $C=I$, the products in \eqref{eq:conditional-product} no longer
depend on $S$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-s-products}
        T_I(a,b)
        =\sum_{t=1}^s\{-a_jb_j\}_{(t)},
\end{equation}
where braces denote decreasing order statistics.  The general departure
from this static product list is measured by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-isotropy}
        \rho_s(C)=\sup_{|J|\le2s}\norm{C_{JJ}-I}_{\mathrm{op}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Static product approximation]\label{thm:static-products}
For every $a,b\in\R^p$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:static-product-bound}
        |T_C(a,b)-T_I(a,b)|
        \le\rho_s(C)\norm{a}_{C,s}\norm{b}_{C,s}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed $S$, put
$u=C_{SS}^{-1/2}a_S$ and $w=C_{SS}^{-1/2}b_S$.  Then
\begin{equation*}
        Q_S(a,b)+a_S^\top b_S=u^\top(C_{SS}-I)w,
\end{equation*}
whose absolute value is at most the right side of
\eqref{eq:static-product-bound}.  Taking the uniform bound over $S$ and
using $|\max_S x_S-\max_S y_S|\le\max_S|x_S-y_S|$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Top-$s$ stochastic approximation]
\label{cor:top-s-stochastic}
Under the assumptions of Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic}, if
$\rho_s(C)=o(1)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:top-s-stochastic}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        &\frac{\max_{|S|=s}
        (\widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\emptyset})}
        {a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}},
        \frac1{n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}}
        \sum_{t=1}^s\{-Z_{1j}Z_{2j}\}_{(t)}
        \bigg)=o(1).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
There are therefore $n$-dependent scalar copies of the two variables in
\eqref{eq:top-s-stochastic} whose unnormalized difference is
$o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{loc}})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Gaussian sparse-score bounds give
\begin{equation*}
        \norm{Z_1}_{C,s}=O_{\Prob}(\sigma_0\sqrt m),
        \qquad
        \norm{Z_2}_{C,s}
        =O_{\Prob}(\sqrt n\,\widetilde\Psi_s+\sigma_r\sqrt m).
\end{equation*}
Theorem~\ref{thm:static-products} therefore replaces $T_C(Z_1,Z_2)$ by
$T_I(Z_1,Z_2)$ with error $o_{L_1}(n\sigma_0^2a_n^{\mathrm{loc}})$.
Corollary~\ref{cor:local-treatment-stochastic} and
\eqref{eq:top-s-products} prove the smooth-distance statement.  The
normalized variables are tight by the same sparse-score bounds, so
Prokhorov--Strassen coupling gives the scalar assertion.
\end{proof}

The products in \eqref{eq:top-s-stochastic} need not be independent.
Their joint covariance is inherited from
\eqref{eq:matched-moment-matrix}.  Independence requires an additional
comparison of the score covariance; it holds, for example, when the
Gaussian pairs $(Z_{1j},Z_{2j})$ are block-independent across $j$.
It does not follow from local isotropy of $C$ alone.

\section{Bounds and the observational landscape}

The moment approximation separates the size of the fluctuations from the
geometry of the population landscape.  The quantities $\phi_s$ and
$\widetilde\Psi_s$ in \eqref{eq:mean-sizes} measure the treatment and
residual-outcome score energies on the searched supports.

\begin{lemma}[Influence envelope]\label{lem:influence-envelope}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:influence-envelope}
        \max_{|S|\le s}
        \norm{DB_S(\Gamma)
        [v^{(i)}(v^{(i)})^\top-\Gamma]}_{\psi_1}
        \lesssim
        \frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\sqrt{\phi_s}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expand $u_Se_S/\eta(S)-xr/\sigma_0^2$ using
\eqref{eq:influence-residuals}.  The common $xr$ term cancels when
$d=h=0$, and every remaining summand contains at least one factor of
$C_{SS}^{-1/2}d_S$ or $C_{SS}^{-1/2}h_S$.  Relative
sub-Gaussianity, H\"older's inequality for Orlicz norms, and overlap bound
its $\psi_1$ norm by a constant times
$\sigma_r\sqrt{\phi_s}/\sigma_0$ or
$\widetilde\Psi_s/\sigma_0$.  Summing these terms proves the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coefficient upper bound]\label{cor:coefficient-upper}
Let $S^*\in\arg\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma)$.  Suppose
Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} holds and $\delta\le\delta_0$ for a
sufficiently small constant.  With probability at least $1-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coefficient-upper}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat\beta_{0,S}
        -\widehat\beta_{0,S^*}
        \le C\bigg(
        &\frac{\widetilde\Psi_s}{\sigma_0}\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta
        +\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2
        \bigg).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Bernstein's inequality, \eqref{eq:influence-envelope}, and a union over the
supports give the first two terms of \eqref{eq:coefficient-upper}; the
linear Bernstein remainder has order
$(\sigma_r/\sigma_0)\delta^2$.  Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} give the quadratic term.  The
population difference $B_S(\Gamma)-B_{S^*}(\Gamma)$ is nonpositive, so
centering at $S^*$ completes the argument.
\end{proof}

When $d=0$, \eqref{eq:coefficient-upper} is the experimental scale in
\eqref{eq:local-treatment-upper-scale}.  When
$\phi_s\lesssim\delta^2$, its treatment-side term is absorbed by the
bilinear term, which gives the observational rate under local treatment
alignment.  For arbitrary $d$, the $\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta$ term is
first-order and cannot be omitted.  Equation~\eqref{eq:score-translation}
expresses the bound in the original model parameters
$(\Psi_s,\beta,d)$.

The stochastic approximation also identifies the correct form of a lower
bound.  For $\epsilon\ge0$, let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-maximal-family}
        \mathcal N_\epsilon
        =\{S:|S|=s,\ B_S(\Gamma)\ge B_{S^*}(\Gamma)-\epsilon\},
\end{equation}
and write
\begin{equation*}
        W_S=B_S(\Gamma^G)-B_S(\Gamma).
\end{equation*}
Then the Gaussianized moment field obeys the deterministic inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:landscape-lower-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)-B_{S^*}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge
        \max_{S\in\mathcal N_\epsilon}
        \{W_S-W_{S^*}\}-\epsilon.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus a lower bound is governed by a quantile of the matched field on the
near-maximal supports.  To reduce this statement to a Gaussian width,
define the linear field
\begin{equation*}
        W_S^{(1)}=\frac1{\sqrt n}DB_S(\Gamma)[G].
\end{equation*}
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse-hybrid-moments} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linear-field-remainder}
        \max_{|S|=s}|W_S-W_S^{(1)}|
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gaussian-lower-reduction}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)-B_{S^*}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge{}&\max_{S\in\mathcal N_\epsilon}
        (W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^*}^{(1)})-\epsilon\\
        &-2\max_{|S|=s}|W_S-W_S^{(1)}|.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The canonical distance of $W_S^{(1)}$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:canonical-distance}
        d_G(S,T)^2
        =\frac1n\E\left[
        DB_S(\Gamma)[G]-DB_T(\Gamma)[G]
        \right]^2.
\end{equation}
\begin{proposition}[Packing lower bound]\label{prop:packing-lower}
Suppose $\delta\le\delta_0$, $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal N_\epsilon$
has cardinality $M\ge2$, and $d_G(S,T)\ge r$ for distinct
$S,T\in\mathcal P$.  Put
\begin{equation*}
        \bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}^2
        =\max_{S\in\mathcal P}\Var(W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^*}^{(1)}).
\end{equation*}
For every $u\ge0$, with probability at least
$1-e^{-u^2/2}-Ce^{-cm}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:packing-lower}
\begin{split}
        \max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma^G)-B_{S^*}(\Gamma^G)
        \ge{}&c r\sqrt{\log M}-u\bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}-\epsilon\\
        &-C\frac{\sigma_r}{\sigma_0}\delta^2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sudakov minoration gives
$\E\max_{S\in\mathcal P}(W_S^{(1)}-W_{S^*}^{(1)})
\ge c r\sqrt{\log M}$.
The Gaussian lower-tail inequality subtracts at most
$u\bar\sigma_{\mathcal P}$ with probability $e^{-u^2/2}$.  The Gaussian
case of \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-tail} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:uniform-expansion} bound the final term in
\eqref{eq:gaussian-lower-reduction}, proving \eqref{eq:packing-lower}.
\end{proof}

The proposition is informative when its Gaussian width dominates the
quadratic remainder.  In a degenerate experiment that remainder is part
of the leading scale, so the correct lower object is instead a quantile
of the full linear--bilinear chaos in
Theorem~\ref{thm:chaos-degeneration}.

Cardinality alone does not control either metric.  For example, take
$s=1$, $C=I$, $d=0$, and $h=\mu e_1$.  Then
$\widetilde\Psi_1=\mu$, but only one support carries signal.  Its linear
contribution is of order $\mu/\sqrt n$, not
$\mu\sqrt{\log p/n}$; the remaining coordinates contribute only the
bilinear noise maximum.  A scalar signal lower bound of order
$\widetilde\Psi_s\delta$ therefore requires a packing or multiplicity
condition.
The matched field records that condition through its covariance instead
of imposing it implicitly.

\section{Studentized search}

Studentization is another smooth function of an augmented second-moment
matrix.  Let
\begin{equation*}
        v_y^{(i)}=
        \begin{pmatrix}x^{(i)}\\y^{(i)}\\X^{(i)}\end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        \Gamma_y=\E[v_y^{(i)}(v_y^{(i)})^\top],
        \qquad
        \widehat\Gamma_y=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n
        v_y^{(i)}(v_y^{(i)})^\top.
\end{equation*}
For a second-moment matrix indexed by $(x,y,X)$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-functional}
        \tau_S(A)
        =\frac{A_{xy\mathbin{\cdot}S}}
        {\{A_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}A_{yy\mathbin{\cdot}S}\}^{1/2}}.
\end{equation}
The searched partial-correlation statistic satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-moment-identity}
        \widehat t_{0,S}
        =\sqrt{n-s-1}\,\tau_S(\widehat\Gamma_y).
\end{equation}
For the baseline support,
$\widehat t_{0,\emptyset}=\sqrt{n-1}\,\tau_\emptyset(\widehat\Gamma_y)$.
The conventional least-squares statistic is
$\sqrt{n-s-1}\,\tau_S/(1-\tau_S^2)^{1/2}$; it follows from the same
argument, under a uniform $1-\tau_S^2$ floor, by replacing
\eqref{eq:t-functional} with this smooth transform.

Let $G_y$ be the covariance-matched Gaussian moment matrix for $v_y$, and
put $\Gamma_y^G=\Gamma_y+G_y/\sqrt n$.  Assume that the treatment and
outcome Schur complements are bounded below uniformly over $|S|\le2s$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t-floors}
\begin{split}
        (\Gamma_y)_{xx\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        &\ge c_x\Var(x),\\
        (\Gamma_y)_{yy\mathbin{\cdot}S}
        &\ge c_y\Var(y).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Let $|H|_{y,s}$ denote \eqref{eq:sparse-moment-size} with $r$ replaced by
$y$ and with $|H_{yy}|/\Var(y)$ included.  Define the anchored functional
and its first-derivative envelope by
\begin{align*}
        c_{s,n}&=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{n-s-1}},
        \qquad
        \Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)=\tau_S(A)-c_{s,n}\tau_\emptyset(A),\\
        \alpha_{t,s}
        & =\max_{|S|=s}\sup_{|H|_{y,s}\le1}
        |D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)[H]|,\\
        a_{t,n}&=\alpha_{t,s}\delta+\delta^2.
\end{align*}

\begin{corollary}[Studentized moment approximation]
\label{cor:studentized-moments}
Suppose the relative sub-Gaussian and sparse-block conditions
of Assumption~\ref{ass:sparse-moments} hold for $v_y$, the floors in
\eqref{eq:t-floors} hold, and $m^7=o(n)$.  If
$\Delta\tau_* =\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y)$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-gaussian-field}
\begin{split}
        d_3\bigg(
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\widehat\Gamma_y)
        -\Delta\tau_*}{a_{t,n}},
        \frac{\max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\Gamma_y^G)
        -\Delta\tau_*}{a_{t,n}}
        \bigg)
        \lesssim
        \left(\frac{m^7}{n}\right)^{1/6}+\delta.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The definition of $c_{s,n}$ gives the exact identity
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat t_{0,S}-\widehat t_{0,\emptyset}
        =\sqrt{n-s-1}
        \max_{|S|=s}\Delta\tau_{S,n}(\widehat\Gamma_y),
\end{equation*}
so \eqref{eq:studentized-gaussian-field} gives its approximation.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating the two Schur moments in \eqref{eq:t-functional} and
using \eqref{eq:t-floors} gives
\begin{align*}
        |D\Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)[H]|&\lesssim
        \{\alpha_{t,s}+|A-\Gamma_y|_{y,s}\}|H|_{y,s},\\
        |D^j\Delta\tau_{S,n}(A)[H_1,\ldots,H_j]|
        &\lesssim\prod_{k=1}^j|H_k|_{y,s},
        \qquad j=2,3,
\end{align*}
on a fixed sparse neighborhood.  Smooth reciprocal and spectral cutoffs
give the localized global version.  The hybrid-moment, leave-one-row, and
smooth-max calculation in Theorem~\ref{thm:matched-moments} then applies
with $a_{t,n}$ in place of $a_n$ and gives the same $m^7$ rate.
\end{proof}

To connect this law with the testing regime, impose the null $\beta_0=0$
and suppose
\begin{equation*}
        \max_{|S|\le s}|\tau_S(\Gamma_y)|\lesssim n^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, put $g=\Cov(X^{(i)},y^{(i)})=C\beta$ and
$K_S=C_{SS}^{-1}$.  The derivative of the numerator Schur moment is
\begin{equation*}
        H_{xy}-H_{xS}K_Sg_S-d_S^\top K_SH_{Sy}
        +d_S^\top K_SH_{SS}K_Sg_S.
\end{equation*}
The analogous denominator derivatives, the two floors, and the local
population-correlation bound therefore give
\begin{equation*}
        \alpha_{t,s}\lesssim
        \frac{\Psi_s}{\sqrt{\Var(y)}}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta.
\end{equation*}
Writing $\theta_s=\Psi_s^2/\Var(y)$, direct moment concentration and the
local population-correlation bound yield
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-studentized-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat t_{0,S}-\widehat t_{0,\emptyset}
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \sqrt m\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\sqrt{\phi_s}+\delta\}+1\right).
\end{equation}
Under the local-treatment condition \eqref{eq:local-treatment}, the
treatment term is absorbed by the quadratic scale, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:studentized-upper-scale}
        \max_{|S|=s}\widehat t_{0,S}-\widehat t_{0,\emptyset}
        =O_{\Prob}\!\left(
        \sqrt m\{\sqrt{\theta_s}+\delta\}+1\right).
\end{equation}

The treatment term in \eqref{eq:general-studentized-upper-scale} is
necessary.  For example, let $p$ be even, $s=p/2$, $n=p^2$, and take
$C=I_p$, $d_j=a/\sqrt p$ for a fixed $a\in(0,1)$.  Let
$X\sim N(0,I_p)$, $y\sim N(0,1)$, and $u\sim N(0,1-a^2)$ be independent,
and set $x=d^\top X+u$.  Every population partial correlation vanishes,
$\theta_s=0$, and overlap holds uniformly, but the top-$s$ treatment--outcome
score term moves the searched statistic by order $\sqrt p$.  The
treatment-free right side of \eqref{eq:studentized-upper-scale} would be
of constant order as $p\to\infty$.  A lower bound in general requires metric separation
for the full first- and second-order studentized field.

\section{Scope of the reduction}

Put
\begin{equation*}
        M_n=\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\widehat\Gamma),
        \qquad
        M_n^G=\max_{|S|=s}B_S(\Gamma+G/\sqrt n).
\end{equation*}
Under $m^7=o(n)$, the reduction has the following nested form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unified-spine}
\begin{gathered}
 \max_{|S|=s}(\widehat\beta_{0,S}-\widehat\beta_{0,\emptyset})=M_n,\\[1mm]
 d_3\!\left(\frac{M_n-b_*}{a_n},\frac{M_n^G-b_*}{a_n}\right)=o(1),\\[2mm]
 \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 M_n^G=\frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2}
 +o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}),\\[2mm]
 \sqrt{\phi_{2s}}\lesssim\delta,\quad \rho_s(C)=o(1)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \frac{T_C(Z_1,Z_2)}{n\sigma_0^2}
 =\frac1{n\sigma_0^2}\sum_{t=1}^s\{-Z_{1j}Z_{2j}\}_{(t)}
 +o_{\Prob}(a_n^{\mathrm{loc}}),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All standing moment and overlap conditions remain in force, and the
conditions accumulate down the display.
The first identity is exact and observational, while the smooth-distance
comparison is the general
stochastic approximation.  A local treatment score gives the chaos, and
local isotropy gives its separable top-$s$ form.
Recursive search branches from either of the first two
fields through Theorem~\ref{thm:general-greedy}.  The partial-correlation
map \eqref{eq:t-functional} passes through the same matched-moment
comparison as another smooth moment functional.

This spine yields the experimental upper scale, the locally confounded
score chaos, conditional-product search, the local-isotropy top-$s$
formula, the observational influence process, and the general
studentized upper scale.  It also shows where additional conditions
enter.  The
observational upper bound needs the treatment term
$\sqrt{\phi_s}\,\delta$, and a lower bound needs metric separation rather
than the cardinality of the near-optimal family alone.  The studentized
scale likewise contains $\sqrt{\phi_s}$ unless treatment is local.

Two interfaces are not discharged by the moment replacement itself.  The
top-$s$ products inherit the matched score covariance, so an
independent-product plug-in or multiplier calibration requires a separate
comparison of that covariance.  The packing result is a lower bound for
the Gaussianized field; an exponential-probability empirical lower bound
requires a stronger probability-level transfer than the smooth distance
proved here.  The exact identity remains valid for singular control
blocks after passing to selected column spans.  Extending the uniform
bounds to that case requires a separate projector formulation; the
differentiable Gaussian law is stated for nonsingular sparse blocks.

The regimes are distinct.  Direct empirical concentration gives the
exponential-probability upper bounds whenever $\delta\le\delta_0$, which
allows $m/n$ to be a sufficiently small constant.  The full Gaussian law
proved here uses $m^7=o(n)$, while the score-only law uses $m^4=o(n)$.
Neither weak approximation is asserted at the critical boundary.
Searches over transformations that are not indexed by sparse principal
blocks also remain outside this reduction.
